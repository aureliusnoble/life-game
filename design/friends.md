# Playing with friends

Draft 1, November 2026. Design area 11 in `design/plan.md`. Section 1 is decided. The rest is a proposal on paper, and nothing here has been tried with a group.

## 1. Decided

- **One world for the group,** five to twenty friends, each with one living species at a time.
- **All unkind behaviour between species is allowed.**
- **A friend's design opens to everyone once the species has ended.** Presets' designs are always open.
- **The score is total life,** with longest-lived and highest peak kept as records beside it.
- **Dominance is expected to turn over,** and to depend on which other species are around.
- **Unlocks widen play and add no power,** so a newcomer is never behind.
- **Little text.**
- **The game opens in a browser tab from a link,** with no account needed first.

## 2. What the game has to supply

Friends will talk about the game in their own chat and in person. The game does not need a chat. It needs to supply what talk is made of:

| Talk | What prompts it |
|---|---|
| "Your Prowls ate half my herd" | Relationships between species, measured and named, with a moment to show |
| "How did yours get through the winter?" | Ended designs, open to all, with the record of which instincts did the work |
| "Who is winning?" | A board for the year, and more than one way to be best |
| "Remember the flood year?" | A history of the basin that everyone shares |
| "Leave mine alone and I will leave yours" | Alliances made of instincts, which nothing enforces |
| "There is nothing eating the reeds" | Openings in the world, shown to everyone |
| "Look at this" | A link that opens on the moment |

## 3. Relationships between two species

A player's relationship with a friend is whatever their two species do to each other. The game measures it and gives it a name.

**The card between two species** opens from either species' page. It shows what was recorded between them, over the last day and over their lives:

| Link | Counted from |
|---|---|
| Eats | Deaths, by killer |
| Raids its eggs | Lost eggs, by who ate them |
| Takes its food | Lost meals |
| Rides on it, or clings to it | Time attached |
| Tends it, feeds it | Tending and giving |
| Heeds its calls | Times an instinct that hears the other's call was the one winning |
| Lives beside it | Share of time in sight of each other |

- **One word for the pair,** taken from the largest link: hunter and prey, rivals, rider and carrier, cleaner and host, neighbours, strangers. It is a name for what was counted. It says nothing about intent.
- **It can run both ways.** Two species can be hunter and prey in one direction and rivals in the other, and the card shows both.
- **Each species has a nemesis and a mainstay:** the species that has killed most of it, and the species it has lived off most. Both are shown on its page with a count.
- **Changes are events.** "Began to be hunted by", "a rival arrived on its food" and "a species it relied on is gone" are already in the event list in `design/loops.md`.

## 4. Designing for and against each other

- **Instincts can name a species.** "A species I have met" is a word in the vocabulary, and Learn fills in kinds from what happened: one that hurt my kind, one that fed me.
- **There is no alliance feature.** A pact between two players is a pair of instincts: a shared display both species respect, or each simply leaving the other alone. Nothing enforces it, and signals can lie. A broken pact is a story.
- **Countering is slow on purpose.** A living species changes by one mutation a day from three drawn at random, so it cannot be rebuilt against a rival overnight. A full answer is a new release, which retires the old species. Rivalries play out over days.
- **What a player knows about a rival's living species** is what can be seen: its body, what it does, its line, its tally and its place in the web. What it thinks is private until it ends (`design/feedback.md`, section 11). Working it out by watching is part of the game.
- **A keeper's nudge reaches only the keeper's own species.** This is a proposal. Signals are public, so a dropped scent or call could be read by other species, and a player's hand would become a weapon that costs nothing. Proposed: other species do not notice a keeper-made signal.

## 5. Seeing each other's work

**A species' page** is open to everyone and reached by tapping any creature or from the board:

- Its face, its name, its maker's mark, its age and generation.
- Its line, where it lives, its tally by cause, and its web.
- Its nemesis and mainstay, its titles, and its chronicle so far.
- A button to follow its named one.

**Visiting a friend's named one.** A visitor can stroke it and offer a treat, and nothing else: no call, no nudge and no talk. It meets the visitor as a stranger. The visit is recorded as an event, so the creature can mention it when its own keeper asks what happened.

**An ended species** opens in full:

- The body, the forms, every page of instincts and the settings.
- The page, used, over its whole life: which rows did the work and which never fired.
- It can be run in the test tank.
- It can be taken as a starting point for a new design. A species started this way is marked "after" the one it came from, and the mark is kept down the generations of designs, so the group's designs form a family tree.
- A player can look at every word in a friend's design, and can release only what they have unlocked themselves. A friend's design is a preview of what the unlocks are for.

## 6. The board

**The year is the season of the board.** A world year is about eight real days, close to the weekly design session. Each year has its own board, and there is one for all time.

| Board | What it ranks |
|---|---|
| Living now | Every living species: its line, its total life so far, its age |
| This year | Total life added during the year, by species |
| All time | The best species ever, by total life |

**Titles** are records that change hands. Each is one way of being best, and they are chosen so that different ways of playing win different ones.

| Title | Held by the species with |
|---|---|
| The Elder | The longest life |
| The Multitude | The highest head count |
| The Giant | The largest animal to live a full life |
| The Wanderer | The most biomes lived in at once |
| The Old Line | The most generations of its named line |
| The Menace | The most of other players' creatures killed |
| The Larder | The most of its own eaten by others |
| The Homebuilder | The largest home |

- A title is held for the year and for all time, and taking one from a friend's species is an event.
- **Firsts** are kept for good: the first species to see a winter through in the Highlands, the first colony, the first to live in each biome, the first to last eight days. Each is written into the basin's history with the player's name. They carry no power.
- **The basin's own score.** The living weight of every species together, added up over the year. It is the one score the group shares, and a reason to care whether the world is thriving as well as who leads it.

## 7. The basin's history

One shared record of the world, open to everyone.

- **The ages of the basin:** the world's living weight through time, drawn as bands, one for each species, in its maker's colour. Presets are drawn in plain paper tones. It shows who rose when, who they displaced, and how long each lasted.
- **Major events of every species** are pinned along it, with the firsts.
- **Dragging along it** runs the whole basin's map through time.
- **Each year closes with an almanac:** the year's board, the titles and who took them from whom, the firsts, the great events, and what is going uneaten as the new year begins.

## 8. Sharing

- **A postcard** is a link with a picture. Any recorded moment, a species' page, an ending, a title or an almanac can be sent as one. For someone in the group the link opens the game on that moment. For anyone else it shows the picture and nothing more.
- **The almanac is the weekly thing to send.**
- **A line a day in the group's chat** is possible where the chat allows it: the day's biggest event across the basin. It would be set up once by whoever runs the server. Which chat services allow this has not been checked.

## 9. Joining and leaving

- **Joining** is a link from a friend, a name, and a colour and mark. The first hour happens in the shared world.
- **A maker's mark** is a colour and a small stamp. Every species a player makes carries it on the map, on the board and in the history.
- **A code** brings a player back on another device.
- **A newcomer is not behind.** The allowance is the same for everyone, and the group's ended designs are theirs to study.
- **A player who stops** needs nothing done. Their species fades and ends like any other, a preset takes the empty place, and their records stay.
- **Whoever runs the server** can remove a player or rename a species. Nothing else needs moderating in a group of friends.

## 10. Set aside

- **Seeing where friends are looking.** With visits of a quarter of an hour, two players are seldom there together.
- **Chat, comments and reactions.** The group already has a place to talk.
- **Shared ownership of a species.**
- **Bets and predictions.**

## 11. Questions

1. **The year as the season of the board,** with titles that change hands. Proposed: yes.
2. **Nudges and other players' species.** Proposed: a keeper's nudge is noticed only by the keeper's own species.
3. **Visiting a friend's named one:** a stroke and a treat, remembered as an event. Proposed: yes.
4. **Taking an ended design as a starting point:** free, marked "after" its source, and releasable only with the taker's own unlocks. Proposed: yes.
