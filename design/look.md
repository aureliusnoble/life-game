# Look, sound and feel

Draft 3, November 2026. Design area 12 in `design/plan.md`. It builds on `design/art-direction.md`. Sections 1, 3 and 14 are decided. The rest is a proposal, and none of it is final art.

## 1. Decided

- **Soft critters in the Paper lagoon direction:** cut and layered paper, like a picture book.
- **"Expressive" means two things:** more variety in what a creature can be built from, and bodies that show what the creature is doing and feeling.
- **The look is the game's own,** and places should feel designed, beautiful and relaxing.
- **Little text.** The world, the animation and the graphics have to let a player see what is happening.
- **The interface is handcrafted, cute and intuitive without losing depth,** and depth is revealed a little at a time.
- **The mockups so far are concept tests.** The interface is designed later, in stages, starting from what each moment should feel like.
- **Creatures grow paler as their species ages** (`design/lifespan.md`).
- **It runs in a browser tab on a phone.**

## 2. What the look is for

The game explains almost nothing in words, so the look does the explaining. It has three jobs, in this order:

1. **Say what is happening.** A player can read what a creature is doing and how it is, at each of three distances.
2. **Make the place worth looking at.** Looking around is meant to be fun in itself.
3. **Feel made by hand,** by someone, for this group.

Where these pull apart, the first wins.

## 3. The view

- **The world is seen from above,** and each creature turns to face where it is heading. Both art studies and the behaviour editor mockup were drawn this way, and the map is the basin seen from above.
- **Direction is part of the rules,** which is why this view suits the game. Sight has a direction and a blind side, a creature can be crept up on from behind, and circling, heading off and facing are all about direction. From above a player can see all of it.
- **Faces stay readable.** The eyes sit on top of the head and look where the creature looks.
- **A front-on close-up** is used where one creature is the whole subject: tending the named one, portraits, and the card for one creature.
- **What each view carries.** From above: where it is going, which way its head is turned, how it moves, its outline, its ears and tail. Close up: the face, leaning, and the mouth.

This was drawn in every study and never written down. It is recorded here on 15 November as the working decision.

## 4. The paper rulebook

"Paper" is a set of rules that everything on screen obeys, the world and the interface alike.

| Rule | What it means |
|---|---|
| Everything is cut from paper | One sheet for each shape, in one flat colour. No outlines, and no shading inside a shape |
| Sheets stack | Ground, low plants, creatures, tall plants, weather, and then the interface. Each sheet drops a soft shadow on the one below |
| There is one light | The sun crosses the sky each day. Shadows are short at noon and long and warm at dusk. Night is a blue wash with small warm glows |
| Edges are cut by hand | A little uneven, with a pale lip on the lit side where the paper's thickness shows |
| Paper has grain | A faint fibre texture over everything. Colour differs from sheet to sheet and never within one |
| Things move as paper moves | They hinge, slide, flutter, curl and pop up |
| Depth comes from sliding | When the view moves, higher sheets move a little farther than lower ones |
| Few colours at a time | Each place has five or six, with paper white and ink. Large areas stay calm and detail gathers in clusters |
| Creatures stand clear of the ground | A species' colours come from a set of inks checked against all eight grounds. Its shadow and pale lip separate it where colours are close |

How paper handles what it is bad at:

| Thing | In paper |
|---|---|
| Water | Strips sliding past each other, paler over darker; deeper water has more strips |
| Grass and reeds | Rows of flaps hinged at the base, which part as a creature passes and stay bent where they were grazed |
| Rain, snow, blown sand | Slivers, punched dots, streaks |
| Fire | Flickering tongues of layered orange, leaving charred black with a glowing edge |
| Mist and spray | Sheets of stippled tissue |
| Glow | A halo sheet under the glowing part, strongest at night |
| Darkness in a cave | A dark sheet with a hole cut round what the followed creature can sense |
| Cover over a creature | The canopy or the reed stand is cut away round the creature being followed |

## 5. Three distances

| Distance | On screen | What has to read |
|---|---|---|
| The basin | The whole map, each species a wash of its colour | Where each species is, how many, and whether it is growing |
| A biome | Creatures as small shapes | Whose they are, what they are doing, groups, chases and deaths |
| Close | A few creatures with faces | Mood, need, what each has noticed, and signals |

Each piece of body language is designed for the farthest distance it has to read at. What a creature is doing is carried by movement and outline, so that it reads in the biome view. Faces are for close up.

## 6. Bodies that show

A body shows four things, each carried by a different part of it so that they do not compete.

| What | Carried by | Reads at |
|---|---|---|
| What it is doing | Where the body is going, which way it and its head point, and how it moves: the gait, the lunge, the huddle | Biome and close |
| How it is | The body's condition: thin or round, a patch where it was hurt, a shiver, colour faded with age | Close. Thinness and a limp also at biome |
| What it feels | The face and the bearing: eyes, the signature part, posture | Close |
| What it is saying | Signals, drawn in paper: rings for a call, a raised flag for a display, a chit left on the ground for a scent | Biome and close |

### What it is doing

The table gives each act in words that fit both views. Seen from above, leaning toward something is the body stretching toward it with the head pointed at it.

| Act | The body |
|---|---|
| approach, stay near | Leans toward it, signature part forward, steady hops |
| move away, keep apart | Leans away and looks back over its shoulder |
| wander | Loose, uneven hops, looking about |
| circle, go along, go the same way, head off | The body turned along its path and the head turned to what it follows |
| face, hold still | Stops square on to it. Held still, only the eyes move |
| creeping | Low and stretched, feet placed slowly |
| sprinting | Flat, signature part streaming back, flecks of ground thrown up |
| bite, to eat | Head down, cheeks working, crumbs |
| bite, to wound | A lunge and a snap. The other flinches and a patch tears |
| rest | Folds down flat, eyes shut, slow breathing |
| lay | Squats and wiggles, and an egg slides out |
| mate | The two press together and wiggle as one |
| grab, carry, drop | Lifts it overhead or in the mouth, and waddles under the weight |
| give | Holds it out. The other takes it and both bob |
| tend | Leans over and pats. The one tended settles |
| cling | Flattened on to the carrier, moving as it moves |
| hide | Pulled into its shell or hollow, eyes showing |
| roll | Flips belly up and back |
| shed | The tail tears off and is left wriggling |
| sting, spit, shock, spray | A jab; an arc of dots; a zigzag burst; a stippled cloud |
| puff up, play dead, crouch | Swells to a bigger outline; flops on its side; flattens and takes the ground's colour |
| mark, wipe | Presses down and leaves a small coloured chit; scuffs one away |
| dig, build | Flings chips of ground behind it; sets pieces down one at a time |
| call, show | Mouth open, rings spreading in the call's colour; a frill or flag raised in the display's colour |

### How it is

| State | The body |
|---|---|
| Hungry | Thinner and slower, and it looks at food |
| Fed | Rounder |
| Tired | Heavy lids, dragging, yawns |
| Hurt | A torn patch, later a stitched one, and a limp on that side |
| Cold | Shivers, huddles small, a blue lip |
| Hot | Flattened and panting |
| Stung or ill | A green tinge and a wobble |
| Carrying eggs | Rounder low down, and slower |
| Young | A larger head for its body, paler, tumbling |
| Old | Softened edges, slower |
| Its species fading | The whole species paler, day by day, as paper bleaches in the sun |
| Dead | Falls on its side and becomes a pale cut-out, which fades as it is eaten or rots |

### What it feels

Moods are the player's own. Each is named by the player and given an icon, and has no fixed meaning, so the body cannot know how to show one.

Proposed: when a player makes a mood they also pick its **bearing** from a small set. A creature wears the bearing of its strongest mood, more plainly as the mood rises.

| Bearing | Posture | Eyes | Signature part | Movement |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Wary | Low and drawn back | Wide, small pupils, darting | Flat back | Short quick steps, freezing between |
| Fierce | Forward and swollen | Narrow and slanted | Raised stiff | Stamping |
| Eager | Up on its toes, leaning in | Large pupils, fixed | Pricked | Bouncing |
| Calm | Loose and settled | Half closed | Relaxed | Slow |
| Tender | Leaning to the other, head tilted | Soft, slow blinks, a blush | Folded forward | Gentle |
| Weary | Sagging | Heavy lids | Drooping | Dragging |
| Playful | Wiggling, head tilted | Bright | Flicking | Hops and spins |
| Gloomy | Head down, small | Downcast | Drooping | Slow, with stops |

- **The player's icon** appears above the creature on a small paper tag at the moment a mood starts. It stays up while the player is watching it think.
- **Feelings the game defines,** such as startled and outnumbered, have fixed flinches of their own.
- **From above the bearings are harder to tell apart** than close up. In the study, calm, weary and tired differ mostly by the eyelids. Ears, tail and movement have to carry more of each bearing in the world's view, and the icon tag matters more.

### What it has noticed

Before it acts on something, a creature glances at it: the head turns and the signature part points. This is how a player sees, in the world, that a creature saw the hunter and went on eating.

## 7. Variety

- **Body plans** set the outline: bun, tadpole, pear, bean, noodle, chonk, and more to come.
- **Each part is a cut piece** that tucks under or over the body's sheet, so no part looks stuck on. Parts take their colours from the species' inks.
- **One signature feature for each species,** which reads in outline: ears, a crest, a tail, a frill. It is also the part that carries most of the bearing.
- **Forms share the base outline.** A caste or a sex is told apart by the parts that differ.
- **The young** are the same sheets with a larger head.
- **At the basin's distance** a species is its pair of colours. The maker's mark is a stamp on portraits and lists, and is not worn on bodies.

## 8. The eight places

| Biome | Colours | Its paper | Movement | Through the year |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Highlands | Grey-violet stone, lichen green, snow white, rust | Rock in stacked sheets like contour lines; scree chips; pools as cut holes; cave mouths as dark cut-outs | Cloud shadows sliding across; wind streaks | The snow line comes down in winter and retreats in summer |
| Woodland | Deep greens, bark brown, mushroom orange, yellow light | A canopy sheet above the creatures with holes that let light through; trunks as dark discs; leaf litter as confetti; fungus rings | Patches of light drifting over the floor | Leaf fall in autumn; a bare canopy in winter lets the light in |
| Heath | Ochre, heather pink, sage, char black | Scrub as crumpled tufts; water holes; burnt ground as black paper with a glowing edge | Heat shimmer; fire late in summer | Pink in flower, then fire, then green shoots on the black |
| Grassland | Green, turning gold, straw and dun | Rows of grass flaps; flowers that open by day; two rivers as sliding blue strips | Wind crossing the grass in waves | The strongest change: green, gold, brown, frost white |
| Lakeland | Blues, teal, lily green, reed gold | Water in strips, darker with depth; lily pads; islands; reeds at the rim | Ripple rings; drifting pads | A green bloom after rain; a white rim of ice in winter |
| Marsh | Olive, mud brown, straw, russet, mist grey | Reed stands as tall flaps that hide what is inside; glossy mud; channels | Mist drifting; bubbles | Spring floods slide a sheet of water over it; winter lays the reeds flat |
| Dunes | Cream sand, peach shadow, grey-green marram, driftwood | Sand as scalloped ripples; hollows with green in them; a driftline of bits | Ripples creeping; blown streaks. Footprints punch in and fill within the hour | Little change, and storms |
| Tidal flats | Wet slate, silver blue, weed green, shell pink | The sea as one broad sheet with a scalloped foam edge; pools left behind; worm casts | The tide sliding in and out several times a day | Little change |

- **Use shows.** Grazed flaps stay bent, paths are scuffed paler, and ground eaten bare is brown.
- **Borders are places.** Where two biomes meet their sheets interleave.
- **Every visit has a different light,** because a day is an hour.

## 9. The interface: direction only

This is not the style system, which comes after the flows, and it is not a design for any screen. It is what can be said now.

- **The interface is paper too, of a plainer kind.** The world is coloured paper. The interface is cream stock with ink, so that it never competes with the world.
- **It rests on the world.** Slips slide in from the edges and cast a shadow on the scene. The world stays visible behind and is never blacked out.
- **One ink means "you can press this".** World colours are not used for interface states.
- **Words are few,** set in a rounded display face for names and a plain face for the little there is to read.
- **Icons are cut paper,** made the way the world's things are made.
- **Touch feels like pressing paper.** A pressed control sinks and its shadow tightens. A dragged slip lifts at the corner. A stamp lands.
- **Flourishes are short** and never delay what the player came to do. Lasting delight belongs in the world.
- **Readable first:** large touch targets, strong contrast, and nothing told by colour alone.

Three materials for the interface are set side by side in a study, to choose between or to mix:

| Direction | Material | Feel |
|---|---|---|
| Scrapbook | Torn slips, tape, pencil, rubber stamps | The most handmade, and the busiest |
| Field guide | A naturalist's notebook: cream pages, ink labels, specimen tags | Calm and ordered; suits the instruments |
| Paper theatre | Bold flat card, thick tabs, punched holes | Toy-like and bright; the largest touch targets |

## 10. Sound

- **The game is whole with the sound off.** Phones are often muted, and nothing is told by sound alone.
- **Each place has a bed:** wind on the tops, a hush and drips in the wood, dry insects on the heath, grass and crickets on the plain, lapping at the lakes, squelch and reed rattle in the marsh, hiss on the dunes, surf on the flats. The bed changes with the time of day, the season and the weather.
- **Creatures have voices.** A species' named calls are real sounds. Body size sets the pitch, the body plan sets the tone, and each call is a short pattern picked from a set: a chirp, a whistle, a click, a hum, a trill. Calls are public signals, so a player can come to know a friend's species by ear.
- **The interface sounds like paper:** a rustle, a snip, a stamp, a page turning.
- **Music is sparse.** A few notes mark the name passing on, a species ending and a record. There is no loop.
- **Sound comes from where it happens** and fades with distance. At the basin's distance there is only the bed.
- **Made in the browser where possible,** to keep the download small. A browser allows sound only after the first touch.

## 11. Feel

- **Nothing snaps.** Things ease in and settle.
- **Everything alive breathes and blinks.**
- **The world is already going on** when the game opens.
- **Quiet, including deaths.** No alarms and no red flashes. A creature falls on its side and becomes a pale cut-out.
- **Slow movement everywhere:** water, grass, cloud shadow, drifting leaves.

## 12. What it costs to draw

The second art study shaded every pixel, which is too heavy for a phone. Proposed for the game:

- **A species is baked once into a small sheet of pieces:** body, face, signature part, feet and any other parts.
- **Body language moves those few pieces.** Nothing is drawn frame by frame.
- **Light is a tint and a shadow offset** over the whole scene, and grain is one overlay.
- **Detail follows distance:** faces only close up, simple shapes at the biome's distance, washes at the basin's.

How many creatures a mid-range phone can draw at each distance has not been measured.

## 14. Answered

Decided on 15 November:

1. **Bearings.** A mood wears one of eight bearings, picked by the player when the mood is made.
2. **Voices.** A species' named calls are real sounds picked from a set.
3. **Deaths are quiet:** a fall and a pale cut-out, with nothing gory.
4. **The interface's material is the scrapbook:** torn slips, tape, pencil and rubber stamps.
5. **The view is from above,** confirmed after seeing both views.
6. **The world and the creatures are not yet good enough.** The studies in section 13 lack small details, a handmade feel and a calm picture to look at, and they do not show how a creature with many parts would be drawn.
7. **Depth before breadth.** One small thing is worked on until it looks like a beautifully crafted game, and only then is it extended or turned into a design language. Each round is judged by an independent reviewer looking at screenshots, with no knowledge of what its maker thinks of it.

## 16. The depth study so far

`design/mockups/paper-study.html` has been through five passes. Four were judged by a fresh independent reviewer each time, from screenshots only.

| Review | Plain creature | Many-part creature | Environment | Paper illusion |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 4 | | 4.5 | |
| 2 | 4.5 | | 5 | |
| 3 | 5 | 4 | 5.5 | 4.5 |
| 4 | 5 | 4 | 6 | 5 |

Scores are out of ten against a beautiful shipped game. Each reviewer came to it cold, so the scores are not a strict series. The fifth pass has not been reviewed.

**What the passes settled, as rules:**

- **One light.** A window to the upper left. Every piece has a height. It gets a tight dark shadow where it touches and a short one thrown by its height, tinted blue-green on grass and water and brown on sand.
- **Flat sheets.** No gradients and nothing translucent. Form comes from how sheets are stacked, from a lit cut edge and a shaded one, and from holes cut through to the sheet beneath.
- **Paper stocks.** Each stock is a small relief lit from the same side: coarse sugar paper for ground, smooth card for creatures, kraft for sand, none for water.
- **Cut by hand.** Outlines wander and wobble, with the odd straight snip.
- **A creature is one spine,** mirrored about it. The order of layers is fixed: tail, limbs, body, things on the back, head, things on the head, face. Each layer up is a little lighter.
- **Each kind of part has its own shape and its own socket.** Ears are round-ended with an inner layer. Horns are crescents. Thorns are folded triangles in one row. Feelers are tapered strips from the brow. Repeated parts shrink toward the tail.
- **Three papers and one bright one** for each creature.
- **The body has to show.** Thin and round, a patch, plates and a shell are all read on the body. A head as wide as the body hid them, so the head is about four fifths of the body's width.
- **The eyes are the darkest marks,** matte, with one dot of light fixed to the window.
- **Low plants are under a creature and tall ones over it.**

**Range tests,** all from the same kit, on the same page: eight very different creatures, one creature in eight states, four other biomes and four events. The creatures and the places came out distinct. The states read once the body was made visible. None of these has been reviewed or polished.

**What is still short of the bar,** by the reviewers and by eye: the props (stones, toadstools, the log, grass) are weaker than the water and the leaves; the many-part creature's parts still look set on in places; and no reviewer has yet said the picture reads as photographed paper.


## 17. Five focused reviews, and what each changed

After the fifth pass, five independent reviewers each took one subject: how the creature moves, the world, states, creature designs, and single assets. Their reports are kept in `design/reviews/`. The study was then revised one subject at a time. This section records what each revision settled, as rules to carry into the real game.

### Set 1: how a creature moves

The reviewer scored the motion 3 out of 10: nothing changed the outline, and paws slid.

- **A paw on the ground stays on the ground.** A step is a stance and a swing; how often it steps follows how fast it goes.
- **Paper does not stretch.** Height is shown by size and by how far the shadow falls.
- **The eyes lead, then the head, then the body.** Ears and tail follow late on springs and overshoot.
- **An act changes the outline, and the world answers it.** A startle is a flinch, a hop away and a landing. A bitten flower tips and springs back. Each lap at the water sends out a ring.
- **Sleep is staged:** lids, ears, a settle, head round, tail wrapped, hind paws tucked, then front.
- **A heavy many-part body moves slower,** with legs that bend at hip and knee and a tail whose joints follow one after another.
- Poses can be held at twelve a second for a cut-out feel. Whether that is better is to be judged in motion.

### Set 2: the world

The reviewer scored composition 4.5, light 4.5, world motion 3, biome identity 3.5 and events 3.

**How a screen is composed:**

- At most three terraces and one cut-down. Each step up is lighter and warmer. A step throws a short firm shadow and a wide faint one.
- About sixty per cent of the screen is quiet paper, in one piece, where creatures walk.
- Things are gathered in three clusters and no more, each where two things meet: an edge and water, the foot of a step, where a path crosses an edge. Nothing stands alone or is evenly scattered.
- One line, a path or a shore, leads from a screen edge to the focal feature, which sits on a third. Water leaves by one edge at most.
- Shade comes only from something on the screen and has its shape. There is one mass of it.
- The creature is the most saturated shape. The scene reads in three values: deep water and tree for the darks, grass for the middle, sand and path for the lights.
- Nothing is smaller than about 12 pixels on a phone, and no line thinner than 1.5.

**How the world moves:**

- One wind for everything. A slow rocking crosses the picture, and every ten seconds or so a gust leans grass, reeds and leaves downwind as it passes.
- One hero mover, the creature, and one secondary, here a butterfly that settles on a flower when nothing is near.
- Small events at long intervals: glints on water, a fish that rises, a petal on a gust, the shadow of a cloud about once a minute.
- The world answers the creature: grass bends away from it and springs back.
- The view drifts a little after the creature, and what is high overhead slides further than the ground.

**Places and events:**

- Each place has one form that is its own: folded stone with scree in the highlands; roots, banked leaves and coins of sun in woodland; crescent dunes with a dark slip face; one braided channel through cracked mud in the marsh.
- Weather and time are built in a fixed order: what happens to the ground, then the light (its angle, length, colour and strength), then a layer in the air with its own shadow, then what the creature does. A tint alone is never enough.
- The whole scene's look is set by a few controls: the colour of every sheet, the pale rim on lit edges, and the colour, length and strength of shadows. Rain, winter and night are made with those.

### Set 3: how it is and how it feels

The reviewer scored the states 3.5 out of 10: only asleep could be named with its caption covered, because every other state was the same outline with a different eyelid or tint.

- **Posture first, face second.** Every body has the same few things that can change: the length of the spine; the width at the middle, the hips and the shoulders; where the head is and how low; how far the limbs are spread or tucked; and how high it stands, which is how far its shadow falls. Each state and each bearing is keyed there first.
- **Each state gets one thing that changes the outline and one to see close up.** Hungry: a pinched middle, and ribs. Fed: a round middle with the belly showing at both sides. Hurt: a paw held in, and a white plaster. Cold: drawn in short with paws under, and a puff of breath. Hot: spread flat and wide, and a flicked drop. With eggs: widest at the hips, and eggs at the rim. Old: head low, and creases with a nick in one ear. Tired: head sunk and tipped, and uneven lids. Ill: a stagger, and ringed spots.
- **Colour belongs to the player.** No state changes it except fading, where the pattern goes first and a pale torn rim appears. Cold, old and dead no longer go pale.
- **Dead is the cut-out turned over:** plain paper, no face, limbs straight, flat on the ground with no shadow.
- **A closed eye means sleep.** The white of an eye shows only when it is wide or looking aside, so alarm reads from far off. Slanted lids are kept for fierce. Nothing uses the mouth, and nothing uses a letter.
- **Each also has a pace and one rhythm of its own:** a hungry one sweeps its head, a hurt one limps, a cold one shivers in bursts, a tired one nods and jerks awake, one with eggs waddles; a wary one stops to listen, a weary one stops to rest, a playful one hops.
- **The eight bearings are postures:** wary long and low and turned half away; fierce a wedge thrust at its target; eager stretched with its head far ahead; calm a loaf; tender bent in a C around another; weary spread and sagging; playful a bow; gloomy hunched small and turned away. Weary spreads and gloomy contracts; fierce points and wary angles away.
- **States are rows of numbers, not drawings.** A new state is a new row, and any body that has the posture can wear it.

Not yet done: the same states on the other bodies, which waits for set 4, where every body is built on the same posture.

### Set 4: creature designs, and one kit for all of them

The reviewer agreed with the owner: the eight varied creatures were one teardrop with the same head, face and stub legs, varied only by what sat on the back. The plated one and the hunter were the weakest.

**What changed.** Every creature is now a row of choices for one kit, and the choices are the ones the game's body design gives a player: a plan, a shape, a size, parts on the outline, a skin, a pattern and papers. The study has sixteen rows. The plated one became one idea, armour. The hunter became a forward stare and big paws, with no frown. Eight are new, and go where variety had not been: a round body with no head, one wider than it is long, a ribbon with no limbs, a body on stilts, one that wears a found thing, one with no front, one that sits, and a giant with its young riding.

**Rules for a design that looks good:**

- The head is 35 to 45% of the length and nearly as wide as the body. Eyes are a fifth to a quarter of the head's width, in the front half, set inside the outline.
- Limbs show: they stand out from the body by an eighth to a quarter of its width, with round ends.
- One idea for each creature. Its largest part is its signature, and gets the one bright paper.
- A predator is made by a forward stare, a wide head and big forepaws. Frowning brows are refused.
- Nothing that reads as a wound: marks are laid on, never cut through on the centre line.
- A design is judged twice: about a centimetre long, and as a cut-out in one colour.

**Rules the kit keeps, so that players' combinations stay good:**

- The player picks hues. The kit sets the steps of lightness between touching parts, and moves a body that is too close to the ground it stands on.
- Eyes are placed by ratio and pulled inside the outline, whatever the head.
- The head takes at most two kinds of thing on it; the back takes one skin.
- Young follow by rule: a larger head, eyes closer, shorter limbs, a simpler pattern.

**How it is made to be added to:**

- Each kind of part, a limb, a tail, a head, a thing on the head, a skin, a thing worn, is one entry in a table, drawn at a socket the body gives it. A new part is a new entry, and every body can then have it.
- Every body answers to the same posture, so every state and bearing fits every body, including ones added later. Where a body has no ears, feelers, horns or fronds answer; where it has no tail, the last of its spine does.
- Any body can walk, feed, drink, sleep and startle, because each act asks only for the posture and for how far the nose is from the middle.

Parts of the game's body design not yet drawn in the kit: eyes on stalks, a skirt of cilia, suckers and hooks, a pouch, a sting, a beak, a tongue, bristles and slime. Each is one more entry.
