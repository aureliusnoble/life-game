# Look, sound and feel

Draft 1, November 2026. Design area 12 in `design/plan.md`. It builds on `design/art-direction.md`. Section 1 is decided. The rest is a proposal, and none of it is final art.

## 1. Decided

- **Soft critters in the Paper lagoon direction:** cut and layered paper, like a picture book.
- **"Expressive" means two things:** more variety in what a creature can be built from, and bodies that show what the creature is doing and feeling.
- **The look is the game's own,** and places should feel designed, beautiful and relaxing.
- **Little text.** The world, the animation and the graphics have to let a player see what is happening.
- **The interface is handcrafted, cute and intuitive without losing depth,** and depth is revealed a little at a time.
- **The mockups so far are concept tests.** The interface is designed later, in stages, starting from what each moment should feel like.
- **Creatures grow paler as their species ages** (`design/lifespan.md`).
- **It runs in a browser tab on a phone.**

## 2. What the look is for

The game explains almost nothing in words, so the look does the explaining. It has three jobs, in this order:

1. **Say what is happening.** A player can read what a creature is doing and how it is, at each of three distances.
2. **Make the place worth looking at.** Looking around is meant to be fun in itself.
3. **Feel made by hand,** by someone, for this group.

Where these pull apart, the first wins.

## 3. The paper rulebook

"Paper" is a set of rules that everything on screen obeys, the world and the interface alike.

| Rule | What it means |
|---|---|
| Everything is cut from paper | One sheet for each shape, in one flat colour. No outlines, and no shading inside a shape |
| Sheets stack | Ground, low plants, creatures, tall plants, weather, and then the interface. Each sheet drops a soft shadow on the one below |
| There is one light | The sun crosses the sky each day. Shadows are short at noon and long and warm at dusk. Night is a blue wash with small warm glows |
| Edges are cut by hand | A little uneven, with a pale lip on the lit side where the paper's thickness shows |
| Paper has grain | A faint fibre texture over everything. Colour differs from sheet to sheet and never within one |
| Things move as paper moves | They hinge, slide, flutter, curl and pop up |
| Depth comes from sliding | When the view moves, higher sheets move a little farther than lower ones |
| Few colours at a time | Each place has five or six, with paper white and ink. Large areas stay calm and detail gathers in clusters |
| Creatures stand clear of the ground | A species' colours come from a set of inks checked against all eight grounds. Its shadow and pale lip separate it where colours are close |

How paper handles what it is bad at:

| Thing | In paper |
|---|---|
| Water | Strips sliding past each other, paler over darker; deeper water has more strips |
| Grass and reeds | Rows of flaps hinged at the base, which part as a creature passes and stay bent where they were grazed |
| Rain, snow, blown sand | Slivers, punched dots, streaks |
| Fire | Flickering tongues of layered orange, leaving charred black with a glowing edge |
| Mist and spray | Sheets of stippled tissue |
| Glow | A halo sheet under the glowing part, strongest at night |
| Darkness in a cave | A dark sheet with a hole cut round what the followed creature can sense |
| Cover over a creature | The canopy or the reed stand is cut away round the creature being followed |

## 4. Three distances

| Distance | On screen | What has to read |
|---|---|---|
| The basin | The whole map, each species a wash of its colour | Where each species is, how many, and whether it is growing |
| A biome | Creatures as small shapes | Whose they are, what they are doing, groups, chases and deaths |
| Close | A few creatures with faces | Mood, need, what each has noticed, and signals |

Each piece of body language is designed for the farthest distance it has to read at. What a creature is doing is carried by movement and outline, so that it reads in the biome view. Faces are for close up.

## 5. Bodies that show

A body shows four things, each carried by a different part of it so that they do not compete.

| What | Carried by | Reads at |
|---|---|---|
| What it is doing | The movement of the whole body: the gait, the lunge, the huddle | Biome and close |
| How it is | The body's condition: thin or round, a patch where it was hurt, a shiver, colour faded with age | Close. Thinness and a limp also at biome |
| What it feels | The face and the bearing: eyes, the signature part, posture | Close |
| What it is saying | Signals, drawn in paper: rings for a call, a raised flag for a display, a chit left on the ground for a scent | Biome and close |

### What it is doing

| Act | The body |
|---|---|
| approach, stay near | Leans toward it, signature part forward, steady hops |
| move away, keep apart | Leans away and looks back over its shoulder |
| wander | Loose, uneven hops, looking about |
| circle, go along, go the same way, head off | The body turned along its path and the head turned to what it follows |
| face, hold still | Stops square on to it. Held still, only the eyes move |
| creeping | Low and stretched, feet placed slowly |
| sprinting | Flat, signature part streaming back, flecks of ground thrown up |
| bite, to eat | Head down, cheeks working, crumbs |
| bite, to wound | A lunge and a snap. The other flinches and a patch tears |
| rest | Folds down flat, eyes shut, slow breathing |
| lay | Squats and wiggles, and an egg slides out |
| mate | The two press together and wiggle as one |
| grab, carry, drop | Lifts it overhead or in the mouth, and waddles under the weight |
| give | Holds it out. The other takes it and both bob |
| tend | Leans over and pats. The one tended settles |
| cling | Flattened on to the carrier, moving as it moves |
| hide | Pulled into its shell or hollow, eyes showing |
| roll | Flips belly up and back |
| shed | The tail tears off and is left wriggling |
| sting, spit, shock, spray | A jab; an arc of dots; a zigzag burst; a stippled cloud |
| puff up, play dead, crouch | Swells to a bigger outline; flops on its side; flattens and takes the ground's colour |
| mark, wipe | Presses down and leaves a small coloured chit; scuffs one away |
| dig, build | Flings chips of ground behind it; sets pieces down one at a time |
| call, show | Mouth open, rings spreading in the call's colour; a frill or flag raised in the display's colour |

### How it is

| State | The body |
|---|---|
| Hungry | Thinner and slower, and it looks at food |
| Fed | Rounder |
| Tired | Heavy lids, dragging, yawns |
| Hurt | A torn patch, later a stitched one, and a limp on that side |
| Cold | Shivers, huddles small, a blue lip |
| Hot | Flattened and panting |
| Stung or ill | A green tinge and a wobble |
| Carrying eggs | Rounder low down, and slower |
| Young | A larger head for its body, paler, tumbling |
| Old | Softened edges, slower |
| Its species fading | The whole species paler, day by day, as paper bleaches in the sun |
| Dead | Falls on its side and becomes a pale cut-out, which fades as it is eaten or rots |

### What it feels

Moods are the player's own. Each is named by the player and given an icon, and has no fixed meaning, so the body cannot know how to show one.

Proposed: when a player makes a mood they also pick its **bearing** from a small set. A creature wears the bearing of its strongest mood, more plainly as the mood rises.

| Bearing | Posture | Eyes | Signature part | Movement |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Wary | Low and drawn back | Wide, small pupils, darting | Flat back | Short quick steps, freezing between |
| Fierce | Forward and swollen | Narrow and slanted | Raised stiff | Stamping |
| Eager | Up on its toes, leaning in | Large pupils, fixed | Pricked | Bouncing |
| Calm | Loose and settled | Half closed | Relaxed | Slow |
| Tender | Leaning to the other, head tilted | Soft, slow blinks, a blush | Folded forward | Gentle |
| Weary | Sagging | Heavy lids | Drooping | Dragging |
| Playful | Wiggling, head tilted | Bright | Flicking | Hops and spins |
| Gloomy | Head down, small | Downcast | Drooping | Slow, with stops |

- **The player's icon** appears above the creature on a small paper tag at the moment a mood starts. It stays up while the player is watching it think.
- **Feelings the game defines,** such as startled and outnumbered, have fixed flinches of their own.

### What it has noticed

Before it acts on something, a creature glances at it: the head turns and the signature part points. This is how a player sees, in the world, that a creature saw the hunter and went on eating.

## 6. Variety

- **Body plans** set the outline: bun, tadpole, pear, bean, noodle, chonk, and more to come.
- **Each part is a cut piece** that tucks under or over the body's sheet, so no part looks stuck on. Parts take their colours from the species' inks.
- **One signature feature for each species,** which reads in outline: ears, a crest, a tail, a frill. It is also the part that carries most of the bearing.
- **Forms share the base outline.** A caste or a sex is told apart by the parts that differ.
- **The young** are the same sheets with a larger head.
- **At the basin's distance** a species is its pair of colours. The maker's mark is a stamp on portraits and lists, and is not worn on bodies.

## 7. The eight places

| Biome | Colours | Its paper | Movement | Through the year |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Highlands | Grey-violet stone, lichen green, snow white, rust | Rock in stacked sheets like contour lines; scree chips; pools as cut holes; cave mouths as dark cut-outs | Cloud shadows sliding across; wind streaks | The snow line comes down in winter and retreats in summer |
| Woodland | Deep greens, bark brown, mushroom orange, yellow light | A canopy sheet above the creatures with holes that let light through; trunks as dark discs; leaf litter as confetti; fungus rings | Patches of light drifting over the floor | Leaf fall in autumn; a bare canopy in winter lets the light in |
| Heath | Ochre, heather pink, sage, char black | Scrub as crumpled tufts; water holes; burnt ground as black paper with a glowing edge | Heat shimmer; fire late in summer | Pink in flower, then fire, then green shoots on the black |
| Grassland | Green, turning gold, straw and dun | Rows of grass flaps; flowers that open by day; two rivers as sliding blue strips | Wind crossing the grass in waves | The strongest change: green, gold, brown, frost white |
| Lakeland | Blues, teal, lily green, reed gold | Water in strips, darker with depth; lily pads; islands; reeds at the rim | Ripple rings; drifting pads | A green bloom after rain; a white rim of ice in winter |
| Marsh | Olive, mud brown, straw, russet, mist grey | Reed stands as tall flaps that hide what is inside; glossy mud; channels | Mist drifting; bubbles | Spring floods slide a sheet of water over it; winter lays the reeds flat |
| Dunes | Cream sand, peach shadow, grey-green marram, driftwood | Sand as scalloped ripples; hollows with green in them; a driftline of bits | Ripples creeping; blown streaks. Footprints punch in and fill within the hour | Little change, and storms |
| Tidal flats | Wet slate, silver blue, weed green, shell pink | The sea as one broad sheet with a scalloped foam edge; pools left behind; worm casts | The tide sliding in and out several times a day | Little change |

- **Use shows.** Grazed flaps stay bent, paths are scuffed paler, and ground eaten bare is brown.
- **Borders are places.** Where two biomes meet their sheets interleave.
- **Every visit has a different light,** because a day is an hour.

## 8. The interface: direction only

This is not the style system, which comes after the flows, and it is not a design for any screen. It is what can be said now.

- **The interface is paper too, of a plainer kind.** The world is coloured paper. The interface is cream stock with ink, so that it never competes with the world.
- **It rests on the world.** Slips slide in from the edges and cast a shadow on the scene. The world stays visible behind and is never blacked out.
- **One ink means "you can press this".** World colours are not used for interface states.
- **Words are few,** set in a rounded display face for names and a plain face for the little there is to read.
- **Icons are cut paper,** made the way the world's things are made.
- **Touch feels like pressing paper.** A pressed control sinks and its shadow tightens. A dragged slip lifts at the corner. A stamp lands.
- **Flourishes are short** and never delay what the player came to do. Lasting delight belongs in the world.
- **Readable first:** large touch targets, strong contrast, and nothing told by colour alone.

Three materials for the interface are set side by side in a study, to choose between or to mix:

| Direction | Material | Feel |
|---|---|---|
| Scrapbook | Torn slips, tape, pencil, rubber stamps | The most handmade, and the busiest |
| Field guide | A naturalist's notebook: cream pages, ink labels, specimen tags | Calm and ordered; suits the instruments |
| Paper theatre | Bold flat card, thick tabs, punched holes | Toy-like and bright; the largest touch targets |

## 9. Sound

- **The game is whole with the sound off.** Phones are often muted, and nothing is told by sound alone.
- **Each place has a bed:** wind on the tops, a hush and drips in the wood, dry insects on the heath, grass and crickets on the plain, lapping at the lakes, squelch and reed rattle in the marsh, hiss on the dunes, surf on the flats. The bed changes with the time of day, the season and the weather.
- **Creatures have voices.** A species' named calls are real sounds. Body size sets the pitch, the body plan sets the tone, and each call is a short pattern picked from a set: a chirp, a whistle, a click, a hum, a trill. Calls are public signals, so a player can come to know a friend's species by ear.
- **The interface sounds like paper:** a rustle, a snip, a stamp, a page turning.
- **Music is sparse.** A few notes mark the name passing on, a species ending and a record. There is no loop.
- **Sound comes from where it happens** and fades with distance. At the basin's distance there is only the bed.
- **Made in the browser where possible,** to keep the download small. A browser allows sound only after the first touch.

## 10. Feel

- **Nothing snaps.** Things ease in and settle.
- **Everything alive breathes and blinks.**
- **The world is already going on** when the game opens.
- **Quiet, including deaths.** No alarms and no red flashes. A creature falls on its side and becomes a pale cut-out.
- **Slow movement everywhere:** water, grass, cloud shadow, drifting leaves.

## 11. What it costs to draw

The second art study shaded every pixel, which is too heavy for a phone. Proposed for the game:

- **A species is baked once into a small sheet of pieces:** body, face, signature part, feet and any other parts.
- **Body language moves those few pieces.** Nothing is drawn frame by frame.
- **Light is a tint and a shadow offset** over the whole scene, and grain is one overlay.
- **Detail follows distance:** faces only close up, simple shapes at the biome's distance, washes at the basin's.

How many creatures a mid-range phone can draw at each distance has not been measured.

## 13. Questions

1. **Bearings.** A mood wears one of eight bearings, picked by the player when the mood is made. Proposed: yes.
2. **Voices.** A species' named calls are real sounds picked from a set. Proposed: yes.
3. **Deaths are quiet:** a fall and a pale cut-out, with nothing gory. Proposed: yes.
4. **The interface's material:** scrapbook, field guide, paper theatre, or a mix.
