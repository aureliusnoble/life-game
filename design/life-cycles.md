# Life cycles: generations, stages and ways of living through the year

Draft 2, November 2026. Design area 2 in `design/plan.md`. A proposal. The numbers are starting points; balance is settled at the end of the design process, not here.

## 1. What this area has to give the game

- **Many generations in one species' life.** The old die, the young replace them, and the species is extinct when the last one dies.
- **A second design space beside body and brain:** when to breed, where, how many young, and how much care.
- **A rhythm for the daily visit.** What you saw as eggs yesterday should be doing something different today.
- **A line to follow.** The name you gave the founder passes down it.
- **Seasons that matter.** There should be several good answers to winter.

## 2. Time

| | Game days | Real time |
|---|---|---|
| A day | 1 | About an hour |
| A season | 48 | About two days |
| A year | 192 | About eight days |
| A species' life | 72 to 168 | Three to seven days |

A species lives through two to four seasons, and which ones depends on when it is released.

## 3. Generations

Size sets the tempo of a life (`design/body.md`, section 4): small lives fast, large lives slow.

| Body | Egg to adult | Lifespan, if nothing kills it | Generations in a five-day species life | What you see on a daily visit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Small | About 3 game days | About 12 | Around 25 | Several generations have come and gone since yesterday |
| Plain | About 8 | About 30 | Around 10 | Two generations on from yesterday |
| Large | About 14 | About 50 | 6 or more | Yesterday's young are grown and laying |

Decided: even a large species should go through six or more generations.

- **Every individual dies of age.** A species that stops breeding is gone within one lifespan.
- **Lifespan follows size, with one design choice on top:** lay once and die, or lay again and again (section 6).
- **Hunger and injury shorten a life.** A hard life is a short one.

## 4. Stages

| Stage | What it is | What the world does to it | What the designer controls |
|---|---|---|---|
| Egg | Cannot move or sense. Sits where it was laid | Eaten by egg thieves; chilled, flooded or dried out; can lie dormant | Where it is laid, how many, how large, whether it waits for a season |
| Young | A smaller body with the same brain. Cannot breed | Fits where adults cannot, eats smaller food, and more things can eat it | Instincts marked "only when young"; parts tied to the stage, so young can have a different body |
| Adult | Full size. Breeds | Everything else in the design docs | Everything |
| Elder | Past breeding. Slower | Dies of age | Instincts marked "only when old": elders can guard, lead to remembered places, or be the first to face a hunter |
| Remains | A body where it fell | Food for scavengers, then richness in the ground | Nothing, except by eating their own dead |

Elders are new. They give the oldest animals something to be for, and they are where a line's memories live longest.

## 5. How a species breeds

Decided: the way of breeding is chosen per species.

| Way | How it works | What it gives | What it costs |
|---|---|---|---|
| Alone | Any adult can lay | Simple and reliable; one survivor can restart the species | One body and one set of habits for all |
| In pairs | Two sexes, which must meet | A second body and second set of habits: one sex can be built to guard, fight or show off while the other carries the eggs. Courtship, rivalry and choosing a mate become possible | Mates have to find each other, and a species scattered thin can fail to |
| One breeder, many helpers | See `design/social.md` | Castes | The breeder is a single point of failure |
| Both sexes in one | Any two adults can mate, and both lay | Mates are twice as easy to find | No second body: there is only one sex to design |
| Splitting | An adult divides into two small ones. There is no egg | Nothing for egg thieves to take, and no mate needed | The parent gives up half its weight each time |

What two sexes are worth, beyond being expressive, is an open balance question. It is noted here and left for the end.

## 6. Life-history choices

Each is made with instincts and a few species settings, not a separate system. Strategies for mothers, fathers and helpers are taken further in `design/social.md`, section 6.

| Choice | Options | The trade |
|---|---|---|
| When to lay | Whenever fed; only in a season; only when it is safe, uncrowded or food is plentiful | Laying freely booms and can eat a place bare; holding back keeps the food but gives ground to rivals |
| How many | Many small eggs, or a few large ones | Small eggs are cheap to lose and hatch weak; large young start strong |
| How often | Once, with everything, and die; or again and again | One huge brood swamps the egg thieves and suits a short season; repeated broods spread the risk |
| Care | None; guard the eggs; feed the young; carry them | Care raises survival and ties a parent down |
| Where | Scattered; in a nest; hidden; in a shared nest | A nest can be guarded and found again; it can also be found by others |
| Eggs | Laid, or kept inside until they hatch | Kept inside they are safe from thieves and weather, and the mother is slower and at risk for longer |
| Waiting | Eggs that wait for spring; adults that sleep through winter; neither | Waiting skips the bad season and skips its opportunities too |
| Growing up | Young like small adults; or a young form with its own body, such as fins first and legs later. Decided: young forms are in the first version | A young form opens a second habitat and costs budget |

## 7. Ways of living through the year

Each of these should be buildable from the choices above and the behaviour vocabulary, and each should be the best answer somewhere.

| Way of life | Built from | Where it should pay |
|---|---|---|
| One-season boom | Lay once and die; eggs wait for spring | Grassland, with the strongest seasons |
| Sleep through it | Store fat; adults sleep in winter | Highlands and anywhere with a long winter |
| Follow the seasons | Move between biomes as food comes and goes | Along the rivers; Grassland to Marsh or Tidal flats |
| Stay and hoard | Carry and store food; hold a territory | Woodland, where fruiting comes in bursts |
| Two homes | A young form in water, adults on land | Lakeland and its shores |
| Slow and steady | Large, long-lived, few well-tended young | Places that change little |
| Fast and many | Small, quick generations, no care | After fires, floods and blooms: Heath, Marsh, Tidal flats |

The stand-in world has run the first two and a fast breeder (`design/world.md`, section 9): sleeping led wherever there was a winter, waiting eggs came second everywhere, and the fast breeder led with no winter and died out in a long one.

## 8. Fading and the end of a species

Decided on 15 November:

- **Fading is the same for every species of the same age.** It does not look at how a species is doing.
- **It keeps rising,** so pushing against it gets harder all the time and in the end is almost impossible.
- **A better design lasts longer.** A beginner's species may last a few days; there has to be room for a really successful one to last a good deal longer.

Still open, for area 7: what fading acts on. It should weigh on everything a species does, in equal measure, so that no way of life escapes it. Acting through the life cycle as well, with fewer eggs hatching and lives shortening, would make a species dwindle generation by generation until its last member dies of age.

## 9. The named line

- **The name-bearer** is the founder you named, and after its death an heir.
- **The heir** is a descendant with most of its life ahead of it: a young adult if there is one, otherwise the oldest of the young. Failing any descendant, the nearest living relative. The line ends only when the species does.
- **What passes down:** the name with a number, and a short list of memories, such as where the nest was, what killed the last name-bearer, and the worst winter.
- **Tempo shows here too.** A small species reaches "the Twentieth" in a few days; a large one may still be on its founder's grandchild.

Which memories pass, and how many, belongs to area 9.

## 10. What the player sees

- **The shape of the species:** eggs, young, adults and elders as four counts or a small stacked bar.
- **Generation number,** and which generation the name-bearer is.
- **Events in the recap:** a brood hatched, the last elder died, the nest was raided, the name passed on.

## 11. Words the behaviour vocabulary needs

Most exist already. To check against `design/neural-blocks.md` in area 6:

- **Conditions:** only when young, adult or old; only when female or male; in a season.
- **Things to notice:** my eggs, my young, my parent, my mate, a nest.
- **Acts:** lay, guard, feed young, carry young, sleep.
- **New here:** "old" as a stage, and feeding young.
