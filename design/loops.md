# Player loops: the first hour, the daily visit, the design session, the end

Draft 2, November 2026. Design area 8 in `design/plan.md`. A proposal, written as flows: each numbered step is one screen or one moment. This is the stage before wireframes, and nothing here has been tried on a person.

It leans on the interface research (`reports/Handcrafted game UI and onboarding.md`), which rests on search summaries, and on the decisions in the other design docs.

## 1. The loops, and why a player comes back

| Loop | How long | How often | The reason to come back |
|---|---|---|---|
| The first hour | 10 to 15 minutes guided, then free | Once | Your eggs hatch overnight |
| The daily visit | About 15 minutes | Daily | What happened to them, the name passing on, the season turning, what your friends' species did |
| The design session | Half an hour or more | When you choose: about weekly | An opening in the world, something unlocked, or a species near its end |
| The end | A few minutes | Each time a species ends | The shape of its life, its place among friends' species, and the next idea |

Rules that run through all of them:

- **Open on the living world,** with your named one on screen. Never on a menu.
- **Teach by doing, in the real editor.** One idea at a time, a few words each, and only where the player is about to act.
- **Unlock by doing, never by date.**
- **Show, don't tell.** Little text and no over-explaining. The world, the animation, the graphics and the tools have to let a player see what is happening, and looking around should be fun in itself.
- **Nothing nags.** No streaks, no alert for every need, at most one notification a day, asked for only after the player has a reason to want it.
- **Everything works in a browser tab** from a link, with nothing to install and no account to make first.

## 2. The first hour

The aim is a living creature of the player's own, doing something they taught it, within about five minutes of opening the link, and released into the shared world within fifteen.

| Step | What the player sees | What they do | What it teaches |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Arrive | The friends' basin, close in on a quiet corner where a few preset creatures are feeding. One line: "Everything here was made by someone." | Type a name. Nothing else is asked | That it is a living, shared place |
| 2. A body | The body designer with a plain starter body on the table, not a blank page | Three small choices, each a callout: where its eyes look; legs or fins; its colours | A body is shaped, not picked from a list. Every choice is valid |
| 3. First instinct | The test tank. Their creature wanders past a plant without noticing it. A half-written row: "see a plant → ..." | Pick "approach". Then add "touch a plant → bite" | An instinct is "notice → act". The creature eats: the first success |
| 4. Fear | A hunter enters the tank and catches it. It hatches again | Add "see a bigger creature → move away", and set it to strong | Strength. Instincts add up. Tapping the creature shows what is pulling it |
| 5. Where to live | The basin, with two or three places lit where there is food and room | Tap one | The world has places, and they differ |
| 6. Release | A clutch of eggs in the chosen place | Name the first one to hatch | The named line |
| 7. Meet it | Close in on the egg. It hatches and looks about. The tending screen opens for the first time | Stroke it, feed it, say hello. It answers in its own way | That it knows the player is there, and that this one is theirs |
| 8. Until tomorrow | A line on what the night will bring, and the way back in: a link or a code, offered, not required | Leave, or stay and play | The reason to return |

Decided: time with the named one is part of day one, so the first session ends on tending it, not on a menu.

What the first hour leaves out, on purpose: moods, signals, forms, the inside of the body, the budget and other players' species. Each arrives later, one at a time, with something to do.

After step 8 the player can stay. The test tank offers three or four small puzzles, each of which teaches one more word and unlocks it.

**The first unlock, on the first return:** moods. The creatures have spent a night fleeing and coming straight back to the hunter. "Scared" is offered as the fix.

## 3. The daily visit

About fifteen minutes, with nothing required. The order is a suggestion the screen makes, not a sequence the player is marched through.

| Step | What the player sees | What they can do |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Open | The world, close in on their named one, wherever it has got to. It notices them | Touch it |
| 2. Since yesterday | A card over the map, drawn on it: where they went, how many hatched and died, the generation number, the line of their living weight. One headline, such as "The name passed to Pip the Fourth" or "Yips found the herd". One thing worth doing | Skip it, or step through it. It is kept, and can be played again |
| 3. Tend | The named one, close | Stroke it, feed it. Later, talk to it through a fixed set of things to say, and learn what is troubling the species |
| 4. Watch | The species going about its day | Follow one. Tap any creature for what it is doing and why, then "watch it think" |
| 5. Nudge | The keeper's tools | Drop one of the species' own scents or calls; stir one of its moods; hold a finger down as a beacon |
| 6. Change | Three mutations, offered at random: small changes to the body, the instincts or the way of breeding | Pick one, or none. It applies to the newly hatched |
| 7. Unlocked | Anything earned since the last visit, one at a time, each with its tank puzzle | Try it, or leave it for later |
| 8. Friends | Rankings by total life, and one notable thing from a friend's species | Visit theirs. The design of any species that has ended can be opened |
| 9. Ahead | One line: "Winter begins tomorrow", "About three days left at this rate" | Leave |

Steps 1 to 3 are the Tamagotchi part and take two minutes. A player who does only those has still had a visit.

## 4. The design session

When the player chooses. A new species can be released at any time, and releasing it retires the current one.

| Step | What the player sees | What they do |
|---|---|---|
| 1. What happened | The last species' story: the shape of its life, what ended or is ending it, and which of its instincts fired | Read why |
| 2. Where the openings are | The basin: foods going uneaten, places with no hunters, places with too many, the season ahead | Pick a place and an idea |
| 3. Design | The body, the forms, the pages of instincts, the species settings. A preset or an old design can be opened as a starting point | Build |
| 4. Try it | The test tank, with the species now living in that place brought in | Watch, change, watch again |
| 5. Release, or hold | The basin and the season | Release now, or keep the design for a better moment |

## 5. The end

| Step | What the player sees | What they do |
|---|---|---|
| 1. The last one | The last member, and the moment it dies. If it is the named one, its name | Watch |
| 2. Its life | The shape of the species' whole life, the score, the records it set, its chronicle of major events, and the named line from first to last | Read; share with friends |
| 3. What is kept | The design, what it unlocked, its records | Open the design to change it, or start afresh |
| 4. What next | The openings in the world as they stand | Begin a design session, now or later |

## 6. Unlocks

Decided: unlocks widen play and do not add power, the budget never grows, and unlocks should draw players toward ways of playing they have not tried. Proposed:

- **Kits by way of life.** Parts and words arrive in small sets that suggest a style: a stalker's set, a swarm's set, a water set, a pair's set, a colony's set.
- **Earned by doing something new.** A first hunter, a first species to see a winter through, a first home founded, a first species in a new biome. Repeating a design earns nothing more.
- **Pointed at the world's openings.** When a food is going uneaten or a place has no hunters, the next kit on offer leans that way.
- **One at a time, with something to do.** Each word or part comes with a small tank puzzle that shows what it is for.
- **Never by the calendar.** The one well-documented failure in the research was a game that unlocked its tools by date.

## 7. Events

The game does not explain. It records what happened, classifies it, and shows it where it happened. The simulation knows what an event was, where, when and who was involved; it never guesses at why.

| Family | Events | Where it shows |
|---|---|---|
| Numbers | A brood hatched; the species doubled; a new peak; fell by half; down to the last few | The line of living weight; a mark on the map |
| Deaths, by cause | Hunted, and by whom; starved; cold; old age; drowned or swept away; killed by its own kind; a flood or a fire | A tally by cause; where they fell |
| The named line | The name passed on; how the last name-bearer died; the line reached another generation | The named one's portrait |
| Homes and spread | Reached a new biome; founded a home; lost a home; crossed a river; made a seasonal journey | Trails and pins on the map |
| Society | A new breeder; a home split in two; a fight between homes; a raid; first young raised by helpers | At the home |
| Other species | First met; began to be hunted by; began to hunt; a rival arrived on its food; a species it relied on is gone | The faces of the species involved |
| The world | First winter; a flood; a fire; a bloom; food left by the tide | The season line |
| The player | A mutation chosen; a nudge given; a species released or retired | The player's own mark |
| Records and endings | A record set; overtook a friend's species; retired; extinct | Badges |

- **Each event carries** its kind, time, place, who was involved and how big it was.
- **Major and minor.** Major events go into the species' chronicle and can be the day's headline. Minor ones show only on the map and the timeline.
- **One list, many uses:** the recap's headline, the chronicle kept at the end, pins on the map, what the named one remembers, what friends see, and the one notification a day.

## 8. Answered

Decided on 15 November:

1. **The first hour** leaves moods for later, and includes time with the named one.
2. **A living species changes by one mutation a day,** picked from three offered at random. This replaces free edits to a living species.
3. **Friends' designs** are open to all once the species has ended.
4. **Little text.** The game shows and does not explain, with events classified as in section 7.

**What a mutation is,** as a proposal: one small change that fits the species as it is. A part a size larger or smaller; a covering on one side; an instinct made stronger or weaker; a condition added; an instinct using a word the player has unlocked and not yet tried; a step in how many eggs or how large. One of the three could always be of the last kind, so that mutations also nudge players toward what they have not tried.
