Study · depth before breadth

One creature by the water

One small piece of the game, worked on until it looks made by hand: a single creature seen from above, in cut and layered paper. It began as one bank by a pond; the dunes and the marsh are now built to the same rules.

Sixteen creatures from one kit

Each is a row of choices, the ones a player makes: a shape, parts on the outline, a skin, a pattern, papers. The last eight go where variety had not been: round, wide, a ribbon, stilts, something worn, no front, sitting, a giant.

As cut-outs

A design is good when it can be told from the others by its outline alone.

How it is: twelve states

Shown with no words. Each state changes the outline, or puts a piece of its own colour beside the creature, so it can be told from far off: blue-white in front for cold, orange above for hot, purple and yellow rings for ill, a white plaster for hurt. Only fading changes the creature's own colour.

How it feels: eight bearings

A mood wears one of these. They are postures first: the length of the spine, the spread of the limbs, where the head is, and how high it stands.

The size of a fingertip

The same twenty-one, as small as a creature is when a whole bank is on the screen.

Remains

A creature is a stack of skin, flesh and bone, and death takes it apart from the top. The skin is its own sheets turned face down, with crosses where its eyes were, so it is still known whose it was. Rose showing means food is left; bites are clean scallops; it ends in flowers.

Every body, every state

States are rows of numbers for a posture every body has, so each of these was got by asking, not by drawing. Where a body has no ears or no tail, something else answers.

Assets, by one recipe

Each is named by its outline, drawn in plan from two to five flat pieces, and given one of four heights, which sets its shadow. The first eleven are the pond's. Then the general ones, eggs and tracks; things from the dunes and the marsh; and one made new from the recipe. They are shown larger than they are in the scene.

Two places as they began

Six places can be chosen from the menu under the picture: the pond, the dunes, the marsh, the highlands, the woodland and a lakeshore. These are the first sketches of two of them, kept to show how far a place has to come.

Four events

Weather and disaster, each built in the same order: what happens to the ground, then to the light, then a layer in the air, then what the creature does.

What this is

  • A paper diorama on a table, photographed from above in window light. Everything has a height, and its height decides how far and how soft its shadow falls.
  • Each sheet has the tooth of its own paper, a lit edge where it was cut and a shaded one, a tight shadow where it touches and one for its height. Shadows are one tone, their length is the height, and where two overlap they are no darker than one. Nothing has an outline, and nothing is shaded with a gradient.
  • The creature is built like a paper toy: a body over a paler under-sheet, a head that overlaps it, ears and a snout laid on top, and one shadow for the whole of it.
  • A place is a row too: its wind, its ground, what lives under a creature and what stands over it, and where a creature goes. The dunes have one dune, a damp hollow to drink at and a line of things the sea has left; each gust wipes the oldest prints. The marsh has a lane of dark water, a pool with a drowned tree, and a bed of reeds that a creature walks under and vanishes.
  • Every creature here comes from one kit. Each kind of part, a limb, a tail, a head, a skin, a thing on the head, is one entry in a table, drawn at a socket the body gives it. A new part is a new entry, a new creature is a new row, and any of the land ones can be chosen to walk the scene.
  • The ground steps down in three terraces to a pond cut through them, and a worn path runs from the top corner to the sand. Things are gathered in three places and no more, each where two things meet; the rest is left quiet.
  • How it is and how it feels are shown by posture first and the face second, so they can be told from far off. Each is a row of numbers for the same posture, and can be chosen under the picture to see it move: a hungry one sweeps its head, a hurt one limps, a cold one shivers, a tired one nods.
  • Left to wander, it walks the path: it looks about, nibbles at the flowers where the path steps down, drinks at the water, walks the sand and curls up to rest in the quiet corner. The buttons make it do one thing where it stands. Tap it and it startles; tap elsewhere and it looks.
  • Its ears and feelers follow its head a moment late, its paws step in diagonal pairs, and it leaves prints on the sand. Grass bends away from it and springs back.
  • One wind moves everything: a slow rocking that crosses the picture, and every ten seconds or so a gust that leans the grass, the reeds and the tree as it passes, and sometimes carries a petal. Light glints on the water, the pads turn, a fish circles under the surface and now and then rises, a butterfly settles on a daisy, and once a minute the shadow of a cloud goes over. The view drifts a little after the creature, and the leaves overhead slide further than the ground.
  • Everything is drawn in code. It is one small scene, worked on in rounds and judged each round by an independent reviewer. It is not finished.
