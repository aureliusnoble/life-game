What this is

  • A paper diorama on a table, photographed from above in window light. Everything has a height, and its height decides how far and how soft its shadow falls.
  • Each sheet has the tooth of its own paper, a lit edge where it was cut and a shaded one, a tight shadow where it touches and a short one for its height. Nothing has an outline, and nothing is shaded with a gradient.
  • The creature is built like a paper toy: a body over a paler under-sheet, a head that overlaps it, ears and a snout laid on top, and one shadow for the whole of it.
  • The second creature is a test of the same rules with many more parts: plates, spikes, six legs, a long tail, horns and feelers.
  • Left to wander, it walks a route: it looks about, nibbles at the flowers, drinks at the water and curls up to rest. The buttons make it do one thing where it stands. Tap it and it startles; tap elsewhere and it looks.
  • Its ears and feelers follow its head a moment late, its paws step in diagonal pairs, and it leaves prints on the sand. Grass and reeds sway, the pads turn, a fish passes under the water, a butterfly drifts with its shadow under it, and the shade of a tree stirs.
  • Everything is drawn in code. It is one small scene, worked on in rounds and judged each round by an independent reviewer. It is not finished.
