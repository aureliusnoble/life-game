Concept test · playing with friends

Ages of the basin

A test of one question: does a group's world have a history worth talking about? Forty days of a made-up basin, six friends and the species they released. Each band is one species. Its thickness is its living weight, so its area is its score.

What to try

  1. Drag across the bands. The card under them follows the species under your finger.
  2. Pick one year to see it larger. The pale blue stripes are winters.
  3. Tap a name to light up everything that friend released.
  4. Under the card, pick a second species to see what passed between the two.
  5. Tap a row on the board, a title or a first to jump to that species.

What this is testing

  • Whether one shared picture can show who rose, whom they pushed aside and how long each lasted.
  • Whether titles give more than one way to be best. The total-life board and the titles are often led by different species.
  • Whether a design taken from a friend's ended species, marked "after", makes a family tree worth following.

What is real and what is not

  • The history is made up by a small model written for this page: seven foods that follow the seasons, grazers, hunters, scavengers, fading as designed, and players who release a new species when the old one ends or is failing. It is not the game's simulation, and no real group played it.
  • The pale mass on top is the presets. Twelve stand in for the game's twenty, and the one most like a new species steps aside for it.
  • Counts between two species come from the model's flows of food and prey. The game would count real meals and deaths.
  • Three titles are missing because this model has no places or homes: The Wanderer, The Homebuilder, and the map run through time.
  • The layout, the look and the wording are not designs.
