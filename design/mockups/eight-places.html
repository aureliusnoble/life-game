Eight places in paper

A test of one question: can the paper rules make eight places that are each their own and still one world? Every shape is a flat sheet with a cut edge and a shadow from one sun. The same two creatures stand in all eight.

What to try

  • Pick a place, then step through the day. Shadows swing round and lengthen, and night is a blue wash with small glows.
  • Step through the year. The Grassland and the Woodland change most; the Dunes and the Tidal flats hardly at all.
  • Look for the two creatures in each place. Their colours are the same everywhere, and should stand clear of every ground.
  • Things to find: the snow line, the canopy cut away round a creature, the burnt ground, the grazed patch, the ice rim, the reeds laid flat, the footprints filling in, the tide.

What is real and what is not

  • Everything is drawn in code from the paper rules: flat sheets, cut edges, one shadow direction, few colours. It stands in for drawn art, and a person would compose each place far better.
  • Each picture is about one phone screen of a biome that is ten screens across. Landmarks are placed by hand in the code; nothing here is the game's map.
  • Paper grain, the pale lip on cut edges and sheets that slide as the view moves are left out.
  • Only the large picture moves. How fast a phone can draw this has not been measured.
