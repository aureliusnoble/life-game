Four ways the pond could look

One pond and one cast of critters, rendered four ways with real lighting. Each look is a different material: modelled clay, translucent jelly, needle felt, and cut paper. Switch the look and the time of day, then tap a critter for a close-up or tap the ground to call them over.

The four directions

Clay hollow

Modelled clay in a mossy hollow

Matte, hand-pressed bodies under dappled leaf light, with mushrooms in the shade and flowers in the sun. Feels like stop-motion. Parts melt into the body, so any combination looks made on purpose.

Jelly cove

Translucent jelly in a bright cove

Glossy, see-through bodies over pale sand and clear water. Light passes through thin parts, and at night the critters glow. The most toy-like and the most distinctive in motion.

Felt garden

Needle felt on a stitched garden

Fuzzy edges, bead eyes and running stitches, on layered felt with a stitched pond. The warmest and most handmade. Needs the least lighting, so it is the cheapest to draw on a phone.

Paper lagoon

Cut paper in a layered lagoon

Flat painted shapes with white cut edges, and a pond made of stacked sheets stepping down into the deep. Reads like a picture book, and would make the best portraits and share cards.

What changed from the first pass

  • Bodies have plans. Bun, tadpole, pear, bean, noodle and chonk change the outline before any part is added.
  • More parts. Round, pointed and droopy ears, antennae, gills and horns; whip, fan and stub tails; feet, fins and many legs; a head sprout, a shell or a crest; four patterns and four kinds of eye.
  • Real light. Bodies and props are shaded from one light, cast shadows, and change with the time of day.
  • Placement rules. Reeds grow along the shore, flowers in sunny spots, mushrooms in shade, and lily pads only over deeper water.

Everything here is still generated by code: no drawn textures or painted plants. A hand-made set of plants, stones and ground textures, placed by rules like these and lit the same way, is what would take the environments further.
