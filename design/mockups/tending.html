Concept test · pet play

Tending Pip

A test of one question: is the named one pleasant to spend two minutes with? Stroke it with a finger, give it a treat, call it, and talk to it through a few fixed things to say. It answers by how it moves, and with very few words.

Pip the Fourthyoung adult · day 3

What to try

  1. Stroke it. It meets you as a stranger: a shy one flinches and backs off at first, and settles as you keep it gentle.
  2. Give it a treat. A shy one waits until your finger is away. After three it is full and turns the next one down.
  3. Call it. Whether it comes depends on its character and how far it trusts you.
  4. Talk to it. There are five a day. When they are gone it has said what it has to say: it settles down, and still answers a stroke.
  5. Pass the name on. The heir is a young one that has never met you.

What this is testing

  • Whether a creature that shows how it is, with almost no words, is enough to feel like a pet.
  • Whether character read from a design (bold or shy here) makes two creatures feel like different individuals.
  • Whether running out of talks reads as the creature's own doing.

What is not real

  • There is no simulation behind it and no language model. Its answers are stock lines.
  • The creature and its ground are placeholders for the Paper lagoon look, and there is no sound.
  • Grooming and the nudges that reach the whole species are not here.
