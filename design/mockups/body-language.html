Study · look, sound and feel

Body language sheet

A test of one question: can a paper creature show what it is doing, how it is and what it feels, with no words? One creature, 47 ways, drawn as it is seen in the world: from above, turning to face where it is going. Hide the names and see how many you can tell.

What it is doing

Carried by where the body is going, which way it faces and how it moves, so that it still reads from a distance.

How it is

Carried by the body's condition: its shape, its colour, a patch, a shiver.

What it feels

Eight bearings. A player picks one for each mood they make, and the creature wears the bearing of its strongest mood.

What it has noticed

A glance before it acts. The same hunter comes up from behind in both. In one the creature saw it.

What to try

  • "From above" is the world's view, where nearly all of the game is played. "Close up" is the front-on view used when tending the named one and in portraits.
  • Press "Names shown" to hide the names, then tap a card to see what it was.
  • Press "From a distance" to see each one at about the size it has when a whole biome is on screen. Faces are lost, and movement and outline are what is left.
  • Look for pairs that are hard to tell apart. Those are the ones to fix.

What is real and what is not

  • The creature is drawn in code from a few moving pieces: body, feet, ears, eyes, mouth. That is the method proposed for the game. The drawing itself stands in for real art.
  • One body is shown. Ears carry most of the bearing here; another species would use its own signature part.
  • From above, which way a body and its head point carries a great deal: looking back, circling, a glance at a hunter. Leaning, lying down and the mouth carry less than they do close up.
  • Left out: stings, sprays and other acts that belong to a body part.
  • Nothing here has been tried on a person. Whether each reads is for you to judge.
