# Social organisation: building blocks for many kinds of society

Draft 1, November 2026. Design area 3 in `design/plan.md`. A proposal on paper: nothing in it has been run, and balance is left to the end.

## 1. The aim

There is no menu of societies to pick from. There is a small set of blocks, and a pack, a harem, a hive or something with no counterpart in nature is what a player gets by combining them. Two things have to hold:

- **Breadth:** the blocks reach as much of the range of real societies as possible, and beyond it.
- **Reasons:** every way of living together buys something in the world and costs something. Otherwise it is decoration.

Most of the blocks are already in the behaviour vocabulary (`design/neural-blocks.md`). Section 8 lists the few that are new.

## 2. A society is seven answers

| Question | From | To |
|---|---|---|
| Who breeds? | Every adult | One individual |
| Who mates with whom? | Anyone, once | One partner for life |
| Who looks after the young? | No one | Everyone |
| Who stays together? | No one | A swarm of thousands |
| Who belongs? | No groups at all | Closed colonies that fight each other |
| Who does what? | All alike | Castes with their own bodies |
| Who gives way? | No one | A strict order |

Each answer is built from the blocks in section 3, and any combination is allowed. A wolf pack and a beehive differ on most of the seven; many interesting designs will be mixtures that no animal uses.

## 3. The blocks

### 3.1 Knowing who is who

An instinct can only treat others differently if it can tell them apart.

| A creature can tell | How | Status |
|---|---|---|
| My kind from others | Always | Exists |
| Female from male; young, adult and old | The bodies differ | Exists; "old" is new in the life-cycles draft |
| Caste | The bodies differ | Exists, as a Born with mood |
| One particular individual: my mate, my parent, my young, my rival | A Bond mood | Exists, tested |
| Familiar from stranger | The Learn function | Exists, on paper |
| What another is doing or announcing | Its display, call or mark | Exists |
| The biggest, smallest, weakest or lone one | Selectors | Exists |
| Bigger or smaller than me; older or younger than me, among my kind | A comparison | **New** |
| Of my home, or of another home of my kind | Home scent (3.2) | **New** |

### 3.2 Belonging: homes

This is the block that societies with more than one group need.

- **Home scent.** Every creature carries the scent of the home where it hatched. Its marks carry it too. "Of my home" and "not of my home" are things an instinct can notice in its own kind.
- **A home is a Place.** A creature that digs or builds and then lays has founded one. Whatever hatches there belongs to it.
- **Leaving.** Instincts decide who leaves and when: all the young, one sex, only those born as founders, or no one.
- **Joining.** A creature that is tended or fed at a home for long enough takes on its scent. That covers adoption, and it also means stolen eggs hatch as loyal members of the home that stole them.
- **Many homes.** A species can treat its other homes as friends, as strangers to avoid, or as enemies. That is one instinct, and it decides a great deal (section 7).

### 3.3 Breeding rules

Set for the species in the life-cycle settings (`design/life-cycles.md`), and shaped by instincts.

| Block | What it does | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Way of breeding | Alone, in pairs, or one breeder with helpers | Decided: chosen per species |
| Brood mix | What share of eggs become each sex and each caste, with up to two rules such as "more founders in autumn" or "more soldiers after an attack" | **New** |
| Who may lay | An instinct with conditions. "Lay, not when I smell the Queen mark" keeps everyone but the queen from laying, for as long as she lives | Exists |
| Mating | The mate act, which needs both to want it. What makes one willing is its own instincts: a display, a gift of food, a held territory, a Bond | Exists |
| Keeps what it was given | Mates once and lays for life | **New** |
| What breeding costs the parent | Nothing; the mother dies after laying; the father dies after mating; the father is eaten | **New** as settings; the last is an instinct |
| Young forms | A young body that differs from the adult's | Decided: in the first version |

### 3.4 Roles

Five ways for members of one species to do different jobs. They can be mixed.

| Way | How | Example |
|---|---|---|
| By birth | A Born with mood, with body parts tied to it | Workers, soldiers, a queen |
| By age | "Only when young", "only when old" | Young adults nurse, older ones forage, elders guard |
| By sex | "Only when female" | Males guard while females feed |
| By signal | A creature takes a job by showing it, and others hold off while they can see it shown | One sentinel at a time; one leader at a time |
| By need | A Count, or "few of my home near me showing X" | More foragers when the larder is low |

One addition makes roles that are taken up later in life possible:

- **For life (new mood function).** Once on, the mood never fades, and it can carry body parts that grow in over a few days. A worker can become a queen when the old one dies. The biggest of a group can change sex. A home under attack can turn some of its young into soldiers. Founders can grow fins or wide feet to cross to new ground.

### 3.5 Giving and sharing

All existing: grab and drop, give, tend, dig and build, and a Place to bring things back to.

- **Feeding others:** the young, the breeder, the guards. This is what lets some members never forage.
- **Passing food along:** "give" shares food already eaten, so a forager can come home and feed several.
- **Stores:** a larder at the home for winter.
- **Carrying the young or the eggs:** by either parent, or by anyone.

### 3.6 Acting together

All existing: calls, marks and displays; Catching moods; Count; "go along" and "go the same way".

- **Recruiting:** a call or a trail that brings others to food, or to a fight.
- **Alarm:** one sees, all flee.
- **Quorum:** "only when many of my home are near" before attacking, moving home or spawning.
- **Following a leader:** go the same way as the oldest, or as the one showing a Lead display.
- **Tradition:** the young follow elders to a place and remember it. Routes, water holes and nesting grounds then pass down the generations for as long as the generations overlap. This is how a species comes to "know" things its designer never told it.

### 3.7 Rank and rivalry

Built from displays and one mood; nothing new.

- **Contests:** two rivals show a threat. The smaller or older-weaker one's Beaten mood rises, and while Beaten it keeps apart and does not mate or lay.
- **Rank by size or by age:** using the new comparisons, "give way to my kind bigger than me" at food.
- **Taking over:** a challenger that beats the breeder drives it out.
- **Policing:** "eat an egg that does not smell of the Queen".
- **Driving out:** the young are chased off when they come of age.

### 3.8 Space

All existing: Place, marks, dig, build.

- **Territory:** mark the edges; chase strangers of my kind inside them.
- **Nest, burrow, hive:** dug or built, and defended.
- **Range without borders:** stay near a Place, tolerate neighbours.
- **No home:** nomads that camp where they stop.
- **Several homes:** a main nest and outposts.

## 4. Why live together: what it buys and what it costs

Each row needs its mechanism to exist in the world, or the society built on it is filler.

| What a group buys | Through | In the world today? |
|---|---|---|
| Earlier warning | Any member's alarm reaches all | Yes |
| Safety in numbers | A hunter takes one at a time, and is torn between targets | Yes |
| Fighting back | Many small bites hurt a hunter that one could not | Yes, and too strong |
| Bigger prey | Several biters; clingers that slow the prey | Partly: needs a rule for wounding prey larger than yourself |
| Finding food sooner | Recruiting calls and trails | Yes |
| Specialised bodies | A guard and a forager, each built for one job, instead of two all-rounders | Needs the caste budget rule (section 9) |
| More young surviving | Tending speeds hatching and growth; guards keep thieves off | Yes |
| Getting through winter | A shared larder; huddling for warmth | Larder yes; warmth is proposed |
| Knowing the land | Tradition | On paper |

| What a group costs | Through |
|---|---|
| Food runs out near home | Members travel farther for every meal |
| Easy to find | A crowd is loud, visible and leaves scent |
| One point of failure | A species with one breeder can lose it |
| Rivals inside | Contests, stolen matings, idle members |
| Brain budget | Every social instinct is one not spent on something else |

## 6. Mothers, fathers and others

The strategies you listed, and their neighbours, with what each trades.

| Strategy | How it is built | The trade |
|---|---|---|
| The mother dies when she lays | Setting; one huge brood | Everything goes into the eggs; nothing is left to guard them |
| The father dies after mating | Setting, with a cheap, short-lived male body | Males cost almost nothing to make |
| The father is eaten | "After mating, bite my mate" | The mother's next brood is paid for by him |
| Many males to one female | Brood mix heavy on males; she lays for each | Plenty of carers and guards; many mouths that lay nothing |
| One male to many females | Contests between males; brood mix or driving out handles the spare males | The best male fathers most; most males never breed |
| One queen at a time | Queen mark and For life | Very efficient; one death can end it |
| Several queens | Tolerated marks | Safer, and each queen gets less |
| The father carries the eggs | Grab and carry, only when male | The mother is free to feed and lay again |
| Both tend | Bond, then take turns by signal | High survival; two adults tied to one nest |
| Helpers | The young stay | A bigger family; fewer new families |
| A nursery | A few tend everyone's young | The rest are freed to feed; one raid takes the lot |
| Someone else raises them | Lay in another's nest | No cost of care; depends on a host being there |

## 7. Many colonies

Once a species has more than one home, how its homes treat each other is a design choice with large effects.

| Stance toward other homes of my kind | What follows |
|---|---|
| Enemies | Homes space themselves out and each guards its food, which limits overgrazing. Lives are spent on border fights |
| Avoid | Spacing without the fighting, and no help in a crisis |
| Friends | Homes sit close and share. The species can become very dense in one place, and can eat it bare |
| Mother and daughters | Friendly to homes founded from mine, hostile to the rest: needs a scent that founders carry with them |
| Raid | Other homes are a source of eggs and stores |

How new homes start is the other choice: a lone founder, a founder with a retinue, the old breeder leaving with half the home, or a fight that splits it.

## 8. What is new in this draft

Six additions. Everything else above uses words the vocabulary already has.

| Addition | Kind | What it unlocks |
|---|---|---|
| Home scent: "of my home" | A thing to notice | Every society with more than one group |
| For life | A mood function, able to carry body parts | Queens that are made, sex change, soldiers on demand, founders with their own bodies |
| Brood mix, with up to two rules | A species setting | Castes in set shares, skewed sex ratios, raising what the home needs |
| Keeps what it was given | A species setting | Mate once and lay for life |
| What breeding costs the parent | A species setting | One brood and die; short-lived males |
| Older or younger, bigger or smaller than me, among my kind | Comparisons | Rank by age or size; elders that lead |

## 9. Budget, and keeping it learnable

- **How castes are paid for is open.** A proposal: each caste's body has to fit the body allowance on its own, the brain is shared by all, and each caste after the first costs a fixed amount. Specialising then pays only if the specialists are really better at their jobs.
- **Societies are advanced play.** A first species needs none of this. The blocks suit the idea of unlock kits in the plan: a pair kit, a herd kit, a colony kit.
- **Templates.** A few working societies to open up and change will teach more than any description.

## 10. What the player sees

- **Homes on the map,** each with its numbers.
- **Who does what:** counts by role, and who is breeding.
- **The named line in a colony** is the breeder's line.
- **Events:** a new home founded, a queen replaced, a border fight, a raid.

## 11. To check in the stand-in, for direction only

1. A sentinel by signal: does the group eat more and lose fewer?
2. A queen mark that stops others laying: does it keep a home from eating its ground bare?
3. A guard caste and a forager caste against the same number of all-rounders.
4. Homes that are enemies against homes that are friends: spacing, and what happens to the food.
5. A pack against prey no lone hunter can take.

## 12. Questions for you

1. **Unkind behaviour.** The blocks allow killing a rival's young, eating a mate, stealing eggs and raiding one's own kind. They follow from the same pieces as everything else. Allow them all, or rule some out for a game this cute?
2. **Castes and the budget.** Is the proposal in section 9 the right starting point?
3. **Is anything missing** from the range in sections 5 and 6 that you want to be possible?
