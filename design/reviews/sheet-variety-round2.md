# Species sheet, second review of variety and appeal

16 November 2026. A second independent reviewer (a fresh agent on Fable 5.2) judged a new hundred species made by chance, after the changes that followed the first review. Some of this hundred spend the whole budget and some very little.

The verdict in the reviewer's words: the flexibility to go far beyond the presets is there, but the parts are not yet expressed clearly enough. The second half of that is the art pass's work, and the list below says what it has to do.

## Where this leaves the requirement

Both halves of the owner's requirement were tested twice. "Wide enough to go far beyond the presets": the second reviewer counts about eighteen body ideas against the presets' twelve, and 43 of 100 chance bodies leave the preset outlines. "Clear enough": every part can be told from its drawing in a quiz (60 of 62), but in a crowd of parts on one small body many still cannot, and a third of chance creatures are appealing where all the presets are. The systems are in place; the finish is not, and it is listed above and in `design/look.md` section 21 for the art pass.

## The reviewer's report

## Ten-line summary

1. Verdict: half met. The sheet has the range. It does not yet have the clarity.
2. At phone size, in colour, I would confuse only about a dozen pairs. Colour does much of that work.
3. I count about 18 body ideas in the hundred. The presets already hold about 12 of them.
4. 17 cells show a body no preset has. 26 more bend a preset build into a new outline. 57 are preset builds in new dress.
5. New bodies and charm rarely meet: of the 17 new bodies, 2 are appealing, 9 are flawed and 6 are bad.
6. Appeal: 34 appealing, 47 acceptable with a flaw, 19 ugly, cluttered or confusing. All 37 presets are appealing.
7. At a centimetre I could pick out 30 alone, 26 with care, 6 only as a group, and 38 not at all.
8. The face is the main casualty: covered, dark on dark, or too small in about 35 cells. Eye-spots pass for eyes in 5.
9. About 18 kinds of part cannot be named from the picture. A cream ball floats before the face in 7 cells. Nothing is cut by the frame.
10. Scores: variety 7, appeal 5, craft 6. The biggest gains are one large paired side-shape, a two-colour coat, and rules that guard the face and the heading.

I judged from the three pictures only, with enlarged crops and a few pixel measurements.

## 5. Rules the designer should enforce

1. **Guard the face.** Nothing layers over the eye zone, and eyes are drawn last. Broken in 3, 13, 31, 45, 49, 54, 61, 66, 77, 86, 90, 98.
2. **Give eyes contrast and a least size.** Add a light rim or a lighter face on dark bodies. Broken in 2, 69, 81, 100, 51, 73, 87, 30, 55, 78.
3. **Keep eye-spots below the eyes.** Make them smaller and duller, with no pupil, and keep them off the rear third. Broken in 8, 43, 48, 65, 84.
4. **Scale the part count to the body.** Small bodies show fewer kinds, drawn larger. Broken in 11, 16, 28, 38, 54, 55, 65, 73, 78, 87.
5. **Hold one colour scheme.** Use body, accent and pale; parts take from it. Broken in 16, 28, 49, 54, 73, 87.
6. **Mark the front.**
   - Draw the arrow tail and the dart mouth differently.
   - Give stars and tripods a front arm or eyes on the leading edge (4, 12, 46, 53, 67, 70, 75, 83).
   - Push the head clear of a body that is wider than long (23, 25, 59, 81, 98).
7. **Give rim parts slots.** Ears go on the head only, and round things are limited to two kinds per creature. Broken in 9, 12, 25, 33, 44, 58, 62, 67, 84, 96, 99.
8. **Join everything.** Fix the nose ball (11, 17, 23, 50, 65, 77, 89) and the arcs (50, 70, 72, 73, 87).
9. **Keep the head a fair share of the body and a tone lighter**, unless the player chose a tiny head. Broken in 5, 7, 14, 30, 38, 100.
10. **Tone the cargo and patches.**
    - No dung-brown lumps on pink, lilac or blue (41, 68, 93).
    - No huge cream oval on a dark body (17, 74).
11. **Light the edges of dark plates and legs** so they separate (2, 69, 74, 81, 100).
12. **Pose limbs with the walk**, never straight out, and always on both sides (6, 23, 59, 77, 99). Cargo may not hide one flank (36).
13. **Clamp one part against the body**, or grow the body to carry it: fan 17, fluke 45, club 98, frill 3.

## 6. What a player still could not make

- A snake, eel or worm. No body is longer than about 3.5 to 1.
- A long neck.
- A butterfly, moth, bat, bird or ray. Wings never pass one body length across.
- A crab, lobster or scorpion with real pincers.
- An octopus or jellyfish with trailing arms.
- A snail with an offset shell.
- A rabbit, bat or elephant by its ears.
- A frog or hare with large hind legs. Leg pairs do not differ.
- Anything shaggy or woolly. Fur is only a rim.
- Anything known by its coat: panda, skunk, orca, bee, ladybird, tabby, cow.
- Anything lopsided.
- A head that is not round or egg-shaped.

The two additions that would widen the range most:

1. **One large paired side-shape.** A row of outlines (butterfly, bat, bird, ray, flap, long ear) with a size that can pass the body's width. It opens a whole family of outlines, and they survive at a centimetre.
2. **A two-colour coat.** A second body colour with a layout row: mask, saddle, spine stripe, front and back halves, blotches, bands. It makes species tell apart at a glance. It costs no clutter.

A third would be a longer body with front and hind legs sized apart.

## 7. Scores

- **Variety, 7.** The range of parts, colours and proportions is wide and 43 cells leave the preset outlines, but 57 are presets in new dress and half merge at a centimetre.
- **Appeal, 5.** A third are charming, a fifth are bad, and the new bodies are the least charming.
- **Craft, 6.** The paper, layering and shadows are handsome and nothing is cut, but faces get buried, a ball floats in seven cells, and about 18 kinds of part cannot be named.

**Verdict: half met.** The flexibility to go far beyond the presets is there, but the parts are not yet expressed clearly enough.
