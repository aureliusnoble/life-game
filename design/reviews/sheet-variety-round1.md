# Species sheet, first review of variety and appeal

16 November 2026. An independent reviewer (a fresh agent on Fable 5.2) was shown a hundred species made by chance from the sheet, the same hundred as outlines a centimetre long, and the 37 presets. It was asked whether the choices are clear enough and wide enough to make creatures far beyond the presets. Scores: variety 6, appeal 4, craft 5. Verdict: half met.

## The reviewer's summary

1. Verdict: the requirement is half met. The choices are flexible enough but not expressed clearly enough. Scores: variety 6, appeal 4, craft 5.
2. Variety: in colour nearly any two can be told apart, but colour does the work. Near-twins are 40/66, 37/83 and 26/48, with eight looser pairs.
3. I count eleven body ideas. 43 of 100 are still the presets' default body, and about 20 show a body no preset has (spiders and crabs, a legged dome, arms, slabs, heart and teardrop, sail backs).
4. At a centimetre there are about 13 outline types and about 33 outlines I could pick out again; 49 differ only in size.
5. What survives at that size: long legs, star arms, arrow and ball tails, prongs at the front, the bend. What vanishes: the neck, paws, ears, spikes and everything on the back.
6. Appeal: 18 appealing (18, 30, 32, 39, 52, 59, 60, 62, 64, 65, 76, 77, 82, 83, 87, 90, 93, 100), 44 acceptable, 38 cluttered or faceless (worst: 53, 17, 8, 69, 54).
7. Five causes cover nearly all the bad ones: too much on the back; a full parts list on a small body; a covered or hidden face; eyespots that out-face the real eyes; papers all pale, all saturated or matching their ground.
8. Faults: detached hard shadows under spikes (15 cells), drawings touching or cut by the frame (13, 52, 23, 24, 92), heads hidden (58, 84, 68, 8, 69, 95), and a dark blob behind the rear that reads as a dropping (a dozen cells).
9. Rules: three layers on the back with one thing on top that stops at the neck; nothing over the face; only real eyes get a highlight; detail follows drawn size; paper roles with tone steps and blocked pairings.
10. Missing: necks, waisted bodies, wing-led animals, long tails, big claws, soft fur, coils. The one added choice I would make is a free "waist": how the body divides and how the head joins.

## The report

## Verdict

The requirement is half met. The choices are flexible enough: the hundred show bodies, limbs and heads that no preset has. They are not yet expressed clearly enough: 38 of the hundred are cluttered, faceless or confusing, and at a centimetre about half the sheet collapses into three plain outlines. The presets look designed because each shows three or four ideas. The random ones show eight to ten at equal weight, and nothing in the drawing decides which idea wins.

Words used below:

- The **bean** is the presets' default body, an oval with a round head at one end.
- **Shards** are the pale blue-white pointed pieces.
- **Discs** are the plain paired circles beside the neck.
- **Things on the back** are the leaf, reeds or sprigs, rock, mud and sand patch. I cannot tell from the pictures which are skins and which are loads.

## 5. Rules the designer should keep for the player

None of these removes a choice. The part stays owned and counted; the drawing places it, shrinks it or re-tones it.

| Area | Rule | Seen in |
|---|---|---|
| Back | At most three layers: the skin (pattern or plates), one marking (eyespots or saddle), one thing on top (leaf, reeds, rock, mud or sand). About sixty of the hundred carry something on the back and many carry two or three | 4, 17, 20, 73, 97 |
| Back | Order of yielding: a thing on top may hide pattern but never eyespots. With eyespots present it shrinks and sits behind them, or they move to the flanks. Plates and shells take the pattern onto themselves | 4, 20 |
| Back | A thing on top covers no more than about 40% of the back, stops at the neck, overhangs the outline by no more than a fifth of the body's length, sits on the spine and bends with it | 13, 5, 8, 54, 69, 75 |
| Back | Reeds travel as one bundle of up to three, along the spine, not scattered at angles | 4, 6, 20, 96, 97 |
| Face | Nothing lies over the head except the head's own parts | 22, 43, 63, 92 |
| Face | Teeth and tusks start at the sides of the mouth and point outward or forward, never over the eyes | 20, 38, 42 |
| Face | Two kinds of thing may project forward of the head (from horns, tusks, fangs, pincers, nose stick, antennae). A third and later shrink to about 60% or fold back | 20, 31, 69, 86 |
| Face | A face mark is smaller than one eye and sits behind the eyes | 3, 25, 49, 78 |
| Face | Every eye style keeps a minimum dark mass, about a fifth of the head's width each. On a dark head the eye gains a pale ring automatically | 47, 51, 77, 89 |
| Face | Only real eyes have a highlight. Eyespots are flat rings in the pattern paper, no pupil, no white | 4, 9, 16, 17, 24, 77 |

## 6. What is missing

Kinds a player still could not make:

- **Anything with a neck**: goose, heron, a turtle with its head out. Every head sits pressed against the body.
- **Waisted or lobed bodies**: ant, wasp, peanut, snowman. Every body is one convex mass.
- **Wing-led animals**: butterfly, bat, bird, dragonfly. Wings and fins stay inside or just outside the body's width; 57 is the furthest any goes.
- **Long tails**: lizard, rat, a scorpion's curl, a peacock's fan. The longest tail (30, 76, 82) is about a third of the body.
- **Big claws and lopsided animals**: a crab's pincers, a fiddler crab. Arms end in small hands (56, 68, 71), and everything is symmetrical.
- **Soft animals**: sheep, chick, bumblebee. Fur shows only as a rim (71, 89), never as the body's surface.
- **Coiled or trailing forms**: a snail's spiral, a coiled snake, a jellyfish's tentacles.

**The single added choice of looks that would widen the range most is a waist.** It is a free choice of how the body is divided and how the head joins it: one piece (as now), on a neck, two lobes, three lobes. Length and area stay the same, so play is unchanged.

- It is the only kind of choice that changes the outline at every size.
- It multiplies with every body shape and limb already there.
- It goes straight at the finding in section 2: 49 outlines differ only in size because every body is one mass with the head pressed on.

Longer tails and wider wings are the runners-up, and both are new entries in tables that already exist.

## How this was judged

Only the three pictures were used. I viewed them whole and as enlarged crops of the same pixels, and ran two small measurements on them: how close each drawing comes to its frame, and how much each cut-out overlaps every other when centred. The overlap figure is crude (no rotation or scaling) and is offered as a check on the eye, not as the count itself. Names of parts are my guesses from the drawing.
