# Second review: how the creature moves

15 November 2026. An independent reviewer, shown film strips of each act, of states and moods in motion, of two other bodies walking the route, and of a gust, and allowed to read the motion code. It re-ran the motion's arithmetic to measure what stills cannot show. A unit is 1/360 of the screen's width, a frame 1/60 of a second, and the creature is about 100 units long.

## Still wrong, worst first

1. **Paws skate.** Yaw, turns, the cold shiver and the stop all move the whole cut-out, paws included. On a 100° corner a planted toe drifts 22 units on average and 58 at worst. Fix: store each paw's place in the world when it touches down, and draw it from its socket to that place through the stance; step early once the socket is 1.25 rest lengths away. Yaw, shiver and push act on body, head and tail only.
2. **Other bodies.** The stilt's legs are waves at hip and knee, so no foot is ever still; the hind foot sweeps 24 units sideways. Solve two-bone legs to planted feet, in alternating tripods, 65% stance. The long one's spine has no memory: it is ruler-straight at every stop and pivots about its middle. Each round must follow the one ahead at a fixed distance.
3. **Nothing is eaten.** At the bite, pull the flower 5 units toward the mouth and take a petal off for good. Chew with the whole head: 1.2 units along the neck, cheeks 1.5 units past the outline in turn, 8 frames each. Drink: turn each new ring by 40°; they stack in three spokes.
4. **Ways out pop.** Sleep ends with eyes open in one frame and a walk half curled. Give waking 50 frames: an 8% breath, lids half open for 6 frames, the head up past its mark, a stretch to 1.12 of its length held 14 frames. Cross-fade states over 24 frames. Put ear angle and perk on the ear springs. Startle: begin the hop at 0.95 of its size; ease its length over 8 frames.
5. **States share a motion.** Tired, weary and gloomy are one slow walk. Weary: hind paws drag. Gloomy: head still, a sigh of 8% every 6 seconds. Hurt: a 4° lean off the held paw. Cold shivers too fast: a 6-frame period, 1.2 units, body only. Playful hops while its paws walk: all four must leave and land together. A wary halt keeps 48% of its speed after 10 frames: stop in 5.
6. **Eyes and startle.** Shift the pupil, show white, and lead the head by 7 frames. The startle travels 8 units: make it 35; land at 0.94 of its size for 3 frames with ears flopping 20°; the grass flattens and the butterfly bolts.
7. **Weight and clocks.** Bob 4%, with the shadow moving 2 units. Lead the head's target by 8 frames so it holds within 2°. Everything rocks every 240 frames: give grass 150, flowers 190, reeds 260 and leaves 330. A gust comes in over 25 frames and goes out over 110 with one overshoot, moving ears and tail too.

## What only a person watching can judge

- Whether 4 to 5 units of paw slip shows at phone size, and how bad corners look.
- Whether 1.7 steps a second under a body 100 units long reads as walking.
- The springs: lively or wobbly, the head above all.
- Whether a quarter larger reads as height or as swelling.
- "Hold poses, twelve a second": charm or stutter.

## Rules worth keeping

1. A planted paw is fixed in the world. Nothing that moves the body may move it.
2. No switches: every value passes through a key or a spring, on the way out of an act as well as in.
3. An act leaves the world changed: a petal gone, grass flat, a mark in the sand.
4. Each state and mood owns one motion no other has.
5. Nothing repeats to the frame, and no two kinds of thing share a clock.
