# Review: assets, and a style guide for future ones

15 November 2026. An independent reviewer, shown close-ups of every asset in the pond scene and the range tests of other places. `u` is 1/360 of the screen's width. Shadows fall to the lower right. The colours are the reviewer's proposals, not samples from the study.

## Four faults that recur

- **Three kinds of shadow:** hard (stones), translucent copies that darken twice where they overlap (grass, reeds), and blurred (leaves, shade).
- **Side views in a world seen from above:** grass fans, reed sprays, the log's end cap.
- **One cream value** shared by stones, toadstools, daisies and sand, so they merge.
- **Too small:** flowers and pebbles are about 6u.

## Scores (5 is a competent prototype)

| Asset | Score | Why |
|---|---|---|

| Overhead leaves | 7 | Crease and two-tone halves work; the serration reads as noise |
| Daisies | 6.5 | Good tiers of petals; a glossy bead for a centre; reads as a water lily |

| Sand and foam | 6 | The wet band is good; the "torn" foam is a smooth line |

## Redesigns

- **Toadstools:** three pieces. A scalloped cream skirt showing 1u; a cap split in two halves across the light (lit #C8553D, far #A84633); five to seven punched cream dots of 1.5 to 2u. Diameters 10, 14 and 18u; cast 2.5u. Always three, touching a log or stump.
- **Stump:** four pieces. A root flare of five or seven lobes, unequal in length and spacing (#6B4A35, cast 2.5u); an irregular eleven-sided ring of bark, radius 22u (#7D573D, cast 9u); a pale cut face, radius 19u (#D9B98A), with four scored rings set 3u off centre; one wedge-shaped crack.
- **Dune ripples:** on each dune sheet, five to nine scored lines across the wind, 40 to 120u long, 6 to 9u apart, wobbling by 2u, with breaks and the odd fork. A score is 0.5u of #D9C08A beside 0.5u of #FBF0CF. On the windward 70% only; the lee strip blank and one step darker.
- **Log:** a tapered strip 52u long, 13u narrowing to 11u, in three flat bands along its length (#96693F, #7A5236, #5F3F2B). Zigzag-cut ends showing a 2u pale core (#D8B88A). One branch stub, two or three slits of bark. Cast 5u.
- **Driftwood:** the log in #E4DDCC, #CFC6B4, #B8AE9A, with a forked end, and a piece of sand overlapping its lower third.
- **Grass:** a full rosette. A lower ring of five blades (12 to 14u, #74A85C) and an upper ring of four (8 to 10u, #8FBF6E) turned 36°; each a creased blade 2.5u wide. Opaque, one hard 2.5u shadow. 18 to 26u across, three variants. The upper ring sways ±3° over 3 s.
- **Leaf litter:** three shapes of 6 to 9u (a creased oval, a lobed oak leaf, a folded half-leaf) in #C9773A, #A85A2E, #D9A441, #7A5A3A. Seventy per cent in overlapping drifts of five to nine on the far side of trunks, logs and stones. Cast 1u.
- **Reeds:** opaque; one merged hard shadow the plant's own size; heads set 70% of the way along a stem.
- **Stones:** two or three different irregular sheets in cool grey (#A9ADA6, #BFC2BA, #D6D8D0), upper sheets shifted toward the light.
- **Butterfly:** two hinged wings (lit #F7D774, far #E9B93F), 14u across, its shadow the same outline.

## The style guide

1. **A piece** is one closed flat cut of one paper, nothing narrower than 2u.
2. **Count:** two to five pieces for a prop, up to nine for a landmark.
3. **Plan view only.** Never draw a side or an end cap. The length of its shadow says how tall a thing is.
4. **Cuts:** plants in arcs, mineral in six to nine unequal straight cuts. No perfect circles or regular polygons. Torn edges only for foam, snow, moss and mud.
5. **Folds and scores:** one fold to a piece, the two halves 6% lighter and darker. A score is a dark hairline beside a light one, at most five to a piece.
6. **Height, and the shadow it throws:**

   | Level | What | Shadow |
   |---|---|---|
   | H0 | A score | None |
   | H1 | A lying sheet: litter, pads | 1u |
   | H2 | Low: flowers, caps, grass, pebbles | 2.5u |
   | H3 | A body: stones, logs, creatures | 5u |
   | H4 | Tall: reeds, stumps, boulders | 9u |
   | H5 | Overhead or flying | 16 to 24u, detached |

   A recess is shaded inside the lip on the lit side, 3u for each step down.
7. **Shadows:** one direction, hard-edged, one tone (the ground 22% darker). Merge a layer's shadows into one shape before darkening. Blur only at H5.
8. **Edges:** a lit edge 12% lighter, 0.6u wide, on light-facing edges only; a shaded edge 15% darker, 0.8u, opposite. Never white. Worked out again after a piece is turned.
9. **Stock:** card for ground and stone; flecked kraft for sand, wood and mud; smooth tissue for petals, wings and foam. Grain at 4% contrast at most.
10. **Colour:** three papers and one accent, the accent no more than a tenth of the area. Layers step 6 to 8% lighter upward; water 10% darker downward. An asset differs from its ground by at least 15% in lightness. Only creatures, flowers and caps are saturated.
11. **Size:** no asset under 10u, no detail under 2u; landmarks 40 to 70u.
12. **Variants:** three outlines each, not three sizes; turned in steps of 15°.
13. **Placement:** odd clusters of three to five, one large with small ones trailing. Most props within 20u of an edge. Empty rests at least 80u across.
14. **Overlap:** straddle boundaries: a stone across the foam, a pad across a depth line. Creatures are H3: they walk over H0 to H2, are stopped by H3, and pass under H4 and H5.
15. **Motion:** thin pieces only, hinged at a fold or a base, ±3 to 5°, over 2 to 4 s, out of phase. Nothing fades or blurs.

## Do and don't

- Draw in plan. Don't show sides or end caps.
- Use one hard merged shadow. Don't use ghost copies.
- Take shadow length from the height table. Don't judge it by eye.
- Make the outline name the thing at 12u. Don't rescue it with detail.
- Break symmetry. Don't use regular polygons or concentric copies.
- Separate an asset from its ground by 15% in lightness. Don't put cream on cream.
- Light only the light-facing edge. Don't rim the whole piece.
- Cluster against edges and leave rests. Don't sprinkle evenly.
- Keep tones flat. Don't add sheen, photographic grain or bead highlights.
- Draw natural surfaces with hand-placed scores. Don't tile a pattern.

## A recipe for a new asset

1. Find a reference seen from directly above, and describe the thing in one sentence.
2. Name the one feature that identifies it at 12u. For a toadstool, the spotted cap.
3. Cut the outline as one piece and test it as a solid shape at phone size. If it cannot be named, cut it again.
4. Give each part a height, three levels at most.
5. Choose three papers against its ground, and one accent.
6. Stack one to three pieces, lighter upward; add one fold and up to five scores.
7. Apply edges and shadow by rules 6 to 8.
8. Make three outlines; write its rule of placement and its motion, or none.
9. Check it beside the creature in the scene.

Example, a rock pool: a recess cut into a sheet of stone, two steps deep, a torn pale rim, one limpet disc at H2.

## Redo first

Fix the shadow pass (rule 7) before anything. Then grass, toadstools, the trunk, dune ripples, and the log and driftwood as one build. Stones and leaf litter after.
