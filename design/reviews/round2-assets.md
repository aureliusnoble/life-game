It was told the owner's remark: assets for biomes outside the lake are not nearly as extensive, the lake needs more beyond one screen, and general ones are needed. ## The family

- **Shadow:** one language now.
- **View:** nearly one. Reeds and the lopsided grass are sprays pressed flat; the marsh's reeds are an older side-view fan.
- **Standing off the ground:** flowers, toadstools, stones and pads do. Driftwood does not. The lit blades of grass are 3 points of lightness off the meadow.
- **Too small:** leaves at 7 units; scree and duckweed at 3.
- **The real fault:** every asset is a garnish of 12 to 55 units. Nothing can hide a creature, feed a herd or fill ten screens. The "reed stand" is two clumps.

## The guide: could a newcomer make a good asset from it alone?

No. It gives a family likeness, not a good asset; the rock pool's 6 is the evidence. Tested on three assets it had never seen, it left these to guess:

- **A nest with eggs.** The cup is a recess, and the rule for cutting down never reached the guide. Twigs are thinner than 2 units. Eggs are domes: how, with one fold? May eggs be saturated? Is "nothing under 10 units" the asset or each piece?
- **A burrow.** How dark? A hole in the face of a bank cannot be seen from above. The rule for what creatures do has no "goes into". Sized to which body?
- **Snowy grass.** An asset or a state? The winter ground's colour is not given, and white on white breaks the 15% rule. How does snow arrive when nothing fades?

To add:

1. Numbers: the light's direction, the shadow's tone, the steps for edges and folds.
2. Recesses: the shade inside the lip, the steps, the darkest paper.
3. A palette: each ground by biome and season; papers for wood, stone, earth, bone and snow.
4. States: eaten, burnt, flooded, frozen, snowed on, occupied. The same outline with pieces swapped.
5. Masses and cover: stands and swards, and how a body inside one shows.
6. Lines and traces, which age by losing pieces.
7. Domes, and things thinner than 2 units.
8. Food may carry the accent.
9. "Solid" as its own property apart from height: the guide sends creatures under stumps. "Four heights" lists five.
10. What an asset drops at the far distance.
11. Code: an asset's data, the helpers, and one full worked example.

## Still wrong with the twelve, worst first

1. **Driftwood.** Delete the oval. Run a torn edge of the ground sheet over the wood's lower third. Grey 20% darker than the sand; a forked outline.
2. **Log.** One kink at a knot, a taper from 15 units to 9, notches in the bark, one end rotted hollow, two bands.
3. **Fallen leaves.** 10 to 12 units each, one score, five to nine overlapping, touching what they bank against.
4. **Grass.** Blades 8 to 12 units by 3, bent at two thirds, nine to twelve all round.
5. **Stump.** Five broad roots running under the ground sheet; three open, wobbling arcs for rings.
6. **Reeds.** Heads 3 units by 10, at 70% of the stem; five to seven stems splayed by under 30°. Replace the marsh's fans.
7. **Rock pool.** A grey-green rim that is truly torn; water cut in straight cuts like its stone; the slab at 2.5 units with sand lapping it.
8. **Butterfly.** Fore and hind wings cut apart, a band at the edge, a body 2 units wide.
9. **Lily pads.** The light edge to the upper left.
10. **Stones.** A second recipe: one sheet with one fold. Build the crag from ridged folds, not tiers.
