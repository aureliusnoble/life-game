## 1. Evidence on tutorials: Andersen et al. (CHI 2012), later studies, Hodent, NN/g

### Takeaway
The best controlled evidence says tutorials pay off in proportion to how complex and undiscoverable the game is: they lifted play in the complex game (Foldit) and did nothing measurable in two simpler ones. Up-front, out-of-context instruction is the weakest form; in-context, interactive teaching at the moment of need is the best supported, including in the one large study of a game-making tool.

### Cited Findings
- Andersen et al., "The impact of tutorials on games of varying complexity" (CHI 2012, pp. 59–68): eight tutorial designs tested across three games of differing complexity (Refraction, Hello Worlds, Foldit) in a multivariate study of over 45,000 players. — [UW project page](https://grail.cs.washington.edu/projects/game-abtesting/chi2012/); [paper PDF](https://grail.cs.washington.edu/projects/game-abtesting/chi2012/chi2012.pdf)
- Headline result, quoted from the abstract: "Although tutorials increased play time by as much as 29% in the most complex game, they did not significantly improve player engagement in the two simpler games." In Foldit, player progress rose by as much as 75%. — [paper PDF](https://grail.cs.washington.edu/projects/game-abtesting/chi2012/chi2012.pdf)
- The authors' stated conclusion: investment in tutorials may not be justified for games whose mechanics can be discovered through experimentation. — [UW project page](https://grail.cs.washington.edu/projects/game-abtesting/chi2012/)
- Variables tested: tutorial presence; context-sensitivity (information shown in context just before the player needs a concept, versus out of context in an up-front manual); tutorial freedom (how far the tutorial forces the player to perform the mechanic; the context-sensitive condition could block progress until the player did the action); and on-demand help. — [paper PDF](https://grail.cs.washington.edu/projects/game-abtesting/chi2012/chi2012.pdf)
- On-demand help was not a safe default: it improved engagement in Foldit, had no effect in Hello Worlds, and had negative effects in Refraction. — [paper PDF](https://grail.cs.washington.edu/projects/game-abtesting/chi2012/chi2012.pdf)
- The authors note that requiring players to practise specific mechanics could improve effectiveness but could also frustrate them. — [paper PDF](https://grail.cs.washington.edu/projects/game-abtesting/chi2012/chi2012.pdf)
- Later lab study (Passalacqua et al., 2020, Multimodal Technologies and Interaction 4(3):41, "Demystifying the first-time experience of mobile games"): 40 players, mobile context; the presence of a tutorial had a positive effect on non-expert players' perceived flow and no effect on expert players' flow. A lab experiment, not a retention test. — [MDPI](https://mdpi.com/2414-4088/4/3/41); [author PDF](https://publications.polymtl.ca/9418/1/2020_Passalacqua_Demystifying_First-Time_Experience_Mobile_Games.pdf)
- Dominic Kao, "Exploring Help Facilities in Game-Making Software" (FDG 2020): 1,646 participants each built a first-person-shooter level under one of six help conditions (text, interactive, intelligent agent, video, all help, no help). "Interactive Help has a greater positive impact on time spent, controls learnability, learning motivation, total editor activity, and game level quality. Video Help is a close second across these same measures." A review of 85 game-making programs found most have text help, about half video help, and only a small number interactive help. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.03519); [author PDF](https://people.csail.mit.edu/dkao/pdf/kao2020fdg.pdf)
- Nielsen Norman Group, mobile tutorials study: participants who read tutorials were not more successful (91% task success versus 94% for those who skipped); they rated tasks as harder (single-ease score 4.92 versus 5.49 on a 1–7 scale); completion times were not significantly different (93.49 s versus 85.17 s). NN/g's reading: tutorials can make an app seem more complicated, and it reports the same effect with coach marks and instructional overlays. — [NN/g, Mobile Tutorials](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/mobile-tutorials/); numbers via (secondary) [eLearning Industry](https://elearningindustry.com/offer-tutorials-for-using-elearning) and (vendor) [HelpHero](https://helphero.co/blog/product-tours/)
- The NN/g study covered four unfamiliar iOS apps with 70 participants and measured task performance, not long-term retention (secondary summary). — [HelpHero](https://helphero.co/blog/product-tours/)
- NN/g distinguishes "push revelations" (help pushed at the user, such as tutorials at launch) from "pull revelations" (help triggered by a signal that the user would benefit at that moment); it advises making the existence of contextual help visible without detail until asked, and not requiring users to hold multi-step information in working memory. — [NN/g, Onboarding Tutorials vs. Contextual Help](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/onboarding-tutorials/)
- Celia Hodent (who worked on Fortnite's user experience at Epic): her Interaction 19 slides argue from cognitive science that memory is fallible and attention is limited: "We can hardly pay attention to tutorial texts when busy killing zombies." — [IxDA slides, "The UX of Fortnite", 2019](https://interaction19.ixda.org/download/talk-the-ux-of-fortnite-celia-hodent.pdf); same point reported by [ComputerBase, July 2019](https://www.computerbase.de/2019-07/interface-design-fortnite/)
- Hodent's GDC 2016 talk "The Gamer's Brain, Part 2: UX of Onboarding and Player Engagement" has sections titled "Learning: Active Tutorials" and "Discovery: Remove barriers". — [Class Central listing of the GDC talk](https://classcentral.com/course/youtube-the-gamer-s-brain-part-2-ux-of-onboarding-and-player-engagement-158058)
- A secondary account of a Hodent interview (Lennart Nacke's newsletter, April 2023) describes a five-step onboarding plan that starts by identifying what players need to learn and stresses telling players why a concept matters, not just listing what to know. — [Acagamic newsletter](https://acagamic.com/newsletter/2023/04/04/dont-spook-the-newbies-unveiling-5-proven-game-onboarding-techniques/)
- George Fan (Plants vs. Zombies), as reported from his GDC 2012 talk: if you give players the option, they will almost always skip the tutorial; hence blend it into play. — [Game Developer write-up, March 2012](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/gdc-2012-10-tutorial-tips-from-i-plants-vs-zombies-i-creator-george-fan)
- Other academic leads surfaced by title only: "The First Hour Experience: How the Initial Play can Engage (or Lose) New Players" (CHI PLAY 2014) — [Microsoft Research](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/?p=167075); a first-time-user-experience study finding that FTUE affects perceived "information quality" — [Bournemouth eprint](https://eprints.bournemouth.ac.uk/32321/1/ftue240418.pdf)

### Inferences
- A game that asks a newcomer to assemble a body and wire behaviours with a block editor sits at the Foldit end of Andersen's range (mechanics not discoverable by poking), so the "tutorials are not worth it" reading of that paper does not apply; the "tutorials help when complexity is high" reading does. Kao's game-making result points the same way and names the form: interactive help inside the tool.
- The three strands agree on form: teach in context, at the moment of need, by having the player do the thing. They also agree on what fails: front-loaded screens, text shown while attention is elsewhere, and anything skippable that sits between the player and play.
- An always-available help button is not a substitute for in-context teaching; in Andersen's data it helped only in the complex game and hurt in one simple game.
- Andersen's and NN/g's samples were anonymous players and test participants. Invited friends arrive with social motivation, so absolute drop-off figures will not transfer; the direction of the effects should.

### Gaps
- I could not read the Andersen paper itself, so per-game sample sizes, the exact conditions and the result for tutorial freedom are unconfirmed. One snippet contained the phrase "restricting player freedom in order to focus the player's attention on a target concept improves learnability"; I believe that is the paper restating a prior-literature hypothesis, not its result. Unverified recollection: the paper's finding was that tutorial freedom did not affect player behaviour in any of the three games, that context-sensitive tutorials beat context-insensitive ones in Foldit only, and that samples were roughly 13,000 (Refraction), 22,000 (Hello Worlds) and 10,000 (Foldit). The writer should check before quoting any of this.
- No study was found that directly tests forced versus skippable tutorials on retention.
- No large-scale replication of Andersen on a modern mobile game was found; Passalacqua (n=40, lab) is the only later study retrieved.
- Hodent's own wording of her onboarding method (from "The Gamer's Brain" book or the GDC talk) was not retrieved; only slide fragments and a second-hand interview summary.
- NN/g article dates and authors were not shown in snippets. Unverified recollection: "Mobile Tutorials" and "Onboarding Tutorials vs. Contextual Help" date from about 2020.

## 2. Classic designer accounts and their concrete rules

### Takeaway
The first-hand accounts converge on a small rule set: fold teaching into real play, introduce one concept at a time in a deliberately bare space, have the player do each thing once, keep words to a handful, show hints only to players who need them, and let playtests decide where an extra step is needed. Duolingo adds a rule about order: let people play before asking them to sign up.

### Cited Findings
- George Fan's GDC 2012 talk, "How I Got My Mom to Play Through Plants vs. Zombies", was reported as ten tutorial tips. — [GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1015541/); [Game Developer write-up](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/gdc-2012-10-tutorial-tips-from-i-plants-vs-zombies-i-creator-george-fan); [Filament Games summary](https://www.filamentgames.com/blog/10-tips-designing-game-tutorial)
- Tips confirmed in snippets: (a) blend the tutorial into the game; (b) spread out the teaching of mechanics; (c) just get the player to do the action once; (d) use fewer words, with "no more than eight words on the screen at any time"; (e) use unobtrusive, passive messages that do not interrupt play; (f) use adaptive messaging, so that only players "doing the wrong thing" see a tip and others see none; (g) use visuals to teach, such as animated arrows and blinking, and make each character's look express its function. — [Filament Games summary](https://www.filamentgames.com/blog/10-tips-designing-game-tutorial); [talk notes](https://notes.hamatti.org/sources/talks/how-i-got-my-mom-to-play-through-plants-vs.-zombies); [Game Developer write-up](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/gdc-2012-10-tutorial-tips-from-i-plants-vs-zombies-i-creator-george-fan)
- Fan on pacing: "quite conservative… we introduced peripheral mechanics very slowly"; money is not introduced until the player has finished 10 levels, and then by a coin with a large arrow over it (page attribution uncertain between these two reports of the talk). — [AOL/Joystiq report, March 2012](https://www.aol.com/2012/03/09/plants-vs-zombies-casual-gamers-strategy-game/); [GameZone report](https://gamezone.com/news/plants-vs-zombies-creator-trusts-in-good-tutorials/amp)
- A UX reading of the same talk: limiting the interaction space lets the player learn the system by observation without any text. — [UX Collective, "Lessons in onboarding from Plants vs Zombies"](https://uxdesign.cc/lessons-in-onboarding-from-plants-vs-zombies-d936d58c10c)
- Portal (2007), in-game developer commentary. Robin Walker: "Portal is effectively an extended player training exercise"; the button-and-box mechanic is taught before portals. The team "very deliberately introduce and train each gameplay concept" so later chambers can assume it. — [Portal developer commentary transcript](https://theportalwiki.com/wiki/Portal_developer_commentary); [Combine OverWiki transcript](https://combineoverwiki.net/wiki/Developer_commentary/Portal)
- Portal playtest-driven changes: unnecessary objects scattered around rooms distracted players from the training, so chambers were made cleaner; Test Chamber 08 "was shown to introduce too many new concepts at once", so two simpler chambers were added before it; "flinging" was hard to train and needed a dedicated set-up; Chamber 11 is a deliberate review after concepts were introduced "in small, easily-understood segments". — [Portal developer commentary transcript](https://theportalwiki.com/wiki/Portal_developer_commentary)
- Portal 2 commentary: playtesters became fatigued when solving many complex chambers in a row. — [Portal Wiki page listed in search results as "Portal 2 developer commentary"](https://theportalwiki.com/wiki/Bob_the_Janitor)
- A formal breakdown of how Portal introduces and elaborates concepts exists. — [Game Developer, "Analysis of game concepts and player learning in Portal"](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/analysis-of-game-concepts-and-player-learning-in-portal)
- Breath of the Wild's Great Plateau (2017): a walled starting region described as "an intricately designed miniature version of the entire map" in place of a conventional tutorial mission; the player cannot leave until earning the paraglider; it emphasises exploring and experimenting, "only giving hints on what to do"; Nintendo Life's Jim Norman called it "something of a World 1-1-like tutorial masterclass" (quotations are from coverage surfaced in search; which page carried which was not shown). — [Wikipedia, Great Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Plateau); [Zelda Universe, 2023](https://zeldauniverse.net/2023/09/07/realm-of-memories-creating-the-perfect-tutorial-for-a-franchise/)
- Supercell: Clash Royale puts the player into the thick of the action moments after launch. — [PlaytestCloud blog](https://blog.playtestcloud.com/first-time-user-experience-can-make-or-break-your-game/)
- Duolingo lets people try a lesson before creating an account. Moving the sign-up screen back a few steps produced about a 20% increase in daily active users (Gina Gotthilf, First Round Review interview; date recalled as about 2017, not shown in the snippet). — [First Round Review](https://review.firstround.com/the-tenets-of-a-b-testing-from-duolingos-master-growth-hacker)
- Duolingo follow-up tests: skippable "soft wall" sign-up prompts before a blocking "hard wall"; "without those soft walls priming a sign-up as they're ignored, those hard walls perform significantly worse"; the refinements together gave a further 8.2% increase in daily active users. — [First Round Review](https://review.firstround.com/the-tenets-of-a-b-testing-from-duolingos-master-growth-hacker); (vendor) [Taplytics](https://taplytics.com/blog/duolingo-ab-test-onboarding)

### Inferences
- A consolidated rule list the accounts support: (1) teaching happens inside real play, never before it; (2) one new concept per step, in a space stripped of everything else; (3) the player performs each action once, then moves on; (4) about eight words at a time; (5) hints fire on evidence of trouble, not on a schedule; (6) secondary systems wait (PvZ held its currency back ten levels); (7) a review step gathers concepts before the game opens up; (8) when a playtester stalls, add a smaller step before the hard one instead of adding text.
- The Great Plateau and PvZ both show that "restricted" need not feel like a tutorial: a small, complete version of the real thing teaches while still being the game.
- Fan's and Valve's method is observational (watch one unfamiliar person play), which suits a game with 5 to 20 players better than funnel analytics.

### Gaps
- Three of Fan's ten tips were not confirmed in snippets. Unverified recollection of the full list: blend the tutorial into the game; better to have the player "do" than "read"; spread out the teaching of mechanics; just get the player to do it once; use fewer words; use unobtrusive messaging; use adaptive messaging; don't create noise; use visuals to teach; leverage what people already know.
- No primary source retrieved for Super Mario Bros. World 1-1 (Unverified recollection: Miyamoto and Tezuka explained in a 2015 Eurogamer video that the first Goomba, blocks and mushroom are placed so the player learns by a safe first mistake).
- No designer account retrieved for Animal Crossing's first days, Pikmin (one article surfaced by title only: [Retroware, 2022](https://articles.retroware.com/2022/01/10/pikmins-first-ten-minutes-are-totally-brilliant/)), King's first-time experience, or Monument Valley.
- Duolingo figures are from an interview, are about daily active users not retention, and date from around 2017; no Duolingo-published retention figure for delayed sign-up was found.

## 3. Callouts, coach marks, spotlights and tooltips

### Takeaway
Callouts work when they are few, short, attached to the thing they describe and triggered by the user's own action; they fail as a pushed sequence. The measured pattern across vendor data is steep: about three steps is the ceiling, and user-launched guidance completes at roughly double the rate of guidance that appears unasked.

### Cited Findings
- NN/g on instructional overlays: showing multiple coach marks or tips in a row strains short-term memory and can make an app look complicated; keep them as short as possible; set coach-mark text in a different font from the interface so it is not mistaken for part of the UI. — [NN/g, Instructional Overlays and Coach Marks](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/mobile-instructional-overlay/)
- NN/g's tutorial study found no task-success benefit from reading tutorials and a worse perceived-ease score (4.92 versus 5.49 of 7), and it reports the same effect for coach marks and overlays. — [NN/g, Mobile Tutorials](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/mobile-tutorials/)
- NN/g's push versus pull distinction: pull help is triggered by a signal that the user would benefit now; show that help exists without showing its detail until asked. — [NN/g, Onboarding Tutorials vs. Contextual Help](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/onboarding-tutorials/)
- Chameleon 2025 benchmark (vendor; figures via Chameleon's blog and third-party pages): three-step tours reach 72% completion; adding a fourth step drops completion to 45%; tours of seven or more steps reach 16%. Click-triggered tours (started by the user) complete at 67%, the highest of any trigger, against 31% for tours that appear after a set delay. — [Chameleon benchmark report](https://chameleon.io/benchmark-report); [Chameleon, "What 550M data points say about your product tour"](https://www.chameleon.io/blog/mastering-product-tours)
- Chameleon 2024 report (vendor): average tour completion 30% to 33.5%; five-step tours 21.6%; tours with more than four steps fall below average. — [Chameleon Benchmark Report 2024](https://www.chameleon.io/benchmark-report-24)
- Chameleon 2023 report (vendor): average 30% for all tours; four-step tours did best that year; tours started by the user from a launcher reached 61.65% completion. — [Chameleon Benchmark Report 2023](https://www.chameleon.io/benchmark-report-2023)
- Chameleon 2019 report (vendor, 15 million interactions): average completion 61%; four-step tours 46%; a progress indicator raised completion by 12%. — [Chameleon, 15 million interactions](https://www.chameleon.io/blog/product-tour-benchmarks-highlights); [2019 report PDF](https://www.chameleon.io/assets/chameleon-product-tour-benchmarks-report-2019.pdf)
- Userpilot 2024 (vendor, 188 companies): onboarding checklist completion averages 19.2% with a median of 10.1%; best sector 24.5%, worst 12.5%. — [Userpilot checklist benchmark 2024](https://userpilot.com/blog/?p=197841)
- Apple Human Interface Guidelines: tips are "a great way to teach people about new or less obvious features in your app, or help them discover faster ways to accomplish a task"; keep help relevant to the current context; put instructional content near the interface area it describes; ask for permissions when people first use the feature that needs them. — [HIG, Offering help](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/offering-help); [HIG, Onboarding](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/onboarding)
- Apple TipKit: tips are contextual and meant for "new, interesting, or unused features people haven't discovered on their own yet"; the framework has a display-frequency setting that controls how soon a new tip may appear after another has been shown; Apple's WWDC 2023 session says effective tips are actionable, instructional and easy to remember. — [TipKit documentation](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/tipkit); [WWDC23, Make features discoverable with TipKit](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2023/10229/)
- George Fan's limit of eight words on screen at once, and his rule that hints appear only for players doing the wrong thing. — [Filament Games summary](https://www.filamentgames.com/blog/10-tips-designing-game-tutorial)
- Hodent: text shown while the player is busy is not read. — [IxDA slides, 2019](https://interaction19.ixda.org/download/talk-the-ux-of-fortnite-celia-hodent.pdf)
- Practitioner guidance (lower authority): onboarding may be unnecessary for simple, self-explanatory apps and is most justified for non-standard interactions such as bespoke gestures. — [Shopify Partners blog](https://www.shopify.com/partners/blog/mobile-app-onboarding)
- Design systems that document a coach-mark component and content rules exist and could be mined for wording limits (surfaced by title only). — [Adobe Spectrum, Coach mark](https://spectrum.adobe.com/page/coach-mark); [Productboard content patterns, coachmarks](https://design.productboard.com/latest/foundations/content/content-patterns/coachmarks-3ohOXqoO-3ohOXqoO)

### Inferences
- A defensible callout budget from the sources: no more than three in any one sequence; one idea each; about eight words where possible; anchored to the control it explains; shown when the player is about to use that control and never while something else demands attention; a visible step count if there is a sequence; visually distinct from the interface.
- Prefer callouts the player starts (tap a marker, tap "show me") over ones that arrive on a timer: vendor data shows roughly 61 to 67% completion for user-launched tours against roughly 30% for pushed ones.
- A checklist is weak as the only teaching device (about one in five complete it in software products), but those figures come from work software with unmotivated users; treat them as a caution, not a prediction.
- Apple's frequency control implies a rule worth copying: after one tip, wait before showing another, and never stack them.
- An owner-requested "tutorial with callouts" is compatible with the evidence if each callout is the prompt for a real action in the real editor (Fan's "do it once", Kao's interactive help), not a tour of the screen.

### Gaps
- The Chameleon averages are not comparable across years (61% in 2019, about 30% in 2023 and 2024). The "72% for three steps, 45% for four" figures attributed to 2025 closely resemble the 2019 report's numbers and may be recycled; I could not open the 2025 report to check.
- All completion data is vendor-published, from business software on desktop. No measured data was found for coach marks in games or on phone browsers.
- No data found on pulsing hotspots or beacons specifically, on time-to-dismiss, or on how often callouts are dismissed unread.
- Nothing retrieved from Material Design or Appcues. Unverified recollection: older Material guidance described three onboarding models (self-select, quickstart, top user benefits) and a "feature discovery" tap-target prompt, and this guidance was not carried into Material 3.
- Nothing retrieved on empty states as teaching.
- The rules "always allow skipping" and "do not re-show a dismissed tooltip in the same session" appeared only in low-authority sources.

## 4. Mobile game first-time user experience benchmarks, 2024 to 2026

### Takeaway
Commercial mobile games keep roughly a fifth to a third of installers to day 1, and the figure fell again in 2025; no trustworthy public benchmark exists for tutorial completion or tutorial length. Most of the numbers describe strangers acquired by advertising and are a poor yardstick for a private friends game.

### Cited Findings
- GameAnalytics, Q1 2024: global median retention 22.91% on day 1, 4.20% on day 7 and 0.85% on day 28; mid-core titles averaged 31.7% day-1 retention in North America (page attribution probable, not certain). — [App Developer Magazine summary](https://appdevelopermagazine.com/mobile-gaming-industry-benchmarks-offer-fresh-look-on-industry/)
- GameAnalytics 2025 report (16 genres): day-1 retention continued its downward trend from 2024; median day 1 near 22% at the start of 2025; top quartile just over 30%. — [GameDev Reports summary](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/gameanalytics-mobile-gaming-benchmarks); [report PDF](https://investgame.net/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/2025-GameAnalytics-Mobile-Gaming-Benchmarks.pdf)
- GameAnalytics 2026 reports (January 2026): day-1, day-7 and day-30 retention declined again in 2025. — [GameDev Reports summary](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/gameanalytics-mobile-and-pc-game); [retention benchmarks PDF](https://investgame.net/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/2026-01-20-Mobile_retention_benchmarks_2026.pdf)
- Secondary figures that conflict or could not be traced (page attribution uncertain among the candidates listed): a top-quartile day-1 figure of 26.48 to 27.69% attributed to GameAnalytics; a "35/15/5" (day 1/7/30) target said to replace the older "40/20/10" rule of thumb; a median day-1 figure of 10.3% that looks like an outlier. — [Segwise](https://segwise.ai/blog/mobile-gaming-app-user-retention-strategies.md); [Game Growth Advisor](https://gamegrowthadvisor.com/blog/2026-03-17-mobile-game-kpis-benchmarks-2026/); [AppFollow](https://appfollow.io/blog/mobile-game-retention); [Playio](https://blog.playio.co/mobile-game-kpi-performance-goals-retention-strategy)
- By genre (secondary, attributed to Mistplay, not GameAnalytics): day-1 retention 31.85% for puzzle, 30.10% for simulation, 25.39% for strategy. The same source warns that published genre data is about four years old, the last broad breakdown being AppsFlyer's from Q3 2022. — [Segwise](https://segwise.ai/blog/mobile-gaming-app-user-retention-strategies.md)
- Appodeal 2025 (Android casual): retention strongest in sports, racing and tabletop, with some subgenres above 50% on day 1. — [Appodeal Mobile Casual Benchmarks 2025](https://files.gameindustrylibrary.com/documents/mobile-casual-benchmarks-report-2025.pdf)
- Tutorial completion: no published norm found. Vendor pages use illustrative figures only, such as raising install-to-tutorial-complete from 65% to above 80%. — [Bruin use case](https://getbruin.com/use-cases/mobile-gaming/onboarding-tutorial-drop-off/)
- Analysis advice: read the tutorial as a step-by-step funnel, not an average; fix the step that loses the most players in absolute terms (reach at step times drop rate), not the step with the highest percentage drop. — [Bugnet](https://bugnet.io/blog/how-to-measure-and-reduce-tutorial-drop-off)
- Rules of thumb from practitioners (unsourced by them; page attribution uncertain among the candidates listed): day-1 retention of 45% signals a working first session, below 25% a broken one; one vendor tracks distance from a 60-second target for time to core gameplay; a tutorial completion rate such as 70% says less than knowing which step loses 12% of players. — [Playio blog](https://blog.playio.co/mobile-game-onboarding-retention); [Keewano](https://keewano.com/blog/first-time-user-experience-ftue-mobile-games/); [Bruin use case](https://getbruin.com/use-cases/mobile-gaming/onboarding-tutorial-drop-off/); [Pocket Gamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/first-impressions-count-creating-the-ultimate-first-time-user-experience)
- Tutorial length is treated as a function of complexity: a few steps for simple mechanics, much longer for complex titles (page attribution uncertain among the candidates listed). — [Unity, 10 first-time user experience tips](https://unity.com/how-to/10-first-time-user-experience-tips-games); [Keewano](https://keewano.com/blog/first-time-user-experience-ftue-mobile-games/)
- GameAnalytics' own advice: players should do something fun as soon as they open the game. — [GameAnalytics blog](https://gameanalytics.com/?p=4403)
- Benchmarks should be segmented by revenue model: subscription titles are reported to hold 2.5 times the day-30 retention of ad-supported ones (page attribution uncertain among the candidates listed). — [Game Growth Advisor](https://gamegrowthadvisor.com/blog/2026-03-17-mobile-game-retention-strategies-2026/); [Playio, retention benchmarks 2026](https://blog.playio.co/d1-d7-d30-retention-benchmarks-2026)
- A widely repeated claim that 73% of players quit within 24 hours traces to one blog with no primary source. — [Adrian Crook](https://adriancrook.com/?p=8151)

### Inferences
- Advice that is driven by monetisation or paid acquisition and does not carry over to a private friends game: chasing day-1 percentages against genre medians; rushing players past teaching to reach a first purchase, advert or notification opt-in; welcome-back gifts and starter offers; funnel optimisation by A/B test (meaningless with 5 to 20 players).
- Advice that does carry over: reach something enjoyable within the first minute or so; treat onboarding as a sequence of steps and find the one where people stall; scale tutorial length to complexity instead of to a fixed minute count.
- For a group this small, each friend's first session is the data. Watching or logging where each of the first few players hesitates replaces benchmarks.
- Commercial day-1 medians near 22% describe low-intent installs from adverts. An invited friend is a different population; the realistic risk is not mass churn but a friend bouncing off the first session and not returning, which the group will notice.

### Gaps
- No reliable figure for typical tutorial length in minutes or tutorial completion rate in 2024 to 2026.
- No GameAnalytics genre table could be opened; the genre figures above are second-hand and attributed to another firm.
- Nothing retrieved from Deconstructor of Fun or Naavik on first-time experience.
- Retention sources disagree with each other; only the Q1 2024 GameAnalytics medians and the direction of travel (down in 2024 and 2025) are reasonably solid.

## 5. Teaching a creative or programming tool

### Takeaway
Tools that get beginners to a finished thing do it with a guided first build inside the real editor, a deliberately small starting palette and a reward at each step, and they keep a blank canvas available alongside. The one large experiment found interactive in-editor help beat text, video and an agent on both learning and the quality of what people made. Calendar-gated unlocking of creative tools has a documented failure.

### Cited Findings
- Kao (FDG 2020, n=1,646): in game-making software, interactive help had the largest positive effect on time spent, controls learnability, learning motivation, editor activity and the quality of the level built; video help was a close second; most of 85 game-making tools reviewed offer only text help. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.03519)
- Game Builder Garage (Nintendo, 2021), "Ask the Developer" interview with director Naoki Masuda and subdirector Kosuke Teshima: the Interactive Lessons guide players through each step, such as connecting one Nodon to another; the aim was for beginners to finish a game, encouraging them "by rewarding them at each step"; the target audience was people who had never tried programming; it was tested with elementary-school pupils. — [Nintendo, Ask the Developer Vol. 1, part 1](https://www.nintendo.com/us/whatsnew/ask-the-developer-vol-1-game-builder-garage-part-1/); [part 2](https://www.nintendo.com/us/whatsnew/ask-the-developer-vol-1-game-builder-garage-part-2/); [part 4](https://www.nintendo.com/us/whatsnew/ask-the-developer-vol-1-game-builder-garage-part-4/)
- Game Builder Garage personifies its programming nodes: over 80 "Nodon", each a character, with the cast loosely modelled on an idol group. — [Nintendo Life, July 2021](https://www.nintendolife.com/news/2021/07/game_builder_garage_team_took_inspiration_from_idol_groups_for_nodons)
- Super Mario Maker (2015) shipped with its tools unlocking over nine days, requiring at least five minutes of building each day to earn the next day's set. Nintendo's stated reason was to let players learn the tools gradually. — [GameSided, August 2015](https://gamesided.com/2015/08/12/full-super-mario-maker-tools-unlock-after-9-days-of-play); [My Nintendo News, September 2015](https://mynintendonews.com/2015/09/27/nintendo-explains-why-not-all-mario-maker-tools-were-available-at-start/)
- The nine-day wait drew complaints and was removed by a day-one patch (version 1.01), which replaced timed unlocks with unlocking through play; one report describes the new pace as about 15 minutes per batch instead of one day. — [GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/super-mario-maker-patch-removes-item-unlock-delay-now-15-minutes-not-1-day-per-batch/); [GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/au/super-mario-maker-update-unlocks); criticism: [Nintendo World Report editorial](https://nintendoworldreport.com/editorial/40974/super-mario-makers-slow-unlocking-feels-more-frustrating-than-fun)
- Scratch Microworlds (Moran Tsur, MIT Media Lab thesis, "introducing novices to Scratch using an interest-based, open-ended, scaffolded experience"): each microworld is a customised Scratch editor with a small set of blocks for making projects on one theme; a design-based study with children and educators on how to simplify early experiences while still supporting creativity. — [MIT DSpace](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/112561); [MIT Media Lab project page](https://www.media.mit.edu/projects/microworlds/)
- Swift Playgrounds offers both routes: guided lessons ("Get Started with Code") with challenges, and a blank template with no code. This is product documentation, not a study. — [Apple, Swift Playgrounds](https://developer.apple.com/swift-playgrounds); [WWDC22, Create engaging content for Swift Playgrounds](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2022/110349/)
- Spore's Creature Creator (2008): designer Chaim Gingold framed it as a "magic crayon", "a toy that is simple to use and yet gives the user enough power to create something they'll appreciate", contrasted with Photoshop, which is powerful but hard for most people; he called the Creature Creator the hardest design problem among Spore's editors and said "soft mastery" guided it. His site reports over 189 million creations made with it. — [Game Developer, Gingold on the "magic crayon" approach](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/gingold-talks-i-spore-i-s-magic-crayon-approach); [Beyond Sims, 2008](https://beyondsims.com/2008/06/spores-crayon-approach-discussion-with-chaim-gingold/); [chaim.io](https://chaim.io/)

### Inferences
- The supported shape for a first build: the player makes a real, finished thing in the real editor, led step by step, with a reduced set of parts and words (the microworld idea), and each step ends in a visible result. Free building should be reachable early for those who want it.
- "Magic crayon" is a useful test for a first-session creator: whatever a beginner makes should look and behave appealingly with almost no skill, so the first success does not depend on mastering the editor.
- Mario Maker is the closest precedent for unlocking parts and words over days, and its lesson is specific: gating by the calendar alone frustrated motivated players, while gating by activity (use what you have, earn the next set) was accepted. A daily cadence may still suit a 15-minute-a-day game, but eager players need a way to earn more by doing more.
- Giving behaviour blocks character (Nodon) is a tested way to make abstract logic approachable to non-programmers and fits a playful tone.

### Gaps
- No evidence found for a "first success within N minutes" threshold in creative tools; the only time figure anywhere in this research is a vendor's 60-second target for reaching core gameplay.
- Game Builder Garage's "checkpoints" were not confirmed in snippets. Unverified recollection: between its seven guided lessons, short checkpoint puzzles must be solved to prove the idea was understood before the next lesson opens, and a reference guide stays available throughout.
- The Scratch Microworlds thesis findings (as opposed to its design) were not retrieved, and no controlled comparison of guided first build versus blank canvas was found for Scratch or ScratchJr.
- Nothing retrieved on Human Resource Machine, Dreams, ScratchJr or Spore's stage-by-stage introduction of its editors.

## 6. Teaching over days

### Takeaway
The durable pattern is to hold secondary systems back, make every term explainable on demand where it appears, and greet returning players with a short re-orientation and a clear next step. Evidence here is mostly designer practice, not measurement.

### Cited Findings
- Plants vs. Zombies held back peripheral systems on purpose; its currency appears only after ten levels. — [AOL/Joystiq report, March 2012](https://www.aol.com/2012/03/09/plants-vs-zombies-casual-gamers-strategy-game/)
- Super Mario Maker's day-by-day unlock was meant to teach gradually but was replaced by activity-based unlocking after complaints. — [GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/super-mario-maker-patch-removes-item-unlock-delay-now-15-minutes-not-1-day-per-batch/)
- Crusader Kings III (2020) paired a guided tutorial with an in-game encyclopaedia and tooltips nested inside tooltips, described in its developer diary "Tutorials and Tooltips and Encyclopedias, Oh My!" — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/the-crusader-kings-3-team-is-working-hard-to-make-the-game-easier-to-learn)
- Paradox later described nested tooltips, "as made famous by Crusader Kings 3", as letting designers explain anything without writing a novel in each tooltip, while letting players dig deeper only if they choose. — [Victoria 3 dev diary 29, User Experience](https://forum.paradoxplaza.com/forum/developer-diary/victoria-3-dev-diary-29-user-experience.1506484/)
- A designer's analysis of the pattern raises the question of depth and suggests a limit of about two or three levels of nesting. — [Philip Ardeljan, "Tooltips in tooltips"](https://philip.design/blog/tooltips-in-tooltips)
- Apple's TipKit builds in pacing: a configurable minimum gap before another tip may appear, and tips aimed at features a person has not yet found. — [TipKit documentation](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/tipkit)
- Game Accessibility Guidelines: let players replay instructions they have already unlocked. — [Game Accessibility Guidelines](https://gameaccessibilityguidelines.com/)
- Returning players: give a quick, clear re-orientation (a concise refresher and clear next steps) so they resume promptly. — [Bugnet](https://bugnet.io/blog/how-to-design-a-games-onboarding-for-returning-players)
- Do not greet a returning player with a list of everything that went wrong while they were away; the first moments back should make them glad they returned. — [GamesBrief](https://gamesbrief.com/?p=11267)
- In casual mobile games a welcome-back gift is often used to re-engage lapsed players, but it is a one-time lift; follow-up missions sustain engagement better. — [GameRefinery](https://gamerefinery.com/four-ways-how-mobile-games-re-engage-lapsed-players)
- A CHI 2021 study of returning to long single-player games identifies a "pivot point", a moment of reconnection that designers can build around. — [University of Calgary iLab, Hammad et al.](https://ilab.ucalgary.ca/publications/chi-2021-hammad/)
- Checklists used as soft tutorials are completed by a minority in business software (average 19.2%). — [Userpilot checklist benchmark 2024](https://userpilot.com/blog/?p=197841)
- Help that the user starts completes far more often than help that is pushed (61.65% from a launcher in 2023; 67% click-triggered against 31% timed in 2025). — [Chameleon Benchmark Report 2023](https://www.chameleon.io/benchmark-report-2023); [Chameleon blog](https://www.chameleon.io/blog/mastering-product-tours)

### Inferences
- "Available without nagging" has a concrete form in these sources: every game term is tappable where it appears and opens a short explanation that may itself contain tappable terms (two or three levels at most); past lessons can be replayed; new tips are rate-limited and tied to something the player has not yet tried.
- New parts and words arriving over days are themselves the teaching schedule. Each arrival is a natural moment for one short, in-context callout, and nothing else that day.
- For a player who misses a few days, the evidence favours a brief "here is what happened to your creature and here is one thing to do now" over a backlog of missed events.
- Welcome-back gifts are a monetisation-era device; the transferable part is the follow-up task that gives a returning player something to do.

### Gaps
- Nothing retrieved on Hades' codex, Civilopedia, "tip of the day", quests as tutorials, or "why did this happen" explanations; these remain unsourced. Unverified recollection: Hades' codex entries fill in gradually as the player meets and uses things, so the reference grows with experience.
- No designer account retrieved for Animal Crossing's real-time first week. Unverified recollection: New Horizons opens facilities over the first several real days and uses its Nook Miles goals as a rolling checklist of things to try.
- No measured data on drip-feeding mechanics and retention outside commercial free-to-play practice.

## 7. Social onboarding in small-group games

### Takeaway
The invite-driven games that spread fastest ask for nothing but a link or code and a name: no install, no account, play in the browser. Measured data on what each extra step costs is thin; the strongest number is Duolingo's roughly 20% rise in daily users from moving sign-up after first play.

### Cited Findings
- Jackbox: "not a mobile app, and we do not offer or require one to play"; players join from any device with a browser by going to jackbox.tv, entering a four-letter room code and typing a name; only the host needs to own the game. — [Jackbox support, How do I join a game](https://support.jackboxgames.com/hc/en-us/articles/15794759479959-How-do-I-join-a-game); [Jackbox support, Getting started](https://support.jackboxgames.com/hc/en-us/articles/15794771245975-How-do-I-get-started-playing-Jackbox-Games)
- Browser party games such as Skribbl.io and Gartic Phone follow the same model: any browser, no install, no account. A shared full link can be pasted straight from a chat, whereas a short room code has to be read out or typed (page attribution uncertain between these two comparison pages). — [Quizado, games like Jackbox](https://quizado.com/games-like-jackbox); [Fone.tips, games like Jackbox](https://fone.tips/games-like-jackbox/)
- No-install joining also works on locked-down work or school devices where apps cannot be installed (design claim, no data). — [Trivia Anywhere](https://www.triviaanywhere.com/no-app-trivia)
- Practitioner opinion: every "download this first" or "create an account to continue" step loses a percentage of the group. — [DEV Community, browser-based team activity](https://dev.to/forrestmiller/building-a-virtual-team-activity-that-works-in-any-browser-57ea)
- Duolingo: moving account creation until after a first lesson raised daily active users by about 20%; skippable sign-up prompts shown before a blocking one performed better than a blocking prompt alone (interview, about 2017). — [First Round Review](https://review.firstround.com/the-tenets-of-a-b-testing-from-duolingos-master-growth-hacker)
- Apple's guidance to ask for a permission only when the feature needing it is first used applies the same principle to permissions. — [HIG, Onboarding](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/onboarding)

### Inferences
- For a link shared in a friends' chat, the lowest-friction path is: tap the link, land in the game already inside the right group, type a name, start making a creature. Anything needed to keep the creature across devices (an account, an email) can wait until the player has something worth keeping, presented first as a skippable prompt.
- A full invite link beats a typed code when friends are remote; the link can carry the group identity so the newcomer never chooses a room.
- The inviter is an onboarding asset that commercial games lack: the first screen can show whose world this is and what friends' creatures are doing, which gives the "what am I looking at" context before any instruction.

### Gaps
- Nothing retrieved on Among Us, Wordle sharing, BeReal, Discord Activities or Pokémon GO friend features.
- No measured conversion data for install versus no-install or account versus no-account in games; the Duolingo figure is the only number and concerns a learning app.
- The quotation attributed to Jackbox's chief executive about removing "the barrier of the controller" appeared only on low-quality explainer pages; the original interview was not found, so it is not cited as a finding.

## 8. Accessibility and tone

### Takeaway
Published guidance asks for interactive tutorials in simple, clear language that can be replayed, with motion effects that can be turned off; the designer accounts add a hard brevity limit of about eight words on screen. Evidence on guide characters was not retrieved, apart from Nintendo's choice to give programming blocks personalities.

### Cited Findings
- Game Accessibility Guidelines: include interactive tutorials; use simple, clear language (a basic-tier guideline); allow players to replay unlocked instructions; avoid flickering images and repetitive patterns; let players turn off motion effects such as screen shake; and do not hide accessibility options in settings menus only: mention them in tutorials and tips. — [Game Accessibility Guidelines](https://gameaccessibilityguidelines.com/); summary in [Games for Change tips sheet](https://gamesforchange.org/studentchallenge/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/Tips_Resources_for-Considering-the-Needs-of-Disabled-Gamers-in-Design.pdf)
- Xbox Accessibility Guidelines: where in-game help such as tutorial hints exists, accessibility features must also be explained in an accessible format. — [Xbox Accessibility Guideline 121](https://devdocs.xbox.com/gaming/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/121)
- George Fan's limit: no more than eight words on screen at any time; prefer arrows and visual cues to text. — [Filament Games summary](https://www.filamentgames.com/blog/10-tips-designing-game-tutorial)
- NN/g: keep coach marks as short as possible and visually distinct from the interface; several in a row overload short-term memory. — [NN/g, Instructional Overlays and Coach Marks](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/mobile-instructional-overlay/)
- Hodent: attention is limited and memory fallible, so instruction given while the player is occupied is lost. — [IxDA slides, 2019](https://interaction19.ixda.org/download/talk-the-ux-of-fortnite-celia-hodent.pdf)
- Fan's adaptive rule: do not show hints to players who are already doing the right thing. — [Filament Games summary](https://www.filamentgames.com/blog/10-tips-designing-game-tutorial)
- Tone through character: Game Builder Garage presents each programming node as a named character with a personality. — [Nintendo Life, July 2021](https://www.nintendolife.com/news/2021/07/game_builder_garage_team_took_inspiration_from_idol_groups_for_nodons)
- Apple: effective tips are actionable, instructional and easy to remember. — [WWDC23, Make features discoverable with TipKit](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2023/10229/)

### Inferences
- A workable text standard from these sources: one short sentence per callout, plain words, the action stated first, nothing the picture already shows.
- Skip and replay belong together: let confident players pass a step, and let anyone reopen any lesson later from a visible place.
- Callouts that pulse, bounce or spotlight should have a still version for people who ask for less motion, and should never flash.
- A guide character is safest when it speaks only at the moment of need and stays silent for players who are doing fine, which is Fan's adaptive rule applied to a mascot.

### Gaps
- Nothing retrieved on reading-level targets for game text, or on web-specific reduced-motion practice. Unverified recollection: browsers expose a "prefers-reduced-motion" setting that pages can honour, and the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines ask that content which moves or blinks for more than five seconds can be paused or stopped.
- Nothing retrieved on guide characters. Unverified recollection: Navi in Ocarina of Time (1998) and Fi in Skyward Sword (2011) are the standard cautionary examples of a guide who interrupts with unrequested or obvious advice, and the 2021 re-release of Skyward Sword made Fi's advice optional; Duolingo's owl and Animal Crossing's Tom Nook are the usual positive examples of a guide tied to a clear next goal. None of this was sourced in this session.
- The Xbox guideline number and wording come from a search snippet; check the page before quoting.
