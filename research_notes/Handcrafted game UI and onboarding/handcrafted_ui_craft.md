## 1. Reference games for handmade / papercraft interfaces, and what their makers and critics say

### Takeaway
The documented pattern in the best-sourced examples (Tearaway, Kirby's Epic Yarn, Yoshi's Crafted World, Cozy Grove) is "material first": teams handle or build the real material, make the interface with the same tools as the world, and deliberately leave construction and imperfection visible. Direct designer commentary on the UI itself (as opposed to the world art) is scarce in what could be retrieved; for most titles on the list nothing citable was found.

### Cited Findings

- GDC 2013 talk "Building the Touchy-Feely World of Tearaway" by creative lead Rex Crowle covered designing a world that can "flex, fold, tear and crumple under the fingertips of players". The video is free on the GDC YouTube channel. [snippet] — [GDC Vault](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1017845/Building-the-Touchy-Feely-World), [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/video-building-the-touchy-feely-world-of-media-molecule-s-i-tearaway-i-)
- Crowle: "Even the UI was prototyped in paper by our Graphic Designer, Tom Kiss - the icons made using the same tools as the game." [snippet; exact page within the result set not confirmed, most likely the Pocket Gamer making-of] — [Pocket Gamer: Unfolding story, the making of Tearaway](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/unfolding-story-the-making-of-tearaway/)

The article's framing is that an emphasis on imperfection made the game more personal. [snippet] — [Game Developer: How Tearaway's emphasis on imperfection made the game more personal](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/how-i-tearaway-i-s-emphasis-on-imperfection-made-the-game-more-personal)

### Inferences
- Four recurring moves can be drawn from the sourced examples: (1) handle the real material before drawing it (yarn and cloth at Good-Feel, paper at Media Molecule); (2) build UI and icons with the same tools and rules as the world, so the interface is not a separate "app layer" (Tearaway); (3) leave construction and imperfection visible on purpose, because it reads as approachable (Tearaway); (4) give the menu a physical identity in the fiction (NookPhone).
- For a cut-paper web UI, move (2) translates to: panels, buttons and icons should obey the same "what can scissors and glue do" rules as the creatures and world (flat sheets, cut silhouettes, stacking, shadow; no gradients, bevels or glows that paper cannot produce).
- Animal Crossing is also the cautionary case: charm delivered through slow, repeated menu steps attracts criticism. Charm should sit on top of short paths, not replace them.
- Single-hand authorship (Wilmot's Warehouse) or a tiny shape vocabulary (see Duolingo below) are the two documented ways to keep a hand-drawn icon set coherent.

## 2. Mobile apps praised for warm, characterful interfaces, and what their teams say

### Takeaway
Duolingo is the best-documented case: a tiny shape vocabulary, explicit shadow and colour rules, and Rive state machines so characters react rather than just play. Andy Works' (Not Boring) apps show the "design it like a game" stance, with sound treated as the core of the experience. For Headspace and Finch only third-party analyses were reachable; for Tiimo, Bears Gratitude, Arc, Things, Amie and Opal nothing was retrieved.

### Cited Findings
**Duolingo**
- Illustration guidance pairs a shape-language approach with a few fixed rules including colour palette and shadow use. [snippet] — [design.duolingo.com/illustration](https://design.duolingo.com/illustration)
- Ground shadows under characters are a pill shape, not an oval, because ovals suggest perspective (the style is flat). [snippet] — [design.duolingo.com/illustration](https://design.duolingo.com/illustration)
- Character guidance: repeat shapes across a character so its shape language is cohesive. [snippet] — [design.duolingo.com/illustration/characters](https://design.duolingo.com/illustration/characters)
- The illustrative language (art director Greg Hartman) is built on three shapes: rounded rectangle, circle, rounded triangle. [snippet; third-party Medium article, not Duolingo's own page] — [GDG VIT on Medium](https://medium.com/gdg-vit/decoding-duolingo-how-technology-design-can-shape-learning-journeys-8a37f48138fc)
- Duolingo's blog says the style's minimalism and playfulness help the team keep up with large illustration and animation demand, i.e. the simplicity is a production decision as much as a look. [snippet] — [Duolingo blog: Shape language](https://blog.duolingo.com/shape-language-duolingos-art-style/)
- Rive was first used for reward animations (a chest shaking, gems jumping out), then for phonetic lip-sync as character animation grew; Duolingo hires "creative technologists" who combine animation and engineering to build and optimise Rive files. [snippet] — [Rive blog: creative technologists](https://rive.app/blog/creative-technologists-duolingo-s-solution-to-the-designer-to-developer-handoff), [Rive blog: Lily video call](https://rive.app/blog/duolingo-s-ai-powered-video-call-brings-lily-to-life)
- Each character needed its own set of mouth shapes per viseme, drawn to fit the house style. [snippet] — [Duolingo blog: world character visemes](https://blog.duolingo.com/world-character-visemes)
- Typography: a custom rounded display face ("Feather Bold") for headings with DIN Next Rounded for body text. [recall] — [design.duolingo.com typography](https://design.duolingo.com/identity/typography)
- Detailed Rive rig breakdowns circulating on DEV Community are by freelance animators advertising services, not Duolingo. [snippet] — [example](https://dev.to/uianimation/how-duolingo-uses-rive-for-their-character-animation-and-how-you-can-build-a-similar-rive-mascot-5d19)

**Headspace**
- 2024 rebrand: identity with Italic Studio, a custom typeface from Colophon, guidelines hosted on Standards; the system covers colour, typography, tone and motion, with illustration central. [snippet] — [Standards: Headspace](https://standards.site/examples/headspace), [It's Nice That, Apr 2024](https://www.itsnicethat.com/articles/italic-studio-headspace-graphic-design-project-250424)
- The rebrand widened the characters' facial range to include stress and sadness as well as joy. [snippet] — [PRINT Magazine](https://www.printmag.com/?p=767405)
- Third-party analyses: characters built from rounded forms and circles, faces of two dot eyes and a curved mouth, deliberately ambiguous bodies in varied shapes and sizes, emotion carried by colour combinations; a breathing animation synced to guided audio. These are outside reconstructions, not official rules. [snippet] — [Blake Crosley guide](https://blakecrosley.com/guides/design/headspace), [Raw.Studio case study](https://raw.studio/?p=1771)

**(Not Boring) apps — Andy Works / Not Boring Software**
- Founding essay "No more boring apps" (2020); stated aim is breaking down the barrier between apps, utilities and games. App Store tagline: "Plays like a game, works like an app". [snippet] — [notbor.ing](https://notbor.ing/words/no-more-boring-apps), [App Store bundle](https://apps.apple.com/us/app-bundle/not-boring-apps-the-essentials-made-fun/id1553834540)
- The team designs "like building a game": the Habits check-off borrows a game's charge-up attack, a long press with an expanding sphere, on one large simple button so the interaction is obvious. [snippet; Japanese-language source, attribution within the result set uncertain] — [Mynavi Tech+](https://news.mynavi.jp/techplus/article/svalley-925/), [App Store developer spotlight](https://apps.apple.com/us/story/id1591100111)
- Sound: the apps play sound even with the iPhone mute switch on because "the sound is the experience"; button taps are not a single sound file each (layered or generated). [snippet] — [RevenueCat Launched podcast, 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/andy-allen-not-boring-software-launched-podcast-2026)
- Skins: plan to commission artists each season, some colourways and some full reinterpretations of the interface. Allen: "This is not going to be a mass market product", and a small team cannot out-feature large ones but design is harder to copy. [snippet] — [Brand Knew](https://www.brandknewmag.com/?p=20392)

**Finch**
- Self-care app where the user adopts a bird that grows as real-life self-care tasks are completed, blending virtual-pet nostalgia with wellness routines. [snippet] — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/DE/app/id1528595748), [FoxData](https://foxdata.com/en/blogs/finch-as-app-store-editors-choice-a-self-care-companion/)
- A Pratt design critique praises visible affordances and strong feedback in the task system, but faults onboarding for violating progressive disclosure and notes many supportive features sit behind the paywall. [snippet] — [Pratt IXD critique, Sept 2024](https://ixd.prattsi.org/2024/09/design-critique-finch-ios-app)

### Inferences
- The shared structure across Duolingo and Headspace: very few base shapes, flat fills, one fixed shadow convention, faces reduced to dots and a curve, and a written rule set that lets many people draw as one hand.
- Duolingo's point that simplicity keeps production sustainable is directly relevant to a small team: a cut-paper style with a short rule list is cheap to extend.
- "Interactive, not played back" is the reason teams choose Rive: the character or control has states and inputs, so it can respond to the player.
- Andy Works shows the trade: heavy juice is a product identity for a niche audience, and it leans on platform features (reliable sound, native haptics) that the web only partly has (see section 3).

### Gaps
- Tiimo, Bears Gratitude, Arc, Things, Amie, Opal: nothing retrieved. One search aimed at Apple's "Behind the Design" coverage of Bears Gratitude returned nothing relevant.
- No first-party Headspace or Finch design-team writing was reachable; the Headspace details above are outside reconstructions.
- No official motion principles document was found for Duolingo or Headspace.
- Unsourced recollections to verify: Bears Gratitude won a 2024 Apple Design Award (Delight and Fun) for its hand-drawn bears; (Not Boring) Habits won a 2022 Apple Design Award; Tiimo was Apple's iPhone App of the Year 2025; Duolingo's buttons use a solid darker bottom "lip" that collapses on press to feel like a physical key.

## 3. "Juice", micro-interaction timing, sound and haptics (including what the web can do in 2026)

### Takeaway
The juice canon says make everything respond (tween, squash, particles, sound) but its own authors and later critics frame it as a demonstration of physicality, not a licence for constant motion; UI guidance converges on 100–500 ms, with about 100 ms for taps and 200–300 ms for panels, exits faster than entrances, and deformation kept to a few percent. On the web in 2026, Android Chrome can vibrate via `navigator.vibrate()`, iOS Safari still cannot (only a fragile checkbox-switch workaround exists), and audio on both needs a user gesture to start, with the iPhone silent switch muting Web Audio unless the page opts into the "playback" audio session.

### Cited Findings
**The juice canon**
- "Juice it or lose it" (Martin Jonasson and Petri Purho, GDC Europe 2012) takes a plain block-breaking game and adds juice layer by layer: tweening, particles, screenshake, sound, animation, showing how each layer changes the feel. [snippet] — [Game Juice resource page](https://gamejuice.pages.dev/resources/juice-it-or-lose-it); talk video [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Fy0aCDmgnxg)
- The talk's definition: a juicy game "feels alive and responds to everything you do", with lots of cascading action and response for minimal input. [recall] — [talk video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Fy0aCDmgnxg)
- A design note on juice in interfaces: a small amount may enhance the experience; a lot may make the task take longer and degrade it, so the intended experience comes first. [snippet] — [Brad Woods' digital garden: Juice](https://garden.bradwoods.io/notes/design/juice)
- "The Art of Screenshake" (Jan Willem Nijman, Vlambeer, INDIGO Classes 2013) starts from a dull side-scrolling shooter and applies 30 small tweaks. A university handout lists steps including basic animation and sound, lower enemy HP, more shooting and enemies, bigger explosions, permanence (smoke, debris), a meaningful game-over and slow motion. [snippet] — [Bayreuth game-feel handout (PDF)](https://medienwissenschaft.uni-bayreuth.de/wp-content/uploads/GxD-10-Game-Feel.pdf), [talk video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AJdEqssNZ-U)
- A screenshake recipe attributed to Nijman via a Nuclear Throne interview: kick the camera back 6 px from the aim point and add 4 to a shake amount that decays quickly (small, directional, fast decay). [snippet] — [Infovore: Making game "feel"](https://infovore.org/?p=5275)
- One critic's caveat: the talk was made to show the importance of physicality, not as a general guide to game feel. [snippet] — [Game Design Skills: game feel](https://gamedesignskills.com/game-design/game-feel/)
- Squash and stretch: overuse reads as cartoony, too little as stiff. [snippet] — [Brush Ninja glossary](https://brush.ninja/glossary/animation/squash-and-stretch)

**Disney's principles mapped to UI (secondary guides)**
- Squash and stretch in UI should be a subtle scale change giving a surface physical presence; guides suggest roughly 2–5% deformation, or within ±5% scale, and flag larger values as a fault. [snippet; low-authority sources] — [UI/UX Atlas](https://uiux-atlas.pages.dev/lessons/motion/the-12-principles-of-animation-adapted-to-ui), [animation-principles skill page](https://skills.sh/dylantarre/design-system-skills/animation-principles)
- Anticipation maps to a brief scale-down on press before the main motion, or a hover hint of what will happen. [snippet] — [UI/UX Atlas](https://uiux-atlas.pages.dev/lessons/motion/the-12-principles-of-animation-adapted-to-ui)
- Springs suit directly manipulated elements (drag-to-dismiss, swipeable cards) because they reverse naturally mid-gesture, where a CSS transition may stutter; interpolated easing is enough for most product UI. Overshoot curves are suggested for playful toggles and success states. [snippet] — [UI/UX Atlas](https://uiux-atlas.pages.dev/lessons/motion/the-12-principles-of-animation-adapted-to-ui)
- Fuller treatment exists but was not read. — [Interaction Design Foundation: Disney's 12 principles applied to UI](https://ixdf.org/literature/article/ui-animation-how-to-apply-disney-s-12-principles-of-animation-to-ui-design)

**Timing guidance**
- NN/g: most UI animations should last 100–500 ms depending on complexity and distance travelled; use the shortest time that is not jarring. About 100 ms for simple feedback such as a checkbox or toggle; 200–300 ms for substantial changes such as a modal; 400 ms is very slow and for large movements only; entrances run slightly longer than exits. [snippet] — [NN/g: Executing UX animations, duration and motion characteristics](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/animation-duration/)
- Material 3 duration tokens run from Short1 (50 ms) to ExtraLong4 (1000 ms); snippets show short1–3 = 50/100/150 ms and long tokens at 500/550/600 ms. Emphasized-accelerate is for exits, emphasized-decelerate for entrances. Third-party sources disagree on the "emphasized" curve: (0.2, 0, 0, 1) versus (0.05, 0.7, 0.1, 1). [snippet] — [Compose rule summary](https://skill-wiki.github.io/atom/android-compose/rule-motion-easing-tokens), [M3 token spec](https://m3.material.io/styles/motion/easing-and-duration/tokens-specs)
- Full M3 table: short 50/100/150/200, medium 250/300/350/400, long 450/500/550/600, extra-long 700/800/900/1000 ms. The disagreement above resolves as: standard and the emphasized fallback are cubic-bezier(0.2, 0, 0, 1); emphasized-decelerate is (0.05, 0.7, 0.1, 1); emphasized-accelerate is (0.3, 0, 0.8, 0.15). [recall] — [M3 token spec](https://m3.material.io/styles/motion/easing-and-duration/tokens-specs)
- Material 3 Expressive (2025) moved component motion to spring tokens: "spatial" springs (position, size; may overshoot) and "effects" springs (colour, opacity; no overshoot), each in fast/default/slow. [recall] — [M3 motion overview](https://m3.material.io/styles/motion/overview/how-it-works)
- Apple, WWDC 2023 "Animate with springs": describe springs by perceptual duration and bounce; bounce 0 is the general-purpose default, about 0.15 a brisk small bounce, about 0.3 clearly bouncy, and above roughly 0.4 tends to feel exaggerated for interface work. [recall, moderate confidence on exact figures] — [WWDC23 session 10158](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2023/10158/)
- Apple HIG Motion: prefer quick, precise animations, make motion optional, and avoid adding motion to interactions that occur frequently. [recall] — [HIG: Motion](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion)

**Haptics on the web**
- iOS Safari does not support `navigator.vibrate()` and Apple has announced no plan to; MDN and caniuse still list no support. A browser-compat-data issue claiming it now works on iOS is unverified. [snippet] — [Software Mansion: Haptic feedback on the web](https://swmansion.com/blog/haptic-feedback-on-the-web-why-the-web-deliberately-refuses-to-be-as-tactile-as-native-apps), [BCD issue 29166](https://github.com/mdn/browser-compat-data/issues/29166)
- Workaround: `<input type="checkbox" switch>` (Safari 17.4+) fires the system haptic when toggled, so libraries (ios-haptics, web-haptics) toggle a hidden switch from script. Sources disagree on the minimum version (Safari 17.4, 17.5 or iOS 18). [snippet] — [web-haptics browser support](https://www.mintlify.com/lochie/web-haptics/advanced/browser-support), [azukiazusa.dev](https://azukiazusa.dev/blog/ios-safari-web-haptics.md)
- That is a side effect, not a specified feature: Safari could remove it, it yields only the one system tick (no intensity or pattern), and users can turn system haptics off. Libraries fall back to `navigator.vibrate()` on Android. [snippet] — [web-haptics browser support](https://www.mintlify.com/lochie/web-haptics/advanced/browser-support), [web-haptic-engine platform support](https://sumitsahoo.github.io/web-haptic-engine/docs/guide/platform-support.html)
- A WICG proposal defines an imperative `navigator.playHaptics(effect, intensity)`; still a proposal, with W3C mailing-list activity in March 2026. [snippet] — [WICG/web-haptics](https://github.com/WICG/web-haptics), [W3C device-apis log, Mar 2026](https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/public-device-apis-log/2026Mar/0000.html)
- On Android Chrome `navigator.vibrate(duration | pattern[])` works but needs prior user activation and gives duration patterns only, no amplitude control. [recall] — [MDN: Navigator.vibrate()](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Navigator/vibrate)

**Audio on the web**
- iOS: an `AudioContext` must be created or resumed inside a user gesture, and an actual sound should be played during that unlock. [snippet] — [Abratabia: mobile web game audio issues](https://www.abratabia.com/web-game-audio/mobile-audio-issues.php), [web-audio-touch-unlock](https://npmjs.org/package/web-audio-touch-unlock)
- The iPhone's silent switch mutes Web Audio output by default. [snippet] — [Bugnet: iOS web game audio muted by the silent switch](https://bugnet.io/blog/how-to-fix-ios-web-game-audio-muted-by-the-silent-switch)
- Since iOS 17 a page can set `navigator.audioSession.type = "playback"` so audio is not suspended by the switch. As of Nov 2025 the Audio Session API was an Editor's Draft implemented only in Safari. [snippet] — [WebKit bug 237322](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=237322), [2025 write-up](https://portfolio-2024-b6r.pages.dev/blog/web-audio-ios-unmute)
- Older workaround: start a looping silent `<audio>` element on first gesture, which upgrades the page's session so Web Audio ignores the switch (libraries: unmute, unmute-ios-audio). [snippet] — [swevans/unmute](https://github.com/swevans/unmute), [feross/unmute-ios-audio](https://www.github.com/feross/unmute-ios-audio)
- Android Chrome also starts an `AudioContext` suspended until a user gesture. [recall] — [Chrome autoplay policy](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/autoplay)

### Inferences
- Working timing budget for a paper UI: press feedback about 80–120 ms; small state changes about 150–200 ms; sheet or panel entrance 250–350 ms and exit about 150–250 ms; only rare celebrations beyond 500 ms. Overshoot in the low-bounce range (a few percent past target, one settle), squash and stretch within about ±5%.
- Scale juice inversely with frequency: the more often an action happens, the smaller and shorter its response; save particles, big bounces and long sounds for rare events. This follows from NN/g's "shortest time", Apple's "not on frequent interactions" and the note that heavy juice slows tasks.
- Screenshake belongs to the world canvas if anywhere; for interface panels the paper equivalent of impact is a small directional nudge with fast decay.
- Because iOS has no reliable web haptics, feedback must be complete with sight and sound alone; treat vibration as a bonus on Android behind a setting, and treat the iOS switch trick as removable at any Safari release.
- Because the silent switch mutes by default and many people play muted, sound cannot carry information on its own. Overriding the switch (as Not Boring does natively) is a deliberate choice that should be the player's, with a visible sound toggle.
- Pitch-varying a small set of paper sounds (rustle, snip, tap, tear) through Web Audio avoids the repetitiveness the juice talks warn about and keeps downloads small.

### Gaps
- Neither talk could be watched; the full list of techniques in "Juice it or lose it" and all 30 Vlambeer tweaks are not transcribed here.
- Apple and Material primary pages were not opened; the figures marked [recall] need checking.
- No research evidence (as opposed to guidance) was found on when UI motion becomes tiring, such as habituation or annoyance studies.
- Unverified: whether the iOS switch-haptic trick still works in the current iOS release and whether it must run inside a user gesture; whether Firefox for Android still exposes `navigator.vibrate()`; current behaviour of Android's silent or do-not-disturb modes with web vibration.
- No source was retrieved on UI sound design practice (loudness, length, pitch variation) for cozy games.

## 4. Typography and iconography for cute but legible

### Takeaway
The products studied pair a chunky rounded display face with a calmer rounded text face (Duolingo: Feather Bold with DIN Next Rounded) and keep icons coherent through a small shape vocabulary or a single hand. Among free Google Fonts, secondary sources favour Nunito for running text and Fredoka or Baloo 2 for titles; no authoritative legibility comparison was found, and nothing was found on how such games handle dense numbers.

### Cited Findings
- Nunito is the rounded face most consistently recommended for readable UI and body text; Nunito Sans is its non-rounded sibling. [snippet; SEO-style font blogs] — [Made Good Designs: Nunito alternatives](https://madegooddesigns.com/nunito-alternatives/)
- Quicksand is light and airy, suggested for labels and subheads, and judged less sturdy for body text than Nunito. [snippet] — [Made Good Designs: Quicksand alternatives](https://madegooddesigns.com/?p=7288)
- Baloo 2 is heavy and chunky, described as a display face best kept to about 32 px and up. Fredoka is bolder and more playful than Nunito or Quicksand, with multiple weights and widths in its variable version. M PLUS Rounded was mentioned once, for multilingual (Japanese) coverage. [snippet] — [Inspo AI: rounded fonts](https://www.inspoai.io/blogs/rounded-fonts), [Made Good Designs](https://madegooddesigns.com/nunito-alternatives/)
- These families are reported as SIL Open Font License (commercial use allowed); not verified against the font pages. [snippet] — [fonttrio friendly collection](https://www.mintlify.com/kapishdima/fonttrio/collections/friendly)
- Axis ranges on Google Fonts: Nunito variable weight 200–1000; Fredoka weight 300–700 and width 75–125; Baloo 2 weight 400–800; Quicksand weight 300–700. [recall] — [Nunito](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Nunito), [Fredoka](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Fredoka), [Baloo 2](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Baloo+2), [Quicksand](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Quicksand)
- Two free variable faces built for a handmade feel: Shantell Sans has axes for weight, italic, Bounce, Informality and Spacing (letters can be made to sit unevenly, or animate); Playpen Sans uses contextual alternates to vary repeated letters like handwriting. [recall] — [Shantell Sans](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Shantell+Sans), [Playpen Sans](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Playpen+Sans)
- Duolingo uses a custom rounded display face (Feather Bold) with DIN Next Rounded for body; Headspace's 2024 identity has a custom typeface by Colophon. [recall for Duolingo; snippet for Headspace] — [Duolingo typography](https://design.duolingo.com/identity/typography), [Standards: Headspace](https://standards.site/examples/headspace)
- Sizes: Apple's iOS default body size is 17 pt with 11 pt as the smallest recommended; Material's body styles are 16 and 14 sp with 11 sp the smallest label. [recall] — [Apple HIG: Typography](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography), [M3 type scale](https://m3.material.io/styles/typography/type-scale-tokens)
- iOS Safari zooms the page when a focused form field's text is under 16 px, so 16 px is the floor for inputs. [recall; a search for a current source returned nothing] — [CSS-Tricks: 16px or larger text prevents iOS form zoom](https://css-tricks.com/16px-or-larger-text-prevents-ios-form-zoom/)
- Touch targets: WCAG 2.2 SC 2.5.8 (AA) requires 24×24 CSS px or equivalent spacing; platform guidance is larger, Apple 44×44 pt and Material 48×48 dp. [snippet for WCAG; platform numbers from a third-party summary plus recall] — [W3C: Understanding 2.5.8](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html), [Apple HIG: Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility)
- WCAG counts text as "large" (3:1 contrast instead of 4.5:1) from 18 pt, or 14 pt bold: about 24 px and 18.7 px. [recall] — [W3C: Understanding 1.4.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html)
- Aligned digits: `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums` selects fixed-width figures where the font has them, so changing numbers do not jitter. [recall] — [MDN: font-variant-numeric](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/font-variant-numeric)
- Icon consistency in practice: Duolingo repeats a few shapes (rounded rectangle, circle, rounded triangle); Wilmot's Warehouse's hundreds of icons came from one artist's hand. [snippet] — [Duolingo characters guidance](https://design.duolingo.com/illustration/characters), [Finji](https://finji.co/news/2019/08/29/Wilmot's-Thoughts.html)
- Animal Crossing's use of universal symbols is cited as why its icons read instantly. [snippet] — [UX Collective](https://uxdesign.cc/animal-crossing-delight-by-design-e2c0a1a4d462)

### Inferences
- A two-tier system fits the evidence: a characterful display face (Fredoka, Baloo 2, or a hand face such as Shantell Sans) only for titles, buttons and big numbers at roughly 20 px and above, and a sturdy rounded text face (Nunito or similar) at 16 px or more for anything read in sentences. Decorative hand lettering should be reserved for a few words at a time.
- Heavy rounded display weights at around 19–24 px also qualify as WCAG "large text", which eases contrast on coloured paper.
- For numbers and dense data: use the text face rather than the display face, tabular figures, right alignment, generous line height, and put figures on a plain flat "label" sheet rather than on grain. Check each candidate font actually ships tabular figures; this was not verified for any of the fonts above.
- For a cut-paper icon set, consistency rules that follow from the art direction: silhouettes only (no outlines), one corner radius family, at most two or three stacked sheets per icon, one shadow direction, a shared grid, and a text label wherever meaning is not universal.
- Hand-style variable fonts with a bounce axis offer cheap handmade character in headings without images; any animation of that axis should stop under reduced motion.

- No authoritative legibility testing of rounded versus plain sans at small sizes was found; the font comparisons come from low-quality blogs.
- Nothing was found on how cute games keep numbers and dense data readable; the advice above is inference.

## 5. Web implementation: paper textures, wobbly edges, shadows, animation tooling and their cost on phones

### Takeaway
Everything needed is in the platform by late 2026 (`linear()` springs, same-document View Transitions, CSS masks, SVG filters), but live SVG filters, `filter: drop-shadow()` and blur are the known mobile hazards, especially in Safari. The safe pattern is to use filters to generate texture and torn edges once (or bake them into images) and animate only transform and opacity. No controlled mid-range-phone benchmarks were found for any of these techniques.

### Cited Findings
**SVG filters for grain and rough edges**
- `feTurbulence` generates noise; fed into `feDisplacementMap` it displaces an element's edges into a wobbly or torn outline. Long-standing support, including iOS Safari since version 6. [snippet] — [feDisplacementMap reference](https://docs.w3cub.com/svg/reference/element/fedisplacementmap), [Smashing Magazine deep dive, Sept 2021 (not read)](https://www.smashingmagazine.com/2021/09/deep-dive-wonderful-world-svg-displacement-filtering/)
- Cost scales with octaves: more `numOctaves` means more calculation; authors typically use about 1–5 and say more is not worth it. [snippet] — [CSS-Tricks: Grainy Gradients, 2021](https://css-tricks.com/?p=335760), [Frontend Masters: Grainy Gradients (mirror)](https://chanhi2000.github.io/bookshelf/frontendmasters.com/grainy-gradients.html)
- Static-grain recipe: embed the `feTurbulence` SVG as a data URI in CSS, flatten it to grey, layer at low opacity for a printed-paper feel; keep the defining SVG in a zero-size hidden element. Warning that Safari sometimes struggles with complex SVG filters layered over heavy CSS shadows. [snippet; short blog post] — [loke.dev: SVG grain textures and performance](https://loke-dev-lokedevs-projects.vercel.app/blog/svg-grain-textures-performance)
- Animated turbulence or displacement is the risky case: a 2022 GSAP forum report found it smooth on desktop but stuttering on mobile, with iOS Safari reloading the tab or dropping the effect on long runs; a reply called SVG filters "pretty horrific for performance". Short bursts or small areas were workable. [snippet; anecdote] — [GSAP forum, 2022](https://greensock.com/forums/topic/33075-gsap-and-feturbulence-mobile-performance/)
- Firefox: `feDisplacementMap` is not GPU-accelerated and falls to a slower software path. [snippet; one of two Bugzilla entries in the results] — [Mozilla bug 1972363](https://bugzilla.mozilla.org/show_bug.cgi?id=1972363), [bug 1883933](https://bugzilla.mozilla.org/show_bug.cgi?id=1883933)
- Safari rendering bugs on record: viewport-dependent tiling or gaps when applying SVG filters; a displacement filter failing to render on text in Safari 16. [snippet] — [WebKit bug 266295](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=266295), [WebKit bug 245510](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=245510)

**Masks, 9-slice and hand-drawn generators**
- An SVG `<mask>` or mask image can be applied with CSS `mask`; SVG masks default to luminance (darker is more transparent), which matters when drawing a torn shape in black and white. [snippet] — [ICS Media: masking techniques](https://ics.media/en/entry/210701/), [Brian Treese: CSS masks guide](https://briantree.se/using-css-masks-to-solve-common-design-problems)
- A browser tool generates irregular torn-edge SVG paths, usable as a reference or as mask geometry. [snippet] — [SVG torn paper edge generator](https://bushe.co/tools/svg-torn-paper-edge-generator/)
- CSS `mask-image` is unprefixed in all engines since Chromium 120 (Dec 2023). [recall] — [MDN: mask-image](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/mask-image)
- `border-image` with `border-image-slice` (and `fill`) gives 9-slice scaling of one decorated frame image, so a torn or deckled border stretches to any panel size; long supported everywhere. [recall] — [MDN: border-image-slice](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/border-image-slice)
- Rough.js draws sketchy, hand-drawn shapes to Canvas or SVG in under 9 kB gzipped, with `roughness`, `bowing`, fill styles and a `seed` option for repeatable output. [recall] — [roughjs.com](https://roughjs.com/), [GitHub](https://github.com/rough-stuff/rough)

**Shadows and blur**
- `filter: drop-shadow()` follows the element's alpha shape (needed for irregular cut-outs), whereas `box-shadow` follows the box and its border radius. [recall] — [CSS-Tricks: box-shadow vs. drop-shadow](https://css-tricks.com/breaking-css-box-shadow-vs-drop-shadow/)
- Shopware's engineering record (Nov 2022) reports "drastic performance issues" from `drop-shadow` in Safari and replaced it with `box-shadow`, which "looks almost the same and is much faster". [snippet] — [Shopware ADR](https://developer.shopware.com/docs/resources/references/adr/2022-21-11-replace-drop-shadow-with-box-shadow)
- Animating a `filter` on mobile is significantly more expensive than animating `box-shadow`; guides disagree on whether `drop-shadow` is GPU-accelerated by default. [snippet] — [CSS shadow guide](https://cssgenerators-dev.pages.dev/blog/css-shadow-guide)
- `backdrop-filter` blur is the costliest effect because the backdrop is re-sampled on every paint; common radii are 8–16 px and larger radii cost more on low-end Android. Unprefixed in Safari 18; older Safari needs `-webkit-backdrop-filter`. [snippet] — [backdrop-filter recipes, 2026](https://www.buildmvpfast.com/blog/liquid-glass-css-backdrop-filter-recipes-2026), [support guide](https://www.testmuai.com/learning-hub/css-backdrop-filter-browser-support/)
- Animations of `transform` and `opacity` can run on the compositor without layout or paint; other properties generally cannot. [recall] — [web.dev: high-performance CSS animations](https://web.dev/articles/animations-guide)

**Easing and transitions**
- CSS `linear()` easing: Chrome and Edge 113, Firefox 112, Safari 17.2; Baseline newly available 11 Dec 2023, due to become widely available 11 June 2026. It approximates spring and bounce curves that `cubic-bezier()` cannot; unsupported browsers ignore it, so declare a `cubic-bezier` first. A generator offers spring, bounce, elastic and Material-emphasized presets. [snippet] — [Chrome for Developers: linear() easing](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/css-ui/css-linear-easing-function), [web-features explorer](https://web-platform-dx.github.io/web-features-explorer/features/linear-easing)
- View Transitions, same-document (`document.startViewTransition`): Chrome and Edge 111, Safari 18, Firefox 144; Baseline newly available October 2025. Interop 2026 lists it as a focus area for edge cases. [snippet] — [web.dev, 16 Oct 2025](https://web.dev/blog/same-document-view-transitions-are-now-baseline-newly-available), [Mozilla Hacks: Interop 2026](https://hacks.mozilla.org/2026/02/launching-interop-2026/)
- View Transitions, cross-document: Chrome and Edge 126, Safari 18.2, not in Firefox per 2026 sources. Transition types reportedly in Firefox 147; nested groups and element-scoped transitions reportedly Chrome-only (both unverified). [snippet] — [caniuse](https://caniuse.com/cross-document-view-transitions), [Chrome: view transitions in 2025](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/view-transitions-in-2025), [Botmonster 2026 overview](https://botmonster.com/web-dev/smooth-page-animations-with-the-view-transitions-api/)

**Animation tooling**
- Rive: vendor says `.riv` files are often 10–15 times smaller than equivalent Lottie JSON (example 240 kB to 16 kB); Callstack cites a Rive CEO example of about 24 kB to 2 kB; Intercom reported sizes roughly halved. No independent benchmark of frame time, memory or start-up was found. [snippet] — [Rive: Rive as a Lottie alternative](https://rive.app/blog/rive-as-a-lottie-alternative), [Callstack](https://callstack.com/blog/lottie-vs-rive-optimizing-mobile-app-animation), [Rive: Intercom case](https://rive.app/blog/intercom-s-product-animation-evolution-embracing-rive)
- Rive's own best-practice notes: poorly optimised files perform badly on low-end devices; cache the `.riv` so it is parsed and decoded once. [snippet] — [Rive docs](https://rive.app/docs/getting-started/best-practices.md)
- Lottie JSON is parsed at runtime and cost depends on complexity; dotLottie (`.lottie`) is a zipped container of the JSON and assets. Rive suits interactive, stateful pieces; Lottie suits playback of After Effects exports. [snippet] — [LottieFiles: LottieFiles or Rive](https://lottiefiles.com/blog/working-with-lottie-animations/lottiefiles-or-rive)
- Both Rive's web runtimes and the dotLottie web player draw to a `<canvas>` using WebAssembly renderers. [recall] — [Rive web runtime docs](https://rive.app/docs/runtimes/web/web-js), [dotlottie-web](https://github.com/LottieFiles/dotlottie-web)
- Motion (the successor to Framer Motion and Motion One, framework-independent since late 2024) offers a small `animate()` built on the Web Animations API with springs; GSAP has been free including all plugins since April 2025. [recall] — [motion.dev](https://motion.dev/), [gsap.com](https://gsap.com/)

**Fonts**
- Font-loading practice: WOFF2, subsetting, self-hosting or preloading the one or two critical files, an explicit `font-display`, and fallback metric overrides (`size-adjust`, `ascent-override`) to limit layout shift. [recall] — [web.dev: font best practices](https://web.dev/articles/font-best-practices)

### Inferences
- Recommended paper recipe from the above: (1) grain as one small tiling image (or a filter result rasterised once) under text-bearing sheets at low contrast, not a live full-screen filter; (2) torn or wobbly edges as pre-made SVG paths, masks, or 9-slice frames, with a few variants per component so repeats are not identical; (3) paper-cut depth from stacked `box-shadow` layers on rounded shapes (a tight dark contact shadow plus a wider soft one), with `drop-shadow()` kept for small, static, irregular cut-outs; (4) never animate a filter, blur or shadow — fake a lifting sheet by cross-fading the opacity of a second, pre-rendered shadow layer while moving the sheet with `transform`.
- "Wobble" can be had without live filters by stepping between two or three pre-generated edge variants at a low frame rate (a "line boil"), or by tiny rotations of a degree or two; both are transform or opacity changes only.
- Avoid `backdrop-filter` and blend modes over the WebGL world canvas on mid-range phones: every world frame would force the overlay to be recomposited. Opaque paper panels suit both the art direction and the performance budget.
- Use `linear()` springs in plain CSS for most UI motion with a `cubic-bezier` fallback; reach for a library only for gesture-driven, interruptible springs. Same-document View Transitions are usable in all engines for sheet and screen changes, but treat them as an enhancement (older Firefox and Safari below 18 simply switch instantly).
- Rive is the natural fit for a few interactive hero pieces (a mascot, a reward), but each instance is another canvas next to PixiJS; simple squash, pop and flutter effects are cheaper in CSS, and anything living inside the world should be PixiJS sprites.
- Web fonts drawn into the PixiJS canvas must be loaded before first draw or text renders in a fallback face; DOM text does not have that problem.

### Gaps
- No controlled measurements on mid-range phones for any technique here (SVG filter area versus frame time, `drop-shadow` count, Rive versus Lottie runtime). The performance guidance is anecdote plus engine bug reports; it needs testing on real devices, particularly an older iPhone and a mid-range Android.
- Whether the WebKit SVG-filter bugs and the Safari `drop-shadow` slowness persist in current Safari was not checked.
- Rive and dotLottie web runtime download sizes were not retrieved.
- Exact Firefox status for cross-document View Transitions, transition types and nested groups in November 2026 is unverified.
- No sourced tutorial for torn or deckled paper edges in CSS/SVG was found (the search returned Photoshop guides); the Smashing and Codrops articles that likely cover it could not be opened.
- No source was found on the cost of DOM overlays (filters, blend modes) composited above a WebGL canvas; that point is inference.

## 6. Keeping handmade interfaces accessible

### Takeaway
WCAG treats text on a textured or pictorial background as a contrast test against the worst-case part of the background, so paper grain must stay faint behind text or text must sit on a flat backing; motion triggered by interaction should be switchable off (WCAG 2.3.3, via `prefers-reduced-motion`), and ambient looping motion needs its own pause. No guidance specific to textured game UI from NN/g or others was found.

### Cited Findings
- WCAG failure F83: using background images that do not give sufficient contrast with foreground text. Contrast must hold against the parts of the image "most like the text and behind the text"; applies to 1.4.3 (AA) and 1.4.6 (AAA). [snippet] — [W3C technique F83](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/failures/F83)
- Thresholds: 4.5:1 for normal text and 3:1 for large text at AA; 7:1 and 4.5:1 at AAA. [snippet, secondary; consistent with the spec] — [W3C: Understanding 1.4.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html)
- Fixes named in guidance: a solid or sufficiently opaque backing or scrim between image and text, checked at every point across the text block, or a contrasting halo around the letters (W3C G17/G18). [snippet] — [W3C technique G17](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG-TECHS/G17.html), [ADA Compliance Pros](https://www.adacompliancepros.com/wcag-guides/text-color-contrast-wcag-aa)
- Non-text contrast (1.4.11, AA): the visual parts needed to identify controls, their states, and meaningful graphics need 3:1 against adjacent colours. [recall] — [W3C: Understanding 1.4.11](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/non-text-contrast.html)
- WCAG 2.3.3 Animation from Interactions (AAA, added in 2.1): motion animation triggered by interaction can be disabled unless essential; some users experience distraction or nausea. Sufficient technique C39 uses the `prefers-reduced-motion` media query. [snippet] — [W3C: Understanding 2.3.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Understanding/animation-from-interactions), [W3C technique C39](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/css/C39.html)
- `prefers-reduced-motion` does not cover auto-playing or looping content, which still needs a pause, stop or hide control under 2.2.2 (when it starts automatically, lasts over five seconds and sits beside other content). Script-driven libraries must check the preference themselves. [snippet, with the 2.2.2 conditions from recall] — [OpenReplay: accessible animation](https://blog.openreplay.com/prefers-reduced-motion-accessible-animation/), [W3C: Understanding 2.2.2](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pause-stop-hide.html)
- web.dev's guidance is to reduce rather than necessarily remove motion: keep opacity and colour changes, drop large movement, scaling and parallax. [recall; the article did not appear in search results] — [web.dev: prefers-reduced-motion](https://web.dev/articles/prefers-reduced-motion)
- Colour must not be the only way information is conveyed (1.4.1, A). About 1 in 12 men and 1 in 200 women have some colour vision deficiency. [recall] — [W3C: Understanding 1.4.1](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/use-of-color.html), [Colour Blind Awareness](https://www.colourblindawareness.org/colour-blindness/)
- Text must be resizable to 200% without loss of content or function (1.4.4, AA). [recall] — [W3C: Understanding 1.4.4](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/resize-text.html)
- Pointer targets at least 24×24 CSS px or equivalently spaced (2.5.8, AA, WCAG 2.2). [snippet] — [W3C: Understanding 2.5.8](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html)
- Apple's Motion guidance independently says to make motion optional. [recall] — [HIG: Motion](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion)

### Inferences
- Texture rule: measure contrast against the darkest and lightest pixels of the grain actually behind the text, not the sheet's average colour. In practice keep grain to a few percent of luminance variation on text-bearing sheets, or seat text on a flat "label" patch; let stronger texture live on borders, backs of sheets and decorative areas.
- Cut-paper depth comes from shadow, which is low contrast by nature. Control boundaries and states (selected, disabled) need a 3:1 cue of their own: a darker sheet colour, a distinct shape or a tab, not shadow or hue alone.
- Reduced-motion mode for this style: keep instant or cross-fade state changes and short opacity transitions; remove squash, overshoot, bounce, idle wobble or boil, parallax between paper layers and any shake. Offer an in-game switch as well as honouring the system setting, since many players never find the OS option, and apply the same switch to the PixiJS world's ambient motion.
- Idle "alive" motion (breathing sheets, fluttering corners) falls under the pause rule if it runs longer than five seconds beside readable content; keep it brief, occasional, or switchable.
- Colour-blind safety for a limited paper palette: pair every colour-coded meaning with a shape, icon or label, and vary lightness as well as hue between meaningful colours.
- Text scaling: size text in relative units, let sheets grow with their content, never disable pinch zoom, and test the layout with text at 200%; fixed-size "cards" with baked-in text are the common failure.

### Gaps
- No Nielsen Norman Group or games-specific guidance on textured backgrounds was found; the only authority is WCAG.
- How iOS Safari and Android Chrome apply system text-size settings to web pages in late 2026 was not researched.
- WCAG 3 and its APCA contrast method (which treats font weight and size differently, relevant to heavy rounded type) were not checked for status.
- No source was found on colour-blind-safe palette construction for limited or pastel palettes; game accessibility guideline sites (Game Accessibility Guidelines, Xbox Accessibility Guidelines) were not consulted.
- No evidence was found on whether rounded or hand-drawn typefaces affect readability for dyslexic or low-vision readers.
