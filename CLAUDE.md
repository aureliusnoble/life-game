# life-game

A mobile-first online creature/ecosystem game for a small group of friends. The project was restarted from scratch in November 2026 and is moving from design exploration into planning.

- `archive/` holds the superseded first version. Ignore it: do not read it for guidance, build on it, or run its tooling unless explicitly asked.
- `reports/` holds research reports; `research_notes/` holds the source notes behind each report.
- `design/` holds the current design proposals. Where a design doc and a report disagree, the design doc is the later thinking.
- `design/mockups/` holds visual studies published as pages. They are explorations, not game code. `watch-it-think.html` is the behaviour editor on a phone, with a toy simulation of its own. `loop-wireframes.html` holds wireframes of the four player loops: layout and words only. `tending.html` is a concept test of pet play with no simulation behind it. `since-yesterday.html` is a concept test of feedback, with a toy simulation of its own. `basin-ages.html` is a concept test of playing with friends; its history is made up by a small model in the page. `body-language.html`, `eight-places.html` and `interface-materials.html` are studies for the look: one creature in every act, state and bearing; the eight biomes in cut paper; and three materials for the interface. They are drawn in code and stand in for drawn art.
- `design/reviews/` holds independent reviewers' reports on the look, as records: a first round of five and a second round. `design/assets.md` lists the assets the world needs, general and by biome, and what exists. `design/performance.md` says what runs on the server and what on the phone.
- `design/checks/` holds small scripts that test claims made in the design docs. They are stand-ins, not the game's code; run with `node design/checks/moodsim.js [scenario]` (recipes), `node design/checks/verbaudit.js [scenario]` (whether each verb differs from its neighbour), `node design/checks/bodyaudit.js [scenario]` (whether each body part changes outcomes) `node design/checks/envaudit.js [scenario]` (whether each feature of the world changes who thrives) or `node design/checks/worldaudit.js [scenario]` (the six habitat patches, recovery after overuse, and ways of living through the year). `node design/checks/socialaudit.js [scenario]` runs direction checks on the social blocks. `node design/checks/castaudit.js [scenario]` tests whether hunters and their prey last together (`roomy` is slow and runs only when named). The balance lab, `node design/checks/balancelab.js check | search <habitat> | report | knockout <habitat> | fading`, searches for designs that win and reports options that are always or never chosen; its results are kept in `design/checks/balance/`, and `design/plan.md` section 3 says how balance is tested. `node design/checks/simcost.js [creatures]` and `node design/checks/framecost.js` (needs Playwright) are the two measurements behind `design/performance.md`: a stand-in for the simulation's cost, and where a frame's time goes in the paper study.
- Every creature in the paper study comes from one kit: a creature is a row of choices, each kind of part is an entry in a table, and states and bearings are rows of numbers for a posture every body has. `design/look.md` sections 17 and 18 hold the rules each review settled and how each system is added to. Keep new content in that form: a new row or a new entry, not a new drawing routine.
- The paper study is a mockup, and it is closed for now: its job was to prove the systems (the kit, the posture, states, acts, assets, places, weather). Six places are built in it: the pond, dunes, marsh, highlands, woodland and a lakeshore. The full pass over the other biomes, seasons, night and every state comes during the build; `design/plan.md` section 7 lists what was settled and what waits. Do not broaden the study further unless asked.
- The body designer and the look are fitted to each other in both directions: the kit must be able to draw every part the rules have, and the parts and choices must be clear and wide enough to make creatures far beyond the presets. One species sheet is read by the rules, the drawing and the designer. This is area 13 in `design/plan.md`, and it is the next piece of design work.
- `design/sheet/` holds the species sheet: `sheet.js` is the one list of every choice about a body, with what each does and how it is seen; `designer.template.html` and `make.js` build `design/mockups/body-designer.html` from the sheet and from the kit, which is taken out of `paper-study.html` between its `<share>` marks. After changing the sheet, the template or the kit, run `node design/sheet/make.js`. `node design/checks/sheetaudit.js [cover | parts | seen | random | variety | presets | quiz | all]` (needs Playwright) checks that the sheet and the kit fit each other in both directions; its pictures go to `design/checks/sheet/`. A part is not finished until it is on the sheet, drawn by the kit and passes `cover` and `seen`.
- A dead body keeps its eyes, as crosses.
- The look is developed depth first: `design/mockups/paper-study.html` is the depth study, reviewed each round from screenshots by an independent agent. The owner called it good enough for the mockup stage on 16 November; the art pass during the build begins with a third review. The interface's material is the scrapbook.
- The world is seen from above, with creatures turned to where they are heading. The front-on view of a creature is only for close-ups: tending, portraits and the card for one creature.
- The interface gets a UI pass (`design/plan.md` section 4): independent reviewers, each a fresh Fable 5.2 agent and no other model, comment over at least three rounds and propose small changes and other layouts; the main thread accepts or rejects each proposal and records why. Reports go in `design/reviews/`.
- Progress through what is left of the design stage is kept in the table at the end of `design/plan.md` section 8. Update it as each step is done.
- The wireframes record what each screen is for and what it says, and nothing about layout or look. When the interface is designed for real, start from what each moment should feel like and think it through carefully; do not dress the wireframes.
- Before balance there is a mechanics pass (`design/plan.md` section 3): every body part and every part of a brain has to matter in play and be worth choosing in some setting, and every complex behaviour the design promises has to be written in the vocabulary and run. It need not be balanced. It comes after the species sheet and before the screens.
- Balance is finalised at the end of the design process. Until then the checks are for direction and for catching broken tests, not for tuning numbers.
- `design/plan.md` is the plan: where the project stands, the decisions made so far, the design areas in order with what each must answer, what is left of the design stage, and the build in seven phases. Work goes into the main branch as reviewed changes.
