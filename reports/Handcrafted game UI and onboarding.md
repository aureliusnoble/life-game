# Open on the creature, teach by doing

The interface should open on the living world with the player's named founder already on screen, keep every tool in opaque cut-paper sheets that rise from the bottom of a portrait screen, work by tap first with drag as a faster layer on top, show the bare "notice → act" sentence as the whole first layer of the behaviour editor, and teach through a guided first build in the real editor in which a few short callouts each prompt one real action. Comparable games support this shape from both sides: the check-in games that are praised (Neko Atsume, Pikmin Bloom, Finch) open on the scene and keep care a tap or two away, while the criticised ones bury it under confirmation chains, per-need alerts and small targets. Progressive disclosure is worth using for everything a player can ask for on the spot, but it is a different thing from unlocking, and the documented costs fall on unlocking: Super Mario Maker's calendar-gated tools were patched on day one, and the recorded complaint about Final Fantasy XII's rule lists is the long wait for enough rule words, not the format. The phone browser of November 2026 sets hard limits (no dependable haptics on iPhone, no fullscreen or orientation lock there, a silent switch that mutes web audio, edge swipes a page cannot block, an install path of about five taps), so the game has to be complete in a Safari tab and merely nicer when installed. The evidence is uneven, and none of it was read at the source: page fetches were blocked for every researcher, so every citation below rests on a search snippet or on recalled knowledge, and is marked as such. The recommendations on the firmest ground are those backed by accessibility standards and platform guidelines (target sizes, a non-drag path for every drag, contrast on textured paper), by controlled studies of tutorials and by first-hand designer accounts. The weakest are the numeric limits on callouts (vendor data), every performance claim about paper effects on phones (no benchmark was found), the advice on notifications and streaks (blogs), and the display of strength-blended instincts, for which no precedent surfaced at all and which therefore needs a prototype more than it needs more reading.

## No page was opened, so every figure here is a lead

Five researchers ran roughly 130 web searches on 15 November 2026, and every attempt to open a page failed because the hosts sat outside the session's network allowlist. Nothing below was read at its source. A plain citation in this report therefore means only that a search-result snippet said this about the linked page; wording and figures are approximate until someone opens the page. Two weaker kinds of statement are marked where they occur. **Attribution uncertain** means the snippet text is real but the search summary did not tie it to one page, so the link is the likeliest candidate. **Recalled** means a researcher supplied the point from memory of the linked page and saw nothing of it this session. Recalled items appear only where a recommendation leans on them; the rest were left out.

Recommendations are graded by what they rest on. **Firm** means a published standard or platform guideline, a peer-reviewed study or a first-hand developer account, with at least two sources pointing the same way. **Moderate** means one such source, or a consistent pattern across product examples and secondary reports. **Weak** means vendor benchmarks, blogs, forum anecdotes, recalled knowledge, or reasoning that no source tests. Even "firm" is firm about direction only, because the pages were not opened.

Several things the question asks about were not found at all, and silence on them is a gap, not a finding. No measured study of thumb reach on today's 6.5 to 6.9 inch phones surfaced; the research base runs from 2013 to 2019. The notes contain no benchmark for HTML panels over a WebGL canvas, or for paper effects such as filters and shadows, on a mid-range phone. No study compares sentence-like blocks with node graphs for novices, gives a tested number of new elements per session, or measures callouts in games or in phone browsers. No source shows how any game presents a large multi-biome world in portrait. The Safari 27 release notes were not read, so the state of Home Screen web apps, haptics and fullscreen on iPhone in November 2026 rests on older sources and one unverified third-party table.

## The praised check-in games open on the living scene

The check-in games that critics praise put the living place on the first screen and very little in front of it. Neko Atsume opens on its yard; the whole menu hangs off one paw icon, a centre control always returns to the yard, and actions take two taps, a structure one write-up credits with making the game playable without reading its language ([GameSkinny](https://www.gameskinny.com/tips/how-to-play-neko-atsume-with-no-japanese-knowledge/), [Medium](https://medium.com/@strawburrymiwk/a-ui-so-good-it-breaks-language-barriers-83b6e11ae4d2)). Pikmin Bloom's fan wiki says the game "makes little distinction between menu screen and gameplay" ([Pikipedia](https://www.pikminwiki.com/Pikmin_Bloom_interface)). The cautionary case is Pokémon Sleep: one outlet calls its interface "fantastic", yet players complain of waiting through menus every morning, and a review digest quotes "Loading screens for loading screens just to hit ok" ([TechRadar](https://techradar.com/gaming/my-sleep-has-gotten-worse-since-playing-pokemon-sleep-but-i-still-wont-stop); [GosuGamers](https://gosugamers.net/entertainment/features/69058-pokemon-sleep-impressions-why-catch-pokemon-when-you-can-catch-some-zs), attribution uncertain; [Marlvel](https://marlvel.ai/apps/jp-pokemon-pokemonsleep), a machine-generated digest to weigh lightly). Good looks did not save a queue of confirmations. For a fifteen-minute daily visit the measure that matters is **the number of screens between opening the game and touching the creature**, and the researchers' proposed ceiling of one is the right budget, although only Neko Atsume's tap count was actually sourced.

The strongest borrowable pattern in the set is Pikmin Bloom's lookback. Once a day, on the first open after a set hour (9 pm by default), it plays a short animation of the day drawn on the map with a handful of totals; past lookbacks replay from a log, and a larger weekly edition follows on Sundays ([Niantic help centre](https://niantic.helpshift.com/hc/en/23-pikmin-bloom/faq/2859-daily-lookback), [Pikipedia](https://www.pikminwiki.com/Lifelog), [GoNintendo](https://gonintendo.com/contents/14208-pikmin-bloom-gets-weekly-lookback-feature)). On PC, Thrive does the equivalent for an ecosystem: a report with a timeline of extinctions, then a map, then the editor ([Thrive wiki](https://thrive.fandom.com/wiki/Talk:Microbe_Editor/@comment-26147399-20190128003421?oldid=6468), an old page; [Revolutionary Games](https://www.revolutionarygamesstudio.com/progress-update-05-07-2022), attribution uncertain). Idle-game writing calls the welcome-back screen one of the genre's most satisfying moments, and players resent one thing about it, a loading bar that turns the recap into a gate ([Game Bootcamp](https://seanking11.github.io/gamebootcamp/modules/34-idle-incremental), attribution uncertain; [Warzone Idle forum](https://www.warzone.com/Forum/581561-purpose-loading-bar-welcome-back-popup)). This game should take the lookback whole: a daily one for the check-in and a weekly one that opens the design session in Thrive's order of what happened, where, and what to change. Two facts about this game sharpen the borrowing. A day here lasts about one real hour, so a player returning after a real day has missed some twenty-four game days, and the recap is always a compression, never a log. And species are designed to fade within three to seven days, so bad news is routine; a games-industry essay warns against greeting a returning player with a list of everything that went wrong ([GamesBrief](https://gamesbrief.com/?p=11267)), which argues for leading with the founder and one thing to do now, the one-task-at-a-time presentation that a 2026 critique credits with reducing overload in Finch ([Pratt IxD critique](https://ixd.prattsi.org/2026/02/design-critique-finch-self-care-pet-ios-app/)).

For the body editor Spore remains the reference, and its lessons cut both ways. Its designers held to one click per action, revealed features gradually, named the kinds of part in the building editor after an early prototype with "no apparent structure" failed, and made sure any creature worked and moved; Chaim Gingold called the goal a "magic crayon", simple to use yet powerful enough to make something its user values ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/gingold-talks-i-spore-i-s-magic-crayon-approach); [GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/gdc-07-creating-spore/1100-6167215/), attribution uncertain; [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2007/03/12/spores-power-struggle-freedom-vs-beauty/)). The Nintendo DS version shows the editor survived reduction to drag, resize and rotate on a small touch screen ([Vooks](https://vooks.net/spore-creatures-nds)). The documented weakness is that a creature's attributes were simply the sum of its named parts, so that, in one player's words, "when you were done, it didn't actually make any difference" ([Soren Johnson](https://www.designer-notes.com/?p=654); [Straight Dope](https://boards.straightdope.com/t/spore-2008/915281), attribution uncertain). Spore also carried two limits, a points budget and a separate complexity meter, and players routinely overrode the second ([Giant Bomb user review](https://www.giantbomb.com/spore-creature-creator/3030-20746/user-reviews/2200-5254/) and [Spore Wiki](https://spore.fandom.com/wiki/Creature_Creator), both attribution uncertain). This game's body design already answers the first criticism by deriving numbers from shape, size and placement; the interface's job is to show those numbers moving while the finger is still down. The second is a live warning, because the current behaviour design keeps a readability cap beside the shared budget (`design/neural-blocks.md`, section 18). That cap reads better as the physical size of the page, a sheet of rows that fills up, than as a second meter. Thrive adds the piece Spore lacked, a panel inside the editor that predicts the species' population under the current edits ([Thrive v0.5.6 release notes](https://github.com/Revolutionary-Games/Thrive/releases/tag/v0.5.6)); the test tank planned for the top half of the designer is the same idea made visible.

For inspecting a living creature the games split into two families, and only one fits a phone. Instrument panels are powerful and repeatedly described as hard to read. Creatures needed several kits, its wiki has to warn that "Norns can be perfectly healthy, yet still close to death!", and fans built a tool to merge three monitoring kits into one ([Creatures Wiki](https://creatures.fandom.com/wiki/Observation_Kit), [PowerKit](https://creatures.fandom.com/wiki/PowerKit)); The Bibites had to patch its brain panel "getting out of screen for certain ui scales" ([The Bibites hotfix notes](https://thebibites.itch.io/the-bibites/devlog/811452/the-bibites-0601-hotfix)). Field-guide patterns show a few plain facts on the animal and file the rest: Viva Piñata lists each animal's requirements at the press of one button, and Bugsnax draws a scanned creature's route on the world and stores quick facts in a journal ([TrueAchievements](https://www.trueachievements.com/game/Viva-Pinata-Trouble-In-Paradise/walkthrough/3), [PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/bugsnax-guide)). That the second family reads better at phone size is the researchers' inference; no study of inspection styles on phones was found. The Creatures warning transfers directly to this game's vigour meter and fading forecast: a summary that says fine must mean fine.

For the behaviour editor the nearest precedents are ordered lists of "condition → action" rules. Final Fantasy XII's gambits were readily understood, described by one outlet as "almost like a simplified programming language", and research on smart-home rules found that inexperienced users quickly learned to write trigger-action programs; a press account of that study adds that **78% of the behaviours people asked for needed only one trigger and one action** ([Retronauts](https://retronauts.com/article/421/the-trials-tribulations-and-trial-mode-of-final-fantasy-xii-the-zodiac-age?amphtml=1), [Ur et al., CHI 2014](https://www.doi.org/10.1145/2556288.2557420), [Brown CS blog](https://blog.cs.brown.edu/2014/04/29/michael-littman-chi2014-programming-smart-home/)). What players resented was not the format. One writer did not enjoy Final Fantasy XII "until around the 15 hour mark", when more than the most basic gambits became available, and reviewers judged Dragon Age: Origins' copy weaker because the game "wasn't built around them" (single voices, with attribution uncertain among the pages surfaced: [GNG Games](https://gnggames.blogspot.com/2015/02/final-fantasy-xii-dragon-age-origins.html), [RPG Tourist](https://rpgtourist.blogspot.com/2022/09/exploring-tactical-systems-in-final.html), [RPGFan](https://www.rpgfan.com/review/dragon-age-origins-4/)). Gladiabots, the closest phone example, needed an update just to make node tooltips reachable on mobile, and its community keeps a wiki entry explaining how priority is evaluated ([App Store update notes](https://apps.apple.com/il/app/gladiabots/id1469623947), [community wiki](https://reddit.birdcat.cafe/r/Gladiabots/wiki/index)). The precedent runs out at one point that matters here: every game found settles conflicts by the order of the rules, whereas this game blends all instincts by strength and order has no effect. Showing which urges are pulling a creature, and how hard, is a new interface problem with nothing to borrow.

The complaints that recur across these games concern waiting and nagging more than looks: confirmation chains, a notification for every need (My Tamagotchi Forever was called "even more annoying than actual Tamagotchi"), red dots that never clear, touch targets too small even in a well-reviewed port, and collections that lean on the player's memory ([App Trigger](https://apptrigger.com/2018/03/19/my-tamagotchi-forever-annoying), [Braze](https://www.braze.com/resources/articles/beware-red-dot-badging.md), [TouchArcade](https://toucharcade.com/2023/03/29/terra-nil-mobile-review-netflix-graphics-performance-ipad-pro-iphone/), [Pratt IxD critique](https://ixd.prattsi.org/2026/02/design-critique-pikmin-bloom-iphone-app/)). The red-dot sources are marketing blogs about apps in general. A friends-only game with no shop escapes the currency complaints by construction.

## Handmade feel comes from material rules, and charm must ride on short paths

The best-documented handmade games share a method more than a look: the team handles the real material, builds the interface with the same tools as the world, and leaves construction visible. Tearaway's creative lead Rex Crowle said "even the UI was prototyped in paper", with icons "made using the same tools as the game", and that leaving the game "exposed rather than covering up how things were built" made it "more approachable" ([Pocket Gamer](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/unfolding-story-the-making-of-tearaway/), attribution uncertain; [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/how-i-tearaway-i-s-emphasis-on-imperfection-made-the-game-more-personal)). Good-Feel's developers were handed real yarn and cloth while making Kirby's Epic Yarn ([Iwata Asks](https://nintendo.co.uk/Iwata-Asks/Iwata-Asks-Kirby-s-Epic-Yarn/Iwata-Asks-Kirby-s-Epic-Yarn/1-Using-Real-Yarn-and-Cloth/1-Using-Real-Yarn-and-Cloth-206925.html)). Animal Crossing gives its menu a physical identity in the fiction, the NookPhone ([Nookipedia](https://nookipedia.com/wiki/NookPhone)). Designer commentary on interfaces specifically, as opposed to world art, is scarce: nothing citable surfaced for most of the games the researchers looked for, and the Tearaway talk most likely to state explicit rules could not be watched. For cut paper the translation is a single test applied to every panel, button and icon: could scissors and glue make this? That admits flat sheets, cut silhouettes, stacking and a soft shadow from the world's one light, and it rules out gradients, bevels, glows and glass.

That rule does not trade against depth or speed, because three independent constraints point to the same place. On performance, `backdrop-filter` blur is the costliest effect to put over a canvas that redraws every frame, so opaque panels are the safe choice ([backdrop-filter guide](https://www.buildmvpfast.com/blog/liquid-glass-css-backdrop-filter-recipes-2026), [Babylon.js forum](https://forum.babylonjs.com/t/any-tricks-for-reducing-photodome-cpu-usage/18495)). On legibility, WCAG's failure F83 tests text contrast against the worst-case part of a textured background, so grain has to stay faint behind text or text has to sit on a flat label ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/failures/F83)). On clarity, Into the Breach's designers "would sacrifice cool ideas for the sake of clarity every time" and limited what a single move could do so that its whole effect could be shown ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/-i-into-the-breach-i-dev-on-ui-design-sacrifice-cool-ideas-for-the-sake-of-clarity-every-time-), [Game Developer on Beastieball](https://gamedeveloper.com/art/deep-dive-developing-a-clear-and-consistent-visual-language-in-the-ui-of-beastieball)). **An opaque paper sheet with a flat label is at once the art direction, the cheap thing to render and the readable thing.** Depth then lives one tap below the cute surface, in the form Paradox uses in Crusader Kings III and Victoria 3: a highlighted term opens its explanation, and a number opens a breakdown of how it was reached ([Victoria 3 dev diary](https://forum.paradoxplaza.com/forum/developer-diary/victoria-3-dev-diary-29-user-experience.1506484/)). On touch, hover becomes a tap that opens a sheet, and one analysis suggests stopping at two or three levels ([Philip Ardeljan](https://philip.design/blog/tooltips-in-tooltips)).

Charm fails when it replaces short paths. Animal Crossing: New Horizons is criticised for delivering its pacing through repetitive menus, to the point that a faster tool ring is something the player buys ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/should-players-buy-their-own-ui-)). The same holds for motion. The "juice" canon says make everything respond, but a design note on juice in interfaces observes that a lot of it makes tasks take longer ([Brad Woods](https://garden.bradwoods.io/notes/design/juice)), and NN/g's guidance is 100 to 500 ms for most interface animation: about 100 ms for simple feedback, 200 to 300 ms for a substantial change such as a modal, exits quicker than entrances ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/animation-duration/)). Apple's guidance, recalled and not re-read, is to avoid adding motion to frequent interactions ([Apple HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion)). The working rule that follows is to scale the response inversely with frequency. A care action repeated every day gets a press of about 100 ms and a small squash; releasing a species or earning a first unlock gets the long, bouncy celebration. The specific deformation limits in the notes (within about 5%) come from low-authority guides.

Sight has to carry every signal, because the browser withholds the other two channels. Safari on iPhone does not support `navigator.vibrate()`; the one workaround is a side effect of a switch control that yields a single fixed tick, and a WebKit bug proposes restricting it ([Software Mansion](https://swmansion.com/blog/haptic-feedback-on-the-web-why-the-web-deliberately-refuses-to-be-as-tactile-as-native-apps), [web-haptics](https://www.mintlify.com/lochie/web-haptics/advanced/browser-support), [WebKit bug 285120](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=285120)). Web audio starts only after a user gesture, and the iPhone's silent switch mutes it unless the page opts into a playback audio session, an API implemented only in Safari ([Abratabia](https://www.abratabia.com/web-game-audio/mobile-audio-issues.php), [Bugnet](https://bugnet.io/blog/how-to-fix-ios-web-game-audio-muted-by-the-silent-switch), [WebKit bug 237322](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=237322)). The (Not Boring) apps, the standard example of an interface that plays like a game, override the mute switch natively because "the sound is the experience" ([RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/andy-allen-not-boring-software-launched-podcast-2026)); a web game cannot count on that. Sound and vibration are enrichment behind a visible switch.

A solo developer keeps a handmade set coherent the way large teams do, with a short written rule list. Duolingo's illustration guidance fixes a small shape vocabulary, a colour palette and one shadow convention (ground shadows are pills, not ovals, because ovals imply perspective), and its blog says the simplicity is what lets the team keep up with demand ([Duolingo design](https://design.duolingo.com/illustration), [Duolingo blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/shape-language-duolingos-art-style/)). Wilmot's Warehouse got the same coherence from one artist drawing every icon ([Finji](https://finji.co/news/2019/08/29/Wilmot's-Thoughts.html)). For type, the products studied pair a chunky rounded display face for titles with a calmer rounded face for sentences. The specific free candidates (Fredoka or Baloo 2 for display, Nunito for text) come from low-quality font blogs; no legibility comparison was found, and whether those fonts ship tabular figures was not checked ([Made Good Designs](https://madegooddesigns.com/nunito-alternatives/), [Inspo AI](https://www.inspoai.io/blogs/rounded-fonts)). Dense numbers belong in the text face on a flat label.

The web platform has what paper needs, with known hazards. Grain and torn edges can be generated with SVG filters, but animated filters stuttered on phones in one forum report, and `drop-shadow` caused "drastic performance issues" in Safari for one engineering team, which switched to `box-shadow` ([GSAP forum](https://greensock.com/forums/topic/33075-gsap-and-feturbulence-mobile-performance/), an anecdote; [Shopware](https://developer.shopware.com/docs/resources/references/adr/2022-21-11-replace-drop-shadow-with-box-shadow)). The researchers' recipe is therefore to bake grain and edges into images, masks or nine-slice frames, build depth from stacked box shadows, and animate only `transform` and `opacity` (the compositor point is recalled: [web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/animations-guide)). Spring-like easing is available in plain CSS through `linear()`, and same-document view transitions reached all engines in October 2025 ([Chrome for Developers](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/css-ui/css-linear-easing-function), [web.dev](https://web.dev/blog/same-document-view-transitions-are-now-baseline-newly-available)). No source benchmarked any of this on a mid-range phone, so it is a starting recipe to measure, not a result. Two accessibility rules bear specifically on paper. Shadow is low-contrast by nature, so selected and disabled states need a cue of their own (the 3:1 requirement for control boundaries is recalled: [W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/non-text-contrast.html)). And squash, overshoot and idle wobble should switch off under reduced motion, which WCAG asks for at its AAA level ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Understanding/animation-from-interactions)).

## Hide by tap, never by calendar: disclosure and unlocking carry different costs

Three mechanisms get lumped together as "revealing depth gradually", and the evidence treats them differently. Progressive disclosure proper keeps a feature one obvious tap away. NN/g defines it as deferring advanced or rarely used features to a secondary screen and credits it with better learnability, efficiency and error rates, on two conditions: the split between first and second layer is right, and the route to the second is obvious ([NN/g](https://nngroup.com/articles/progressive-disclosure/); the second condition is recalled). Staged disclosure is a fixed sequence everyone walks through, like a wizard, and suits steps that do not depend on each other ([NN/g](https://nngroup.com/articles/progressive-disclosure/)). Unlock-gating is the games' addition: the feature does not yet exist for this player. NN/g's warning that designs with more than two disclosure levels lose their users is recalled, not re-read. The useful subtlety is that the first layer teaches what matters: whatever sits on the editor's first screen is read as what the game is about.

Applied to the behaviour editor, the first layer is the bare sentence. The smart-home finding that most wanted behaviours need one trigger and one action supports it, and Apple's API designers state the principle in a form that fits a sentence editor exactly: the complexity of the expression should grow with the complexity of the use ([WWDC22](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2022/10059/?time=276)). So "see · plants → approach" is complete as written, and each further capability adds one visible piece. Conditions are the second layer. Strength is the hard case. The principle says tuning does not belong on the first screen, but the design's own testing found that a chase left at normal strength ambles (`design/neural-blocks.md`, section 17). Both are satisfied by showing strength as a small named chip on the row (weak, normal, strong) and never as a slider, so the row still reads as a sentence. With the row as level one and the slot sheet as level two, there is no room for a third; moods and signals should stay separate objects on the mood board, not deeper layers of a sentence. Error prevention comes from the slots themselves. ScratchJr's blocks are jigsaw pieces whose shapes match their syntax, so an invalid program cannot be assembled, and Google's Blockly guidance warns against suggesting interactions the system does not support; ScratchJr also cut Scratch's 100-plus blocks to about 30 for its young audience ([Designing ScratchJr](https://sites.bc.edu/devtech/wp-content/uploads/sites/181/2018/02/scratchjr_idc_2013.pdf), [Blockly](https://developers.google.com/blockly/guides/design/blocks?hl=en)). The vocabulary here runs to around ninety words, so the palette must always be filtered by slot, by body and by unlock state, which the design already specifies.

The costs are real, and they land on what is hidden badly or withheld too long. Hiding costs discovery: NN/g found discoverability "cut almost in half" when a site's main navigation was hidden, and a 2026 critique of Pikmin Bloom names discoverability as a hurdle for new players ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/videos/hamburger-menus/), [Pratt IxD critique](https://ixd.prattsi.org/2026/02/design-critique-pikmin-bloom-iphone-app/)). Depth that depends on hover degrades on other inputs: Crusader Kings III's console version put nested tooltips behind a mode that reviewers found weaker than the PC original, and Balatro's phone port needed a patch for tooltips that rendered off screen ([TheSixthAxis](https://thesixthaxis.com/2022/03/29/crusader-kings-3-console-review), [App Store notes](https://apps.apple.com/ie/app/balatro/id6502453075)). Withholding too much for too long starves the game, as the one account of Final Fantasy XII's first fifteen hours describes. Withholding by the calendar makes keen players wait: **Super Mario Maker shipped with its tools unlocking over nine days and replaced that with unlocking through use in a day-one patch**, reported as about fifteen minutes of building per batch ([GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/super-mario-maker-patch-removes-item-unlock-delay-now-15-minutes-not-1-day-per-batch/), [GamesRadar](https://gamesradar.com/super-mario-maker-update-unlocks), [Nintendo World Report](https://nintendoworldreport.com/editorial/40974/super-mario-makers-slow-unlocking-feels-more-frustrating-than-fun)). Nintendo kept the gradual reveal, on the reported reasoning that too many tools at once overwhelm some players, and changed only the trigger ([My Nintendo News](https://mynintendonews.com/2015/09/27/nintendo-explains-why-not-all-mario-maker-tools-were-available-at-start/), a secondary report). Marvel Snap shows the same curve on phones: its early, deterministic card unlocks draw little complaint and its late, slow, random ones draw most of it ([Marvel Snap Zone](https://marvelsnapzone.com/marvel-snap-progression-guide/), attribution uncertain). Two costs went unmeasured in everything found: how many features stay undiscovered, and how much extra tapping slows experts.

No source gives a tested number of new elements per session. What recurs in practice is one new element or small batch per five to fifteen minutes of active play, put to use at once: about one new plant per level in Plants vs. Zombies (that cadence is recalled), one concept per five-minute step in Game Builder Garage, a batch per fifteen minutes of building in the patched Mario Maker ([Nintendo](https://www.nintendo.com/us/whatsnew/ask-the-developer-vol-1-game-builder-garage-part-3/)). George Fan gives the reason to start light: "When I first start playing, I only have a certain willingness to learn things", and it grows as the player becomes invested; his game held its currency back until ten levels in ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/gdc-2012-10-tutorial-tips-from-i-plants-vs-zombies-i-creator-george-fan); [AOL/Joystiq](https://www.aol.com/2012/03/09/plants-vs-zombies-casual-gamers-strategy-game/), attribution uncertain). For this game that means unlocking by what the player has done and never by the date, one word or small batch at a time, each arriving with the tank puzzle the design already plans. It means a starting set large enough to build a creature that visibly lives, which is the test Final Fantasy XII failed. By analogy with the held-back currency, it also means letting a first creature be built before the budget starts to pinch; that last point is an inference no source tests. The fit with a friend group is direct: friends play at different rates, and a calendar gate punishes the keenest.

Two things make hidden depth tolerable, and both are part of the interface. The first is a working example to change. In Scratch data covering more than a million users, people who remixed more went on to use a wider range of commands, and exposure to a concept through remixing was associated with using it later; the study is observational, and its authors later removed the word "strong" from their claims ([MIT Media Lab](https://www.media.mit.edu/publications/remixing-as-a-pathway-to-computational-thinking), [paper repository](https://gitea.communitydata.science/collective/cdsc_examples_repository/commit/a3aa7637e0bcf5419ef744ad514e07eeb198b1e5)). Game Builder Garage saves every lesson's result as the player's own editable project ([Nintendo](https://www.nintendo.com/us/whatsnew/ask-the-developer-vol-1-game-builder-garage-part-3/)). The second is being able to ask why. The admired designs attach explanation to the thing explained at the moment it matters: Into the Breach telegraphs every attack on the board, Paradox breaks numbers down where they are shown, Human Resource Machine acts out each instruction, and RimWorld's learning helper fires a lesson when the related event first happens ([Boing Boing](https://boingboing.net/2015/10/19/human-resource-machine-game.html?noamp=mobile), [RimWorld Wiki](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Learning_helper)). This is the thinnest part of the research. No source described a "which rule fired" view, time scrubbing or replays in any named game. The design's "watching it think" view is the right answer in outline and has no evidence behind its details.

## Tutorials pay off in games this complex, when they teach by guided doing

The best controlled evidence says tutorials pay in proportion to how undiscoverable a game is. Andersen and colleagues tested eight tutorial designs across three games with over 45,000 players: **tutorials raised play time by as much as 29% in the most complex game, Foldit, and did not significantly improve engagement in the two simpler ones**. On-demand help was not a safe default; it helped in Foldit, did nothing in one simple game and did harm in the other ([Andersen et al., CHI 2012](https://grail.cs.washington.edu/projects/game-abtesting/chi2012/chi2012.pdf)). A later experiment had 1,646 people build a game level under six help conditions and found interactive help best on time spent, learnability, motivation and the quality of what was built, with video a close second ([Kao, FDG 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.03519)). NN/g's study of mobile app tutorials found that people who read them were no more successful and rated tasks as harder; its figures (91% against 94% success, 4.92 against 5.49 on a seven-point ease scale) reached the notes through secondary and vendor pages ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/mobile-tutorials/), [HelpHero](https://helphero.co/blog/product-tours/)). A game that asks a newcomer to assemble a body and write instincts cannot be learned by poking alone, so it sits with Foldit, and teaching is justified. The same evidence fixes the form: in context, at the moment of need, by doing. Celia Hodent's line from her work on Fortnite covers the alternative: "We can hardly pay attention to tutorial texts when busy killing zombies" ([IxDA slides](https://interaction19.ixda.org/download/talk-the-ux-of-fortnite-celia-hodent.pdf)).

First-hand designer accounts converge on a short rule set. George Fan's tips from Plants vs. Zombies include blending the tutorial into play, having the player do each thing once, keeping to "no more than eight words on the screen at any time", and showing a hint only to players "doing the wrong thing" ([Filament Games](https://www.filamentgames.com/blog/10-tips-designing-game-tutorial), [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/gdc-2012-10-tutorial-tips-from-i-plants-vs-zombies-i-creator-george-fan)). Valve's commentary on Portal describes stripping distracting objects from training rooms and, when one chamber "was shown to introduce too many new concepts at once", adding two simpler chambers before it instead of more explanation ([Portal developer commentary](https://theportalwiki.com/wiki/Portal_developer_commentary)). Nintendo's Game Builder Garage, the closest precedent for teaching non-programmers a logic editor, splits lessons into steps of about five minutes with explicit directions, puts unaided "checkpoint" puzzles between lessons, keeps a separate reference for those who want more, and rewards each step so that beginners finish a game ([Nintendo, part 1](https://www.nintendo.com/us/whatsnew/ask-the-developer-vol-1-game-builder-garage-part-1/), [part 3](https://www.nintendo.com/us/whatsnew/ask-the-developer-vol-1-game-builder-garage-part-3/)). It also gives each logic node a character ([Nintendo Life](https://www.nintendolife.com/news/2021/07/game_builder_garage_team_took_inspiration_from_idol_groups_for_nodons)). Dwarf Fortress's long-awaited tutorial covers only four early roadblocks ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/the-pcs-most-infamously-complex-game-gets-a-tutorial-after-16-years)). The shape these support is a guided first build inside the real editor with a deliberately small palette, a visible result at each step, and free building within reach for those who want it.

Callouts fit this evidence when each one is the prompt for a real action and not a stop on a tour of the screen. NN/g warns that several coach marks in a row strain short-term memory and make an app look complicated, and advises keeping them short and visually distinct from the interface ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/mobile-instructional-overlay/)). Apple's guidance puts instruction next to the control it describes, and its TipKit framework has a setting for how soon one tip may follow another ([Apple HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/offering-help), [TipKit](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/tipkit)). The numeric limits are the weakest link. The usual ceiling of three steps comes from one vendor's benchmarks of desktop business software: three-step tours completing at 72%, four-step at 45%, and user-started tours at about 61 to 67% against 31% for timed ones ([Chameleon](https://www.chameleon.io/blog/mastering-product-tours), [Chameleon 2023](https://www.chameleon.io/benchmark-report-2023)). The researcher could not open the 2025 report and suspects its headline figures are recycled from 2019. Onboarding checklists in the same kind of software average 19.2% completion ([Userpilot](https://userpilot.com/blog/?p=197841)). Three callouts per sequence and eight words per callout are sensible defaults from thin sources, and the preference for callouts the player starts is direction only.

A first session consistent with all of this runs as follows; the sequence is this report's construction, not something a source demonstrates. A friend taps an invite link in the group chat and lands in the right world with a name field and nothing else, as Jackbox does with a room code and a name ([Jackbox support](https://support.jackboxgames.com/hc/en-us/articles/15794759479959-How-do-I-join-a-game)). The first screen shows whose world this is and what the friends' creatures are doing, context no commercial game can offer. The first creature is then built in the real designer with the starting vocabulary: one concept per step, one callout per step, each ending in something visible in the test tank. Released into the world, that creature becomes the player's first template. Only then does the game ask for anything. Duolingo reported about a 20% rise in daily active users from moving sign-up until after a first lesson, and found that skippable prompts before a blocking one worked better than a blocking one alone; the source is an interview from about 2017 about a learning app ([First Round Review](https://review.firstround.com/the-tenets-of-a-b-testing-from-duolingos-master-growth-hacker)). On iPhone the save step matters for a technical reason: an installed web app has storage separate from Safari's (recalled: [firt.dev](https://firt.dev/notes/pwa-ios/)), so a friend who installs later arrives signed out unless the game holds state on the server and offers a link or code to get back in.

After the first session the unlock schedule is the curriculum. Each new word arrives with its tank puzzle and one callout, and nothing else is taught that day. Help stays available without nagging in the form Paradox made familiar, every game term tappable where it appears, and the Game Accessibility Guidelines add that unlocked instructions should be replayable and written in simple, clear language ([Game Accessibility Guidelines](https://gameaccessibilityguidelines.com/)). A returning player gets a short re-orientation and one next step, not a backlog.

Commercial benchmarks are the wrong yardstick for any of this. Median day-one retention for mobile games was about 22.9% in early 2024 and has fallen since, but those figures describe strangers acquired by advertising ([App Developer Magazine](https://appdevelopermagazine.com/mobile-gaming-industry-benchmarks-offer-fresh-look-on-industry/), attribution probable; [GameDev Reports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/gameanalytics-mobile-and-pc-game)), and no trustworthy public figure exists for tutorial length or completion. With five to twenty invited friends, each first session is the data. Fan and Valve both worked by watching one unfamiliar person play, and the behaviour design's own next step, a phone prototype tried on two or three friends, is the right instrument.

## Thumbs and Safari set the layout: tap-first sheets in a tab that needs no install

### Controls of 48 px, frequent actions in the lower middle

The measured evidence on grip is old but consistent. Steven Hoober's 2013 street observations found **49% of people using a phone one-handed, 36% cradling it and 15% two-handed**, with a third of one-handed users on the left thumb ([Hoober, UXmatters](https://uxmatters.com/mt/archives/2013/02/how-do-users-really-hold-mobile-devices.php)). A later tapping test found the centre of the screen fastest and most accurate, and the bottom and top-right corners least accurate for right-handers (a secondary summary: [mobiForge](https://mobiforge.com/design-development/designing-touch-thumb-and-finger-sized-design)). A 2018 study of natural thumb movement found that "the lowermost region and the upper-left region" fall outside the natural zone for every thumb length, with small and medium thumbs covering only about 30% of a 4.7 to 5.2 inch screen ([Kim & Ji](https://rd.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-319-96071-5_50)). The familiar green-and-red thumb-zone maps are, per the search summary, "a heat map of guesses" ([Scott Hurff](https://www.scotthurff.com/posts/how-to-design-for-thumbs-in-the-era-of-huge-screens/)). All of this predates today's phones. For target size the standards are firm: WCAG 2.2 sets a 24 CSS px minimum at level AA and 44 px at AAA, Apple recommends 44 pt and Google 48 dp ([W3C 2.5.8](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html), [W3C 2.5.5](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-enhanced.html), [TetraLogical](https://tetralogical.com/blog/2022/12/20/foundations-target-size/)). Designing to 48 px with about 8 px between targets satisfies all of them; the spacing figure is recalled from Material 2 ([Material](https://m2.material.io/design/usability/accessibility.html)). Hoober's later advice to enlarge targets toward the edges, from about 7 mm in the centre to 12 mm at the bottom, is recalled and unverified ([Hoober 2017](https://www.uxmatters.com/mt/archives/2017/03/design-for-fingers-touch-and-people-part-1.php)).

The layout that follows puts frequent actions in the lower-middle band and not hard against the bottom edge, keeps the top of the screen for read-only status such as season and day, biases nothing important hard to the right, and offers a switch that mirrors the controls for left-handed play. Navigation should be visible, given NN/g's finding on hidden menus, and the platform guides cap a bottom bar at five destinations (recalled: [Material 3](https://m3.material.io/components/navigation-bar/guidelines), [Apple HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/tab-bars)). The world is home. The inspector belongs in a non-modal bottom sheet, the pattern NN/g describes: a sheet that starts minimised and leaves the background usable, becomes modal only when expanded, closes with Back, and is never stacked on another sheet; Apple calls the resting heights detents ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/bottom-sheet/), [Apple HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/sheets)). Tapping a creature raises a peek with its name, mood and one action while the world stays pannable, and the camera re-centres the creature in the uncovered part of the screen. The recap is a card on entry, not a tab. A basin some 26 screens wide needs a visible stepped zoom control, since pinch announces nothing and WCAG requires a single-pointer alternative to multi-finger gestures (recalled: [W3C 2.5.1](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pointer-gestures.html)).

### Tap places a part, and drag is the shortcut

Dragging is the weak point of touch editors. One study reported errors rising from 8% to 36% between click and drag-and-drop tasks; the search summary did not say which of two candidate papers carries the figure ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/978-3-319-07446-7_7.pdf), [UPV](https://riunet.upv.es/bitstream/10251/64752/3/VersionAutor.pdf)). Older adults made fewer errors when the required drop precision was relaxed ([University of Limerick](https://pure.ul.ie/en/publications/improving-accessibility-of-tactile-interaction-for-older-users-lo/)), and students perceive block coding on phones as slower because of the dragging ([University of Luxembourg](https://orbilu.uni.lu/bitstream/10993/61605/1/3649217.3653549.pdf)). The finger also hides both the thing carried and the place it is going. The standard remedies are call-outs and offset cursors ([Böhme](https://www.medien.informatik.uni-muenchen.de/lehre/ss15/ps/papers/Boehme-OvercomingOcclusionProblems.pdf)), and the Castro app's team made drop highlights extend beyond the target because the finger covered them ([Castro](https://cur.at/ZfQ5UKm?m=web)). And **WCAG 2.2 now requires, at level AA, a way to do without dragging anything that needs only a start and an end point** ([W3C 2.5.7](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/dragging-movements)).

The body editor should therefore work by tapping a part to arm it and tapping a lit socket to place it, with drag as the faster path on top: the part drawn lifted above the finger, a generous snap to the nearest valid socket, a ghost showing where it will land, undo always in view, and a miss returning the part to the tray. The specific offsets and radii in the notes are inference. The behaviour editor needs no dragging at all. Tap a slot and pick from a short sheet, which is the accessible "select, select, drop" sequence described for Blocks4All ([Milne & Ladner](https://milnel2.github.io/files/Blocks4All.pdf)) and what the design already proposes.

### The canvas owns its gestures; the screen edges belong to the system

Inside the canvas the game can own every gesture; at the screen edges it cannot. Setting `touch-action: none` on the canvas and using Pointer Events hands pan, pinch and long-press to the game (recalled from [MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/touch-action), and consistent with engine-forum snippets). Swipes that start at an edge belong to the system: iOS gestures "cannot be disabled from JavaScript", and the known workaround for the back swipe is partial ([Abratabia](https://abratabia.com/mobile-web-games/ios-safari-gotchas.php), low authority; [pqina](https://pqina.nl/blog/blocking-navigation-gestures-on-ios-13-4)). So nothing that starts a gesture should sit within about 20 to 24 px of the left or right edge (the margin is the researchers' inference), `pointercancel` is a normal event, and open sheets and editors should be history entries so that the system Back gesture closes them. The bottom edge needs testing in particular. Developers report that in iOS 26 Safari bottom-fixed elements misrender when the address bar hides, with a fix cited for Safari 26.1 that was not checked against Apple's notes ([Apple Developer Forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/800798)).

### A Safari tab is the baseline, and installing is a bonus

As of November 2026 the current iPhone software is iOS 27 with Safari 27, shipped in mid-September, and an unverified third-party timeline says it brought nothing new for Home Screen web apps ([Cinevva](https://app.cinevva.com/news/2026-09-17-safari-27-model-element-webgpu-clip-planes), [State of PWAs on iOS](https://2f2022c1-astro-blog.laromierre.workers.dev/posts/the-state-of-progressive-web-apps-on-ios-limitations-and-workarounds)). The table lists the capabilities that shape the design and the basis for each.

| Capability | iPhone, Safari tab | iPhone, added to Home Screen | Android Chrome | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Vibration | No | No, apart from the fragile switch trick | Yes, after a gesture | Snippets for iOS ([Software Mansion](https://swmansion.com/blog/haptic-feedback-on-the-web-why-the-web-deliberately-refuses-to-be-as-tactile-as-native-apps)); Android recalled |
| Push notifications | No | Yes since iOS 16.4, asked after a tap | Yes | Snippet ([MagicBell](https://www.magicbell.com/blog/pwa-ios-limitations-safari-support-complete-guide)) |

The consequence is a game that is complete in a tab, better when installed, and never dependent on haptics, fullscreen, orientation lock or background work. Game state lives on the server and local storage is a cache, so eviction costs a download, not a creature. Push on iPhone exists only after install and after a tap, which fixes the order for an iPhone friend: play, then install with the game's own illustrated steps, then a notification question. Prompts shown before any interaction are almost always refused; one second-hand account of a Mozilla study gives about 2% acceptance on page load against about 24% after interaction and explanation ([Notificare](https://notificare.com/blog/2020/04/06/Web-Push-Notifications-dos-and-donts/)). The advice on streaks rests on blogs: they work through loss aversion, which is exactly what makes them feel like pressure ([Hubble](https://www.myhubble.money/blogs/streaks-milestones-and-loss-aversion-in-engagement-design), [Yu-kai Chou](https://yukaichou.com/gamification-analysis/streak-design-gamification-motivation-burnout/)). For a game among friends, one daily digest and no streak is the safe default.

HTML over WebGL is a mainstream arrangement (PixiJS's own accessibility layer is a DOM overlay: [PixiJS](https://pixijs.com/8.x/guides/components/accessibility)), and the researchers' guidance for it is consistent reasoning that no source measured: opaque panels, one overlay container that ignores pointer events except on real controls, sheets animated with `transform`, name tags and selection rings drawn in the canvas, text-heavy panels in the DOM. The same split serves accessibility. Canvas content "cannot be directly read by a screen reader" ([Bugnet](https://bugnet.io/blog/how-to-fix-web-game-canvas-no-screen-reader-announcements)), so a DOM list of creatures in view that opens the same inspector, plus text sized in relative units, covers most of the need. Text scaling differs by platform: Safari follows the system setting when a page opts in with `font: -apple-system-body`, Chrome on Android does not scale web text by default, and its opt-in meta tag was in Canary in January 2026 with stable status unconfirmed ([Ionic](https://ionicframework.com/docs/layout/dynamic-font-scaling), [Josh Tumath](https://joshtumath.uk/posts/2026-01-27-try-text-scaling-support-in-chrome-canary)). An in-game text size setting closes the gap. iPhone cannot lock orientation, so the portrait layout should sit centred in a landscape viewport and not refuse it.

The order below follows the plan's own sequence. The first five shape the phone prototype of the designer and test tank, which is the next thing to be tried on friends. The next five belong to the shared world and the daily loop. The last four are production rules that can wait but are cheap to adopt early. Each row names what it rests on and grades it on the scale from the first section.

Read across the table, the recommendations that would survive a hostile reviewer are few and specific: a tap path for every drag, controls of 44 to 48 px, visible navigation, text contrast measured against the worst of the paper grain, teaching by guided doing in context, and unlocking by activity instead of by date. Each rests on a standard, a controlled study or a first-hand account, usually more than one. The recommendations to hold loosely are the ones with numbers attached to thin sources: three callouts, eight words, five to fifteen minutes per unlock, a 20 px edge margin, the 100 ms press. They are reasonable defaults and nothing more. Recommendation 3 is alone in resting on almost nothing. It is ranked third because the game's central promise, that a player can see why a creature did what it did, depends on a display nobody has been found to have built.

## Conclusion

The research changes the shape of the interface problem in two ways. First, the supposed tension between handcrafted charm and depth mostly dissolves on a phone, because the constraints align: opaque paper sheets are the look, the cheapest thing to composite over a live canvas and the easiest thing to make legible, and a field-guide surface with tappable terms is both the cute presentation and the route to every number. What charm must never do is add steps, and what depth must never do is require hover, dragging or a third level of sheet. Second, the constraint that looked like a handicap, a group of five to twenty friends in a browser tab, turns out to remove most of what commercial onboarding advice is for. There is no funnel to optimise and no stranger to retain; there is an inviter whose world gives the first screen its meaning, and a handful of first sessions that can each be watched.

That leaves one piece of real invention and one piece of housekeeping. The invention is showing blended urges: every precedent resolves rule conflicts by order, this game does not, and no amount of further reading will substitute for a prototype in two or three friends' hands. The housekeeping is to open, once the network allows it, the handful of pages on which firm recommendations lean: the Andersen paper, NN/g's articles on progressive disclosure, tutorials and bottom sheets, the WCAG 2.2 pointer criteria, Firtman's iOS compatibility notes and the Safari 27 release notes. Until then the figures in this report are leads that agree with each other, which is a reason to act on their direction and not to quote them.
